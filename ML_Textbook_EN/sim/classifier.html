Description of this simulator (procedure, formulas, conditions, questions)

Procedure and what to observe

  1. Leave ① at its defaults (overlapping data, N = 60, first-order features, λ = 0.01, η = 0.01) and press “Run”. What to observe: at first w = 0 and b = 0, so p = 0.5 everywhere (loss L = 60 ln 2 ≈ 41.6). As the iterations proceed, the boundary settles into a line sloping downward to the right, and the loss approaches the Newton minimum L* ≈ 19.09 (L − L* appears in the readout).
  2. Move the pointer over the figure, or enter coordinates in the “query point” fields. What to observe: under “Putting the current numbers into the formula”, the numbers connect a = w1x1 + w2x2 + b and p = 1/(1 + e−a). Points with a = 0 form the boundary (p = 0.5), and the signed distance to the boundary is a/‖w‖.
  3. Choose the “linearly separable” data, check “no regularization (λ = 0)”, and press “Run”. What to observe: even after the training accuracy reaches 100 %, the loss keeps decreasing toward 0 and ‖w‖ keeps growing (the boundary hardly moves, but the shading changes more abruptly). The readout says “no minimum at finite weights”. When you return λ to 0.01, ‖w‖ stops at a finite value.
  4. Choose the “concentric circles” data and compare the features “first-order” and “with quadratic terms”. What to observe: with first-order features the boundary is a line, so the accuracy stays at about 60 %; adding the quadratic terms x12, x1x2, x22 makes the boundary a closed curve (an ellipse).
  5. Set the learning rate η above the “stability guideline” 2/Lc and run. What to observe: for the default data the guideline is 2/Lc ≈ 0.097. With η = 0.32, the loss falls into an oscillation that alternates between about 22.64 and 22.94 at every iteration and never reaches the minimum 19.09. For η below the guideline the loss decreases every time (the guideline is a sufficient condition, so the iteration can converge even above it; for this data it converges even at η = 0.25).
  6. Look at ② at its defaults (two moons, RBF kernel, C = 1, γ = 1). What to observe: the boundary f = 0 (solid line) and the margins f = ±1 (dashed lines). Points marked ○ (0 < αn < C) lie exactly on the dashed lines, and points marked ◇ (αn = C) lie inside the dashed lines or on the opposite side. Unmarked points (αn = 0) do not enter the sum for f(x), so removing them would not change the boundary.
  7. Increase γ and C (for example γ = 20, C = 100). What to observe: the boundary bends in fine detail around individual points, and the number of support vectors increases (overfitting). When you decrease γ, the boundary approaches a line (ℓ = 1/√(2γ) becomes longer).
  8. Set the kernel to “linear”, the data to “overlapping”, C to 1000, and the “iteration limit” to 100. What to observe: the display says that the limit was reached and that the KKT violation m − M is larger than the tolerance, and the difference between the primal and dual objectives (the duality gap) is far from 0. When you return the limit to 100000, the run converges.

With the keyboard, Tab moves to buttons and sliders, Enter (or Space) presses a button, and the arrow keys move a slider. The tabs can be switched with ← and →. Whatever you can do on the figure (pointer and click) you can also do with the “query point” fields and the “Add a point here” button.

Formulas and algorithms computed

① Logistic regression (labels yn ∈ {0, 1}). The features are φ(x) = (x1, x2)T for first order, and (x1, x2, x12, x1x2, x22)T with quadratic terms.

a=wTφ(x)+b,y^=p(y=1∣x)=σ(a)=11+e−aL(w,b)=−∑n=1N{ynlny^n+(1−yn)ln(1−y^n)}+λ2‖w‖2∇wL=∑n=1N(y^n−yn)φn+λw,∂L∂b=∑n=1N(y^n−yn)w←w−η∇wL,b←b−η∂L∂b

The decision boundary is a = 0 (p = 0.5). For first-order features the boundary is a line, and the signed distance from a point x to the boundary is a/‖w‖. One term of the loss is rewritten as ln(1 + ea) − ya, without passing through σ(a), and ln(1 + ea) is computed in an overflow-free form (a + ln(1 + e−a) for a > 0). b is not regularized.

Learning-rate guideline: the gradient of L is Lipschitz continuous with Lc = ¼ λmax(ATA) + λ (A is the design matrix Φ with a column of ones appended, and λmax is the largest eigenvalue). If η < 2/Lc, the loss decreases at every iteration (a sufficient condition). The comparison minimum L* is found by solving the Newton equations with a Cholesky factorization of the Hessian H = ATSA + λ diag(1, …, 1, 0) (S is the diagonal matrix of ŷn(1 − ŷn); the last component is b), without forming an inverse, and with a line search. When λ = 0 and all points are on the correct side of the boundary, the display says that the minimum is not attained at finite weights.

② Kernel SVM (labels yn ∈ {−1, +1}). The dual problem

maxαD(α)=∑n=1Nαn−12∑n=1N∑m=1Nαnαmynymk(xn,xm)subject to: 0≤αn≤C,∑n=1Nαnyn=0f(x)=∑n:αn>0αnynk(xn,x)+b

is solved. The kernels are linear, k = xTx′; polynomial, k = (xTx′ + 1)d (d = 2, 3); and RBF (Gaussian), k = exp(−γ‖x − x′‖2). The RBF kernel is the text’s σf2 exp(−‖x − x′‖2/(2ℓ2)) with σf = 1 and γ = 1/(2ℓ2).

The KKT conditions (necessary and sufficient for optimality) require one of the following at each point.

αn=0⇒ynf(xn)≥10<αn<C⇒ynf(xn)=1αn=C⇒ynf(xn)≤1

The first line is for points that are not support vectors, the second for points on the margin, and the third for points inside the margin or misclassified. The “KKT residual” on the screen is the largest violation of this condition over the points (max(0, 1 − ynfn) if αn = 0, |ynfn − 1| if 0 < αn < C, and max(0, ynfn − 1) if αn = C). b is chosen so that ynf(xn) = 1 at the points with 0 < αn < C, and the average over those points is taken (if there are no such points, the midpoint of the range allowed by the KKT conditions).

SMO (sequential minimal optimization) repeats the following: to keep the equality constraint Σαnyn = 0, it selects two α as a pair, solves that two-variable problem analytically, and clips the result to the box [0, C]. The pair is the point that violates the KKT conditions most and the point that, paired with it, improves the objective most (the same selection as LIBSVM). With Ft = Σs αsysk(xs, xt) − yt, let m(α) = max (−Ft) (over points where ytαt can be increased) and M(α) = min (−Ft) (over points where it can be decreased); then the KKT conditions are equivalent to m ≤ M. The run stops at m − M ≤ ε = 10−3.

margin width=2‖w‖,
‖w‖2=∑n,mαnαmynymk(xn,xm)
P=12‖w‖2+C∑nmax(0,1−ynfn)≥D(α)

The difference between the primal objective P (in hinge-loss form) and the dual objective D (the duality gap) is 0 at the optimum.

Data and random-number conditions

  • All points are synthetic data for teaching purposes. They are meant to represent samples described by two descriptors x1 and x2 (standardized values, dimensionless) and divided into two classes (for example “glass-forming / not glass-forming”), but they are not data of a real material. The class of point n alternates with parity (n = 0, 2, 4, … have y = 0 (−1 in ②), and n = 1, 3, … have y = 1 (+1 in ②)). ε denotes mutually independent standard Gaussian random numbers.
    • Linearly separable: x = s(1, 1) + 0.7ε, with s = +1 (y = 1) and −1 (y = 0). Points with s(x1 + x2)/√2 < 0.3 are redrawn (this guarantees that the line x1 + x2 = 0 separates the classes with a margin).
    • Overlapping: x = s(0.8, 0.8) + 1.0ε.
    • Concentric circles: x = r(cos θ, sin θ), θ ~ U(0, 2π), r = ry + 0.25ε, r0 = 1.0 (inner), r1 = 2.2 (outer).
    • Two moons: t ~ U(0, π); for y = 0, u = (cos t, sin t); for y = 1, u = (1 − cos t, 0.5 − sin t); x = 1.8{u − (0.5, 0.25)} + 0.22ε.
    • XOR: x = 1.2(a, c) + 0.55ε. Choose a = ±1 with equal probability; c = a if y = 1 and c = −a if y = 0.
  • Points that fall outside the range [−3.4, 3.4] are redrawn. The plotting range is [−3.5, 3.5] on both axes.
  • The random numbers come from a seeded generator (mulberry32). The default seed is 20261112 for both ① and ②. Starting over with the same seed gives the same points. If you change the data type, the number of data points, or the seed, the points added by clicking are discarded and the data are regenerated.
  • Defaults: ① overlapping, N = 60, first-order features, λ = 0.01, η = 0.01, starting from w = 0, b = 0. ② two moons, N = 60, RBF, C = 1, γ = 1, iteration limit 100000.
  • Computation limits: up to 300 points (10–200 generated), up to 50000 gradient-descent iterations, and up to 100000 SMO iterations (two-variable updates).

Simplifications

  • The inputs are created as already standardized values. With real data, the descriptors must be standardized for both logistic regression and the SVM (the RBF kernel in particular uses distances, so it is sensitive to units).
  • Logistic regression uses gradient descent without minibatches (the gradient is computed from all the data). The features with quadratic terms are not standardized, so the stable learning rate is smaller than for first order.
  • Only the training accuracy is shown. The validation data and cross-validation needed to choose C, γ, and λ are not treated.
  • The SVM’s SMO uses the same working-set selection as LIBSVM, but it has no shrinking (temporarily removing variables that no longer move) and no cache.
  • The method of converting SVM scores into probabilities (Platt scaling) is not included.

Conditions under which results become unstable

  • If the learning rate η is larger than 2/Lc, the loss may oscillate or increase, and the weights may diverge. When ‖w‖ exceeds 106 or NaN or infinity appears, the computation is stopped and the reason is displayed.
  • With linearly separable data and λ = 0, the minimum of the loss is not attained at finite weights, and ‖w‖ keeps growing with the iterations (slowly, almost logarithmically). When the data contain only one class, b diverges.
  • The SVM cannot be solved unless there is at least one point of each of the two classes (the display says so). If points of opposite classes coincide at the same position, the solution sticks to αn = C for a large C close to the hard margin.
  • If you use a large C (100 or more) with the linear kernel on overlapping data, SMO converges very slowly and may reach the iteration limit. When the limit is reached, “Reached the iteration limit” and the KKT residual are displayed (the solution is not optimal).
  • The duality gap P − D contains C × (the sum of hinge losses), so for a large C a sizable gap remains even when m − M is below the tolerance (the stopping test is made on m − M).
  • With the polynomial kernel, the cube of xTx′ + 1 becomes large (hundreds), and for a small C all points tend to have αn = C.

Short questions to answer after operating

  1. What is the loss at the start of ① (w = 0, b = 0)? How is it related to the number of data points N?
    Sample answers

    At every point a = 0 and ŷ = 0.5, so the loss per point is −ln 0.5 = ln 2 ≈ 0.693. For N = 60, L = 60 ln 2 ≈ 41.59 (the regularization term is 0). This agrees with the value at t = 0 on the screen.

  2. For linearly separable data with λ = 0, why does ‖w‖ keep growing even after the accuracy reaches 100 %? Why does it stop when λ > 0?
    Sample answers

    When all points are on the correct side, multiplying (w, b) by c > 1 in the same direction increases |an| at every point and brings ŷn closer to the correct label, so the loss always decreases. The loss therefore only approaches 0, and there is no finite weight that gives the minimum. Gradient descent keeps moving in the direction that reduces the loss, so ‖w‖ keeps growing (for seed 20261112 and η = 0.01, about 5.99 after 1000 iterations and about 8.98 after 10000). For λ > 0, (λ/2)‖w‖2 grows as the square of ‖w‖, so a minimum appears at a finite point where it balances the decrease of the cross-entropy (‖w‖ ≈ 6.75 for λ = 0.01).

  3. In ②, when you place the pointer on a ○ point (0 < αn < C), roughly what is f(x)? What about a ◇ point (αn = C)?
    Sample answers

    A ○ point lies on the margin, so ynf(xn) = 1; that is, f ≈ +1 if y = +1 and f ≈ −1 if y = −1 (with a deviation of the order of the stopping tolerance). A ◇ point has ynf(xn) ≤ 1; it lies inside the margin (0 < yf < 1) or on the opposite side of the boundary (yf < 0, misclassified). In neither case is f a probability, so “f = 0.8, hence 80 %” cannot be read off.

  4. Starting from the ② default (two moons, RBF, C = 1), change γ from 1 to 20. How do the number of support vectors and the margin width change, and why?
    Sample answers

    At the default (seed 20261112, N = 60) there are 22 support vectors (18 ○, 4 ◇) and the margin width is 0.581. For γ = 20 there are 59 (57 ○, 2 ◇) and 0.323 (almost all of the 60 points). When γ is large (ℓ = 1/√(2γ) is short), the kernel takes appreciable values only between nearby points, and f becomes a superposition of small hills around each point. The boundary bends along individual points, and many points remain near the boundary, so the number of support vectors increases (fitting the training data too closely, that is, overfitting).
