Procedure and what to observe

  1. Look at ① with the defaults (T = 3, dmodel = dk = 2, WQ = WK = WV = identity matrix, rows of X: (1, 0), (0, 1), (1, 1)). What to see: row 1 of the scores is (1, 0, 1); dividing by √2 gives (0.7071, 0, 0.7071); the weights are (0.4011, 0.1978, 0.4011); the output is (0.8022, 0.5989). The weights of every row sum to 1.
  2. Turn on “causal mask”. What to see: the weights of row 1 become (1, 0, 0), and the output is exactly row 1 of X. Row 2 is (0.3302, 0.6698, 0). Row 3 (the last position) is the same as before masking.
  3. Select the preset “W_Q × 3 (larger scores)” and turn “Divide by √d_k” on and off. What to see: the larger the scores, the more the weights concentrate on a single key (softmax saturates). Dividing by √dk shrinks the scores and makes the weights flatter.
  4. Press “Swap rows 1 and 2 of X” (with the mask off). What to see: rows 1 and 2 of the output Z are swapped in the same way (permutation equivariance). Attention alone carries no information about order.
  5. In ②, select the context preset “金属 の 例 に は” (“metals, for example”) and set the temperature τ to 0.5, 1, and 2. What to see: the top probabilities (at τ = 1: 鉄 “iron” 0.321, 銅 “copper” 0.288, アルミニウム “aluminum” 0.267) become sharper at τ = 0.5 and flatter at τ = 2. With top-p = 0.9, four words remain and their probabilities are renormalized to sum to 1.
  6. In ②, select the preset “水銀 は 常温 で” (“mercury at room temperature is”) and press “One token, greedy”. What to see: although the table says liquid, the model chooses 固体 (“solid”) with probability 0.998, and the judgment is “Wrong (contradicts the table)”. A high probability does not mean the answer is correct.
  7. In ②, select the preset “チタン酸バリウム の 結晶構造 は” (“the crystal structure of barium titanate is”) and press “One token, sampled” several times while changing the seed. What to see: the correct perovskite structure has probability 0.047 (4th place), and plausible errors (such as the rock-salt structure, 0.550) come out often. The rarer the material in the training data, the more error-prone it is.
  8. In ②, look at the attention weights of layer 2, head 2. What to see: at the last position of “ニッケル は 磁石 に” (“nickel, to magnets”), a weight of 0.94 is placed on ニッケル (“nickel”). Note, however, that the weights are proportions in which information is gathered, not necessarily the reason for a decision.

Formulas and algorithms computed

The sequence is arranged in rows (row t of X ∈ ℝT×dmodel is the vector at position t; linear maps are applied from the right).

Q=XWQ,K=XWK,V=XWV,Attention(Q,K,V)=softmax(QKTdk+M)V

The softmax is taken for each row (each query) along the columns (keys). The weights and output of row i are

sij=qi⋅kjdk+Mij,Aij=expsij∑j′expsij′,zi=∑jAijvj,Mij={0(j≤i)−∞(j>i)

(M = 0 when the causal mask is off). The softmax subtracts the maximum of each row before taking exp (the values are the same, and there is no overflow). Since exp(−∞) = 0, the weights at masked positions are 0 and the row sums stay 1.

In ②, the next-token probability is built from the logits oi at position t (i is the vocabulary index) as

pi=exp(oi/τ)∑jexp(oj/τ),pi′=pi∑j∈kept wordspj

The order is: (1) divide the logits by τ; (2) softmax; (3) top-k: keep the k words with the largest probabilities; (4) top-p: add up the probabilities renormalized in (3) in descending order and keep words until the cumulative sum reaches p or more; (5) renormalize the probabilities of the remaining words to sum to 1. Greedy decoding picks the word with the largest logit. In sampling, one uniform random number u is drawn, probabilities are added in descending order, and the first word at which the sum exceeds u is chosen.

Forward pass of the model (pre-LN): ht = WE[xt] + WP[t], and in each layer h ← h + MHA(LN(h)) (MHA is multi-head attention) and h ← h + FFN(LN(h)). Finally, applying LN and multiplying by WET gives the logits. The logits at position t give the distribution of xt+1. The log-probability of a sequence uses the probabilities of the model itself (τ = 1):

lnp(xa:b∣x<a)=∑t=ablnp(xt∣x<t),perplexity=exp(−1nlnp)

(n is the number of tokens). Because of the causal mask, a single forward pass over the whole sequence gives the probabilities at all positions at once.

Generated sentences are judged against the table used to make the training data and the 8 sentence templates: correct (fits a template and agrees with the table value), wrong (contradicts the table), unsupported by the training data (fits a template, but the table has no value for that property of that material, for example the crystal structure of a polymer; a sentence that never appears in the training data), and does not fit a template. A sentence that has not ended with the period token is incomplete and is not judged.

Data and random-number conditions

  • All matrices in ① are hand-entered values (not trained). The default is X = [[1, 0], [0, 1], [1, 1]], WQ = WK = WV = identity matrix (dk = dv = dmodel = 2). Inputs are limited to −5 to 5, T = 3 to 5, and dmodel = 2 to 4. There are no units.
  • The weights of the model in ② were trained for this book with PyTorch (CPU, float32) and written out to 7 significant digits. The training data is a synthetic teaching corpus (sentences generated mechanically from a table of 27 materials and 8 sentence templates; random-number seed 20261112). The model was trained on Japanese text, so it reads and writes Japanese tokens. The corpus is not real text.
  • The random numbers for sampling come from a seeded generator (mulberry32). The default seed is 20261112. Starting over from the same seed, the same settings, and the same context generates the same sequence. Changing the seed and “Start over” both delete the generated part and recreate the random numbers from the beginning.
  • Limits: the context length is at most 24 tokens (the context length of the model). Generation stops when <eos> appears or 24 tokens are reached. τ ranges from 0.05 to 3, top-k from 0 (off) to 67, and top-p from 0.05 to 1 (1 = off).

Simplifications

  • ① uses a single head with dk = dv = dmodel (W is a square matrix), no biases, and no output projection WO.
  • The model in ② is a very small model with a vocabulary of 67 words and a context length of 24, and its tokens are whole words (separated by spaces). A real GPT uses subword tokens (Chapter 26, Section 26.2), far more layers, dimensions, and vocabulary, and different training data.
  • The judgment covers only sentences that match one of the 8 sentence templates exactly. The facts in the table are only textbook facts at room temperature, ordinary pressure, and for pure substances; properties that change with conditions are not in the table.
  • The bigram model is a model for comparison that counts only the previous token (additive smoothing α = 0.1). It is neither a Transformer nor a GPT.

Conditions under which results become unstable

  • If you make the input values large in ① (for example, values at the ends of the −5 to 5 range), the scores become large, softmax saturates, and the weights become almost 0 and 1. The numbers do not overflow because the maximum is subtracted, but in training the gradients become very small (Chapter 24, Section 24.4).
  • When τ is small, such as 0.05, the distribution becomes almost the same as greedy decoding; when it is large, such as 3, the distribution approaches uniform and sentences that fit no template increase.
  • In a context where probabilities are nearly tied (example: for “窒化ガリウム の 結晶構造 は”, “the crystal structure of gallium nitride is”, the top 2 words have 0.244 and 0.226), changing the seed swaps correct and wrong sentences.
  • If NaN or infinity appears (this does not normally happen), the computation stops and the reason is displayed.

Short questions to answer after operating

  1. With the defaults in ①, explain from the scores and the exp values why the weights of row 1 are (0.4011, 0.1978, 0.4011). What happens to row 1 when the causal mask is applied?
    Sample answers

    The scores of row 1 are the dot products (1, 0, 1) of q1 = (1, 0) with each key, divided by √2, giving (0.7071, 0, 0.7071). Taking exp gives (2.028, 1, 2.028), and dividing by the sum 5.056 gives (0.4011, 0.1978, 0.4011). Columns 1 and 3 have the same query–key dot product, so they get the same weight. With the causal mask, row 1 can see only itself (j = 1), so the weights are (1, 0, 0) and the output is v1 = (1, 0).

  2. If WQ is multiplied by 3, how skewed are the weights of row 3 when you do not divide by √dk? What happens when you do divide?
    Sample answers

    The scores of row 3 are (3, 3, 6). Without dividing, the weights are (0.0453, 0.0453, 0.9094), almost entirely on column 3. Dividing by √2 gives (2.121, 2.121, 4.243), and the weights are (0.0967, 0.0967, 0.8066). The more the scores are shrunk, the flatter the weights become, which avoids softmax saturation (small gradients).

  3. In ②, for “水銀 は 常温 で” (“mercury at room temperature is”), why does the model choose 固体 (“solid”) with probability 0.998? Are a high probability and correctness the same thing?
    Sample answers

    In the training data, sentences like “<metal> is solid at room temperature” are frequent, and there is only one sentence about mercury (liquid). The next-token prediction loss only raises the likelihood of words that tend to come next in the training data, so a rare exception is pulled toward the majority value. The ln p of the whole sentence is also larger for the wrong “solid” sentence (−9.57) than for the correct “liquid” sentence (−17.68), so a high probability does not mean correctness.

  4. For “金属 の 例 に は” (“metals, for example”), what are the probabilities of the top 3 words at τ = 0.5 and τ = 2? How many words remain with top-p = 0.9?
    Sample answers

    At τ = 1: 鉄 (iron) 0.321, 銅 (copper) 0.288, アルミニウム (aluminum) 0.267. At τ = 0.5: 0.397, 0.319, 0.275 (the probability ratios are squared, so the mass gathers on the larger ones). At τ = 2: 0.172, 0.163, 0.157 (the ratios become square roots, so the distribution is flatter). With top-p = 0.9 (τ = 1), four words remain: 鉄, 銅, アルミニウム, and マグネシウム (magnesium) (cumulative 0.910), and each probability is divided by 0.910.
