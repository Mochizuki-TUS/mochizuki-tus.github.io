Introduction to Density Functional Theory— A Textbook for Engineering Students with Every Derivation Worked Out —

This book is a teaching text for learning first-principles electronic-structure calculations based on density functional theory (DFT) without omitting any step of the mathematical manipulation. The intended readers are third-year undergraduates in engineering who have learned elementary quantum mechanics (the Schrödinger equation, wave functions, expectation values) and the basics of calculus and linear algebra. Advanced tools such as the many-body problem, second quantization, and functional derivatives are explained in a self-contained way, as “Math Notes,” at the place where they first appear.

The book consists of six parts. Part I covers the overall picture of the many-electron problem and the basics of chemical bonding. Part II starts from the electron gas and proceeds to the Thomas–Fermi model, the Hartree–Fock method, and the jellium model, tracing how the central idea of DFT, that “the energy can be written as a functional of the electron density,” came into being. Part III rigorously derives the core of DFT, namely the Hohenberg–Kohn theorems and the Kohn–Sham equations. Part IV treats the electronic structure of solids (band theory and the density of states), Part V treats actual numerical calculation (basis functions, SCF, pseudopotentials), and Part VI treats applications (structural optimization, quantum transport, and spectroscopy calculations).

How to read this book
  • Readers who want to learn the theoretical framework of DFT in the shortest way: Chapter 1 → Chapter 3 → Chapter 4 → Chapter 5 → Chapter 7 → Chapters 9–11
  • Readers who want to study it together with solid-state physics: all of Part I → Part II → Part III → Part IV, in this order
  • Readers interested in implementing calculations: the above, plus Part V and Part VI

  1. Chapter 4The Thomas–Fermi Model and Functional Derivatives The idea of the local density approximation, the mathematics of functional derivatives, the TF energy functional, Lagrange multipliers and the TF equation, application to atoms and its limitations
