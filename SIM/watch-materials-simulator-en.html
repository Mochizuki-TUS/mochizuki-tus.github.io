What Is a Wristwatch Made Of? — Parts and Materials

A single wristwatch packs almost every class of material into something small enough to sit on your wrist — metals such as stainless steel and titanium, ceramics such as sapphire and zirconia, a quartz crystal oscillator and a silicon IC, natural materials such as leather and mother-of-pearl, rubber and resin bands, and even the electrolyte in the battery — each for a reason that suits its place in the watch. The part that keeps time comes in two kinds: the quartz type, which uses a crystal vibrating under an electric voltage, and the mechanical type, in which the force of a mainspring drives a train of gears. Smartwatches with built-in sensors have also become common.
