From Molecular Orbitals to Bands — Lining Up Atoms Turns Energy Levels into a Band

Start from the bonding and antibonding orbitals of H2 and add atoms one at a time. With N atoms there are N energy levels — yet every level stays inside the window α ± 2|β| and just gets more crowded, so for N → ∞ they form a continuous band of width 4|β|. Closing the chain into a ring and solving again brings out Bloch’s theorem and E(k); the density of states piles up at the band edges (van Hove singularities); and the moment you alternate the atoms or the bonds, a band gap opens. Finally we move on to the two-dimensional square lattice and see how the band shapes of s, p, d, and f orbitals differ, with real space and reciprocal space linked together.
