Comparing Synthesis Methods — Solid-State, Solution, and Vapor Routes (CVD and PVD)

Even for the same inorganic material, the way it is made changes greatly depending on what state the starting materials are in when they are mixed, and where they turn solid. Compare the four methods in four panels (“What is the starting material? → How is it carried or mixed? → Where does it turn solid? → What do you get?”), and press the buttons to make the diagrams move. For ②–④, switch to the “Detailed” view to see the equations, graphs and values as well.

Mochizuki Research Group, Tokyo University of Science | The diagrams of atoms, particles and molecules are schematic, and time is shown compressed. The numbers are calculated from a model that combines equations and values from the literature (with sources) and provisional values chosen for teaching (marked “(provisional)” or “schematic”). The procedures and reagent amounts in ① and ② follow the 2025 edition of the lab manual for Materials Science Experiments (inorganic materials section) at the former Tokyo Institute of Technology (§6.1, §8.1, §8.2) and the lab videos (A1–A3, B1–B4). Calcination is carried out in air (as instructed by Prof. Mochizuki, 2026-09-25). Detailed explanations, sources and points to confirm with Prof. Mochizuki are on the explanation page.
