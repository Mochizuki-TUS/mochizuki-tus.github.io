Comparing Synthesis Methods — Solid-State, Solution, and Vapor Routes (CVD and PVD)

Even for the same inorganic material, the way it is made changes greatly depending on how the raw materials are mixed, which path the matter takes, and where it reacts and is deposited. In ① solid-state synthesis you add ethanol to the powders, grind and mix them, dry them and then calcine them, and watch a product layer grow from the contacts between particles. In ② solution synthesis (coprecipitation), you look at the key points of how ions mix in the solution and precipitate once it becomes supersaturated, and compare how calcination proceeds with the solid-state route. ③ and ④ are vapor routes. In ③ CVD, the source gas reacts chemically on the surface of the substrate and becomes a film; in ④ PVD, atoms ejected by evaporation or sputtering stick to the substrate and become a film.
