The answer: fusion is measured in “MeV.” Chemical reactions (combustion, batteries) are measured in eV, and so are the electrons inside atoms. The moment we turn to what happens inside the atomic nucleus, the unit jumps a millionfold, from eV to MeV. That is what nuclear energy really is.

Why is the weak force “weak”? Because the W particle that carries the force is very heavy (about 86 times a proton). A heavy particle can exist only for an instant because of the uncertainty principle, so its range is only ħ/(mWc) ≈ 0.0025 fm = 1/400 of a proton’s diameter. That is why it becomes a reaction that “rarely happens.”

💪The Strong Force: Why Doesn’t the Nucleus Fly Apart?

Protons repel one another electrically, yet atomic nuclei hold together.

Closer than about 1 fm, the strong force (attractive) overwhelms the electric repulsion all at once and the potential plunges. The depth of this well is itself the binding energy, the source of MeV-scale energy. However, beyond 3 fm the strong force is almost zero, so nothing can start unless the particles first get close.

🔬Seen in Terms of Quarks

Protons and neutrons are each really a cluster of three quarks.

The strong force is the force that binds quarks together via gluons. The “nuclear force” acting between nucleons (protons and neutrons) is the leftover that spills out of it (just as the intermolecular force is a leftover of the electromagnetic force).

🌀The Weak Force: Why the Sun Doesn’t Burn Out Quickly

Smashing two protons together is not enough for them to stick. One of them has to be “remade” into a neutron.

Two protons (the diproton ²He) cannot exist. Because of the Pauli exclusion principle, two identical protons cannot enter the most stable state, and the strong force alone cannot bind them. You can check this by trying “2 protons only” in the Assembly lab.
This is where the weak force comes in. One u quark inside a proton emits a W⁺ boson and changes into a d quark, turning the proton into a neutron. Then the proton + neutron can bind as a deuteron, and 0.42 MeV is released (a positron e⁺ and a neutrino ν also fly out).
This one step is incredibly slow. It takes an average of about 9 billion years for a single proton at the Sun’s center to undergo a p+p reaction. Because the weak force is the bottleneck, the Sun burns slowly instead of exploding, and that gave life on Earth time to develop. Conversely, fusion reactors on Earth use the D–T reaction (which proceeds by the strong force alone) rather than p+p because they cannot afford to wait for the weak force.

📈Binding Energy per Nucleon, B/A

This one curve explains both fusion and fission. Try hovering the mouse over the points.

⬆ Climbing the hill releases energy
Any change in the direction of increasing B/A always gives off heat. Climbing from the left is fusion; climbing from the right is fission.
⛰ The summit is ⁵⁶Fe / ⁶²Ni
B/A ≈ 8.8 MeV. Fusing beyond this point releases no energy. When iron builds up at the center of a star, it leads to a supernova explosion.
✨ ⁴He is unusually high
Although it is a light nucleus, its B/A = 7.07 MeV makes it outstandingly stable (magic numbers 2 + 2). That is why the product of fusion is usually ⁴He.
