What Is a Train Made Of? — Cars, Track, and Materials

A train is a large product: each car weighs tens of metric tons and runs at more than 100 km/h. Almost every class of material is used in it — the aluminum alloy and stainless steel of the car body; the carbon steel of the wheels, axles, and rails; the glass and resin of the windows; the polyurethane foam and fabric of the seats; the rubber of the air springs; the friction materials of the brakes; the contact strips of the pantograph and the copper alloy of the overhead wire; the power semiconductors and magnets; and the concrete and crushed stone under the track — each for a reason that suits its place.
