How Do Cooper Pairs Form? — Retarded Attraction from Lattice Distortion, and the “Superconducting Bubble” from the BCS Limit to the BEC Limit

Electrons repel each other through the Coulomb force, yet inside a superconductor they form pairs of two (Cooper pairs). The key is the slow response of the lattice: when a fast electron passes by, the heavy ions are pulled toward it only after a delay, so a dense “wake” of positive charge remains after the electron has gone. If another electron is drawn to that wake, the two electrons feel an attraction at a distance of several tens of nm without ever coming close to each other directly (the Coulomb repulsion is screened out at 0.1 nm).
