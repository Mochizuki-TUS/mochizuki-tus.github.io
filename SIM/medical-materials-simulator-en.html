What Are Medical Materials Made Of? — Joint Replacements, Teeth, Stents, Eyeglasses, and Materials

Medical materials include almost every class of material — metals such as titanium alloys, cobalt–chromium alloys, and stainless steel; ceramics such as zirconia, alumina, and hydroxyapatite; polymers such as ultra-high-molecular-weight polyethylene and polylactic acid; composites made by binding carbon fibers or glass powder in a resin; rubbers such as silicone; liquids such as joint fluid; and even a person’s own bones and teeth — each used for a reason that suits its place. Most things placed inside the body must resist corrosion in body fluid and be compatible with the body’s tissue (biocompatibility), but the required properties differ from place to place (for example, joint replacements that must withstand repeated loading, polylactic acid that breaks down and disappears, and eyeglasses and hearing aids used outside or on the surface of the body).
