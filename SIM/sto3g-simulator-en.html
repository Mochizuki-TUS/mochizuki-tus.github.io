STO-nG — Imitating the Hydrogen 1s Orbital with Three Gaussian Functions

The correct shape is the Slater-type e−r; the easy-to-compute shape is the Gaussian-type e−αr². Quantum chemistry chose to “imitate the correct shape with a sum of easy-to-compute shapes” — that is the STO-nG basis (Lectures 5 and 6). In ①, we increase the number of Gaussians from one to two to three and see how far the wave function and the energy improve; in ②, we look at the Gaussian product theorem, the source of that payoff, and at the overlap integral S(R).

Lengths are in units of the Bohr radius a0, the Gaussian exponent α is in units of a0−2, and energies are in eV.
