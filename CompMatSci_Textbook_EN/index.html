Introduction to Computational Materials Science— From Quantum-Chemical Calculations of Molecules to the Electronic Structure of Solids —

This book is a textbook, based on the lecture “Computational Materials Science” (instructor: Yasuhide Mochizuki) in the Department of Materials Science and Technology, Faculty of Advanced Engineering, Tokyo University of Science, for learning the basic theory of first-principles and quantum-chemical calculations without omitting any steps of the algebra. The intended readers are second- and third-year undergraduates who studied “Chemistry of Materials 1,” “Quantum Mechanics,” and “Molecular Science” in the second year of the undergraduate program and have a grounding in calculus and linear algebra. Advanced tools such as analytical mechanics, group theory, and second quantization are explained in a self-contained way at the points where they become necessary.

The aim of this book is just one: to become able to explain deductively “what kinds of elements, arranged in what way, give rise to what kinds of properties”. To that end, we first start from the Schrödinger equation of the hydrogen atom (Part I); learn the variational principle, perturbation theory, and basis functions, which are approximation methods for treating many-electron systems (Part II); follow how the exchange energy emerges from spin and the Pauli exclusion principle (Part III); understand the chemical bond itself from the secular equation and symmetry (Part IV); and finally broaden our view from molecules to solids, arriving at the origin of the band gap of ionic crystals (Part V).

How to read this book
  • For preparing for and reviewing the lecture: read from Chapter 1 in order. Be sure to work through the exercises at the end of each chapter by hand.
  • Readers who want to run quantum-chemical calculations right away: Appendix B → Chapter 6 → Chapter 8 → Chapter 10
  • Readers who want to learn only the theory of chemical bonding: Chapter 2 → Chapter 11 → Chapter 12 → Chapter 14
  • Readers who feel uneasy about the mathematics: first read Appendix A (polar coordinates, Gaussian integrals, Lagrange's method of undetermined multipliers, eigenvalue problems) through.
