High School Mathematics— From Logic to Conic Sections: High School Mathematics You Can Derive Yourself —

Based on Yasuhide Mochizuki's handwritten notes, “Mathematics Notes: High School Mathematics,” this book is an educational resource that reorganizes the key points of the high school mathematics that forms the foundation for the mathematics, physics, and chemistry studied at university, with the goal that readers become able to derive formulas rather than memorize them. It contains all the definitions, theorems, proofs, and figures of the original notes; we have filled in the gaps between the lines so that high school students can read it on their own, and we have added examples to each section and exercises at the end of each chapter.

We begin with logic and sets, and then proceed to the properties of triangles, trigonometric ratios and the measurement of figures, coordinate geometry, the trigonometric addition formulas, logarithms, vectors, and conic sections. Each chapter of the companion volume “University Mathematics” (AD 02) refers to the corresponding chapter of this book as a prerequisite.

How to read this book
  • Read in order, starting from Chapter 1. Work out each example yourself before looking at its solution.
  • Solutions to the exercises at the end of each chapter are collected in Appendix A.
  • Readers who wish to go on to university mathematics should make sure, in particular, that they have a firm grasp of Chapter 1 (logic), Chapter 5 (addition formulas), and Chapter 7 (vectors).
