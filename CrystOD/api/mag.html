crystod.mag#

Public magnetism API of CrystOD: the crystod-mag domain.

This module covers the symmetry analysis of spin arrangements. The spins on the sites of one element are treated as axial-vector degrees of freedom, the 3N-dimensional spin space is decomposed into irreps of the space group at a q point, and the resulting symmetry-adapted spin bases (cluster multipoles / symmetry-adapted multipole moments, SAMM, after M.-T. Suzuki et al., Phys. Rev. B 95, 094406 (2017) and Phys. Rev. B 99, 174407 (2019)) are classified as ferromagnetic (cluster dipole) or antiferromagnetic (net moment zero). At q = 0 an AFM basis is named after the next rank at which its irrep occurs in the magnetic multipole representations: octupole, 32pole, … for an irrep even under the inversion, quadrupole, hexadecapole, … for an odd one (the magnetic multipole of rank p has parity (-1)**(p + 1); Cr2O3, Cr: GM1- quadrupole, GM2- hexadecapole, GM1+ octupole). It mirrors the crystod-mag command, whose spin listings, VESTA exports and noncollinear magnetization input (VASP MAGMOM, Quantum ESPRESSO starting_magnetization/angle1/angle2) are built from these functions; the implementation lives in crystod.spin_basis.

Spin representation (crystod-mag --qpoint, and the survey over all special k points when --qpoint is omitted)

  • get_spin_representation: the little-group irreps at q and the axial-vector representation on the selected sites, ready for spgrep.representation.project_to_irrep.

Multipole classification (crystod-mag at q = 0)

  • get_multipole_rank_lists: for every irrep, the ranks of the magnetic multipoles (dipole, quadrupole, octupole, …) in which it appears.

  • separate_ferro_combination: splits the net-moment combination (FM, cluster dipole) off the projected spaces of one irrep; the rest are AFM. analyze_spin_basis falls back on the real form of the irrep when the copies spgrep gives (in a complex basis for the E irreps of trigonal and hexagonal crystals) do not separate cleanly, and turns the partners of all copies onto directions that symmetry fixes.

  • MULTIPOLE_NAMES: rank-to-name table (1: "dipole", 3: "octupole", …).

Magnetic groups and collinear classes (crystod-mag --qpoint, every exported component; --classify-only for the table alone)

  • analyze_spin_basis: the whole q-point analysis as data (SpinBasisAnalysis: decomposition, bases, the real spin field of every component and its magnetic group, responses and class); magnetic_summary_rows gives the rows of the summary table and magnetic_summary_notes the notes under it.

  • magnetic_space_group: magnetic space group of a spin arrangement (spglib): BNS number and symbol, OG number, type I-IV, the magnetic point group derived from the BNS symbol and checked against the operations (MagneticGroupInfo).

  • macroscopic_responses: net magnetization, linear magnetoelectric and piezomagnetic tensors allowed by the magnetic point group (MagneticResponses); format_magnetic_responses prints them.

  • classify_collinear: ferromagnet, ferrimagnet, conventional antiferromagnet or altermagnet (d/g/i-wave, spin-splitting form, spin-degenerate planes) from the sign pattern of a collinear arrangement (CollinearClass), after L. Smejkal, J. Sinova and T. Jungwirth, Phys. Rev. X 12, 031042 (2022).

  • magnetic_point_group_symbol: the MPG symbol of a BNS symbol.

A session mirrors the command:

from phonopy.interface.vasp import read_vasp
from spgrep.representation import project_to_irrep
from crystod import mag, phonon

vib = phonon.SymmetryOnlyVibrations(read_vasp("POSCAR"), standardize=False)
sites = [i for i, s in enumerate(vib.primitive_cell.symbols) if s == "Ni"]
q = [0, 0, 0]
irreps, spin_rep, mapping = mag.get_spin_representation(vib, sites, q)
labels = vib.get_irrep_labels(q, irreps, mapping)
ranks = mag.get_multipole_rank_lists(vib, vib.rotations[mapping], irreps)
for irrep, label, rank_list in zip(irreps, labels, ranks):
    spaces = [s.real for s in project_to_irrep(spin_rep, irrep)]
    if spaces:
        ferro, afm = mag.separate_ferro_combination(spaces, spin_rep, len(sites))

Attributes resolve lazily (PEP 562): importing this module is instant, and phonopy/spgrep are loaded on the first attribute access. Bad input raises ValueError here, where the implementation module raises SystemExit.

crystod.mag.analyze_spin_basis(cell=None, element='', qpoint=('GM',), *, symprec=1e-05, conventional=False, classify=True, vibrations=None)[source]#

Symmetry-adapted spin bases at one q point, classified.

The computation behind crystod-mag --qpoint: the spin representation is projected onto the irreps of the little group, the FM/AFM combinations are separated at q = 0 (copies of an irrep that do not separate cleanly when made real one by one, or whose partners do not lie along directions symmetry fixes – in a trigonal or hexagonal crystal: not along the a axis of the input cell and perpendicular to it, _canonical_spin_partners() – are redone through the real form of the irrep with canonical partners: _canonical_spin_spaces(); an irrep that occurs more than once in a single moment is separated by the uniform moments, _uniform_moment_spaces(); a complex irrep and its conjugate are merged into one real two-dimensional space under their pair label, _merged_pair_spaces(), and so at the other time-reversal-invariant q points, where a pair of d-dimensional irreps gives a real 2d-dimensional space, _real_pair_rows()), every basis vector is resolved into its exported components (x/y/z/111 or comp<N>), the real spin field of each component over the magnetic cell is built (lattice gauge: the spin of site j in the cell at T is Re(w_j exp(2 pi i q.T)); global phase as in the VESTA export, largest moment 1), and with classify every component gets its magnetic space group (crystod.magnetic_group.magnetic_space_group()), the responses its magnetic point group allows (crystod.magnetic_group.macroscopic_responses()) and its collinear class (crystod.magnetic_group.classify_collinear()).

Parameters:

  • cell – The crystal (phonopy PhonopyAtoms, e.g. from phonopy.interface.vasp.read_vasp); not needed when vibrations is given.

  • element (str) – Element whose sites carry the spins ("Ni").

  • qpoint – --qpoint tokens: one label ("GM", "X") or three coordinates in the primitive reciprocal basis (fractions such as "1/2" allowed); a single string is one label.

  • symprec (float) – Symmetry tolerance (spglib, Angstrom); the sites are matched within the tolerance of vibrations (this one when the cell is given).

  • conventional (bool) – Build the magnetic cell from the conventional cell (crystod-mag --conventional).

  • classify (bool) – Compute the magnetic groups, responses and classes.

  • vibrations (SymmetryOnlyVibrations | None) – A prebuilt SymmetryOnlyVibrations(cell, standardize=False) (the CLI passes its own).

Returns:

A SpinBasisAnalysis.

Raises:

  • SystemExit – The element is not in the cell (ValueError through crystod.mag).

  • ValueError – Unknown q-point label; the cell is not primitive; q is not commensurate with a magnetic cell of at most 12 cells along each axis (the message names the smallest commensurate supercell).

Return type:

crystod.mag.classify_collinear(rotations, translations, positions, site_indices, cell_matrix, field, lattice, *, symprec=None)[source]#

Nonrelativistic class of one collinear spin arrangement.

The arrangement is reduced to its sign pattern s = +1 / -1 / 0 along the common spin axis (with the magnitudes); the space-group operations of the non-magnetic crystal, combined with every primitive translation inside the magnetic cell, are sorted into those that keep the pattern (H) and those that reverse every sign (A, the operations that exchange the two sublattices). Net moment nonzero: ferromagnet or ferrimagnet; A empty: compensated arrangement whose sublattices are not related by symmetry; A holding a pure translation or the inversion: conventional antiferromagnet; otherwise an altermagnet, whose wave type is the lowest even l at which the character chi_A (+1 on the rotation parts of H, -1 on those of A) occurs in the angular-momentum representation D_l (2: d-wave, 4: g-wave, 6: i-wave), after Smejkal, Sinova and Jungwirth, Phys. Rev. X 12, 031042 (2022).

Parameters:

  • rotations – Integer rotations of the space group of the primitive cell (SymmetryOnlyVibrations.rotations).

  • translations – Their fractional translations.

  • positions – Fractional positions of the primitive cell.

  • site_indices – Indices of the N magnetic sites.

  • cell_matrix – Integer (3, 3) magnetic cell in primitive units (rows).

  • field – The real spin field (n_cells, N, 3) over the magnetic cell, cells in the order of magnetic_cell_translations().

  • lattice – Primitive lattice vectors as rows (for the Cartesian rotations and directions).

  • symprec (float | None) – Distance tolerance (Angstrom) of the position matching: an image of a site matches the nearest site within it (the tolerance the operations were found with, --tolerance of crystod-mag). Sites within 1e-5 in every fractional coordinate always match; None (default) matches those only.

Returns:

A CollinearClass.

Return type:

CollinearClass

Example

>>> # bcc cell with a +z / -z pair: the body-centring translation
>>> # exchanges the two sublattices
>>> import numpy as np, spglib
>>> from crystod import mag
>>> lattice = np.eye(3) * 2.87
>>> positions = [[0, 0, 0], [0.5, 0.5, 0.5]]
>>> sym = spglib.get_symmetry_dataset((lattice, positions, [26, 26]))
>>> field = np.array([[[0, 0, 1], [0, 0, -1]]], dtype=float)
>>> klass = mag.classify_collinear(sym.rotations, sym.translations, positions,
...                                [0, 1], np.eye(3, dtype=int), field, lattice)
>>> klass.name
'antiferromagnet (conventional: a translation exchanges the sublattices, T tau)'

crystod.mag.get_multipole_rank_lists(vibrations, little_rotations, irreps, max_rank=9)[source]#

Ranks at which each irrep occurs in the magnetic multipole representations.

crystod-mag uses these lists at q = 0 to name the symmetry-adapted spin bases: the spaces of an irrep are named in the order of the ranks in its list, so the ferromagnetic combination (cluster dipole, rank 1) comes first and the antiferromagnetic ones follow as octupole, … (the logic of Table III of M.-T. Suzuki et al., Phys. Rev. B 95, 094406). The rank-p magnetic (time-odd, axial) multipole transforms as the angular-momentum- p representation with parity (-1)**(p + 1) under inversion: the dipole (p = 1) and octupole (p = 3) are parity-even, the magnetic quadrupole (p = 2) is parity-odd, and so on. The multiplicity of every irrep is obtained by character orthogonality against the rotation characters sin((p + 1/2) theta) / sin(theta / 2) of the proper part of every little-group operation.

Parameters:

  • vibrations (SymmetryOnlyVibrations) – Symmetry container of the primitive cell (crystod.phonon.SymmetryOnlyVibrations); its lattice converts the integer rotations to Cartesian form.

  • little_rotations (ndarray[tuple[Any, ...], dtype[int64]]) – Integer rotation matrices of the little-group operations, in the order of the irrep matrices: normally vibrations.rotations[mapping] with the mapping returned by get_spin_representation.

  • irreps – The spgrep irreps of the little group, as returned by get_spin_representation.

  • max_rank (int) – Highest rank p included in the search.

Returns:

One list per irrep (same order as irreps), holding the ranks 1 <= p <= max_rank at which the irrep appears in the rank-p magnetic multipole representation, in increasing order and repeated according to the multiplicity. MULTIPOLE_NAMES translates ranks to names.

Return type:

list[list[int]]

Example

>>> # vib, sites, q: see get_spin_representation (O sites of SrTiO3)
>>> irreps, spin_rep, mapping = mag.get_spin_representation(vib, sites, q)
>>> labels = vib.get_irrep_labels(q, irreps, mapping)
>>> rotations = vib.rotations[mapping]
>>> ranks = mag.get_multipole_rank_lists(vib, rotations, irreps)
>>> dict(zip(labels, ranks))["GM4+(3)"]
[1, 3, 5, 5, 7, 7, 9, 9, 9]
crystod.mag.get_spin_representation(vibrations, site_indices, qpoint, *, symprec=None)[source]#

Irreps and the axial-vector (spin) representation on selected sites at q.

This is the first step of crystod-mag (at the --qpoint given, or at every special k point in survey mode): the spins of the site_indices atoms of the primitive cell are treated as axial vectors, and the little group of qpoint acts on the 3N-dimensional spin space by the same construction as the vibration (polar-vector) representation of crystod-phonon, with the Cartesian rotation R replaced by det(R) * R and the site permutation restricted to the selected sites. The Bloch-phase convention is that of spgrep, so the returned matrices can be projected onto the returned irreps directly with spgrep.representation.project_to_irrep.

Parameters:

  • vibrations (SymmetryOnlyVibrations) – Symmetry container of the primitive cell (crystod.phonon.SymmetryOnlyVibrations); its rotations, translations and primitive_cell define the space group.

  • site_indices (list[int]) – Indices (into vibrations.primitive_cell) of the N atoms that carry a spin, e.g. every atom of the magnetic element.

  • qpoint (list[float]) – The q point in primitive reciprocal coordinates, e.g. [0, 0, 0] or [0.5, 0.5, 0.5].

  • symprec (float | None) – Distance tolerance (Angstrom) within which an operation must map a site onto a site; None (default) takes the tolerance the space group was found with (vibrations.symprec, the --tolerance of crystod-mag), so that every operation spglib accepts permutes the sites. Sites within 1e-5 in every fractional coordinate always match. The Bloch phases are built from the site positions symmetrized by the space group, so a structure off its ideal positions within the tolerance gives the representation of the ideal structure (for a structure at its ideal positions they are the input positions).

Returns:

(irreps, spin_rep, mapping). irreps is the list of spgrep irreps of the little group at qpoint (each an array of shape (order, dim, dim)); spin_rep is the complex array of shape (order, 3N, 3N) holding the spin-representation matrix of every little-group operation, in the order of the irrep matrices; mapping is the integer array that indexes those operations in vibrations.rotations (what get_irrep_labels and get_multipole_rank_lists expect).

Return type:

tuple

Example

>>> from phonopy.interface.vasp import read_vasp
>>> from crystod import mag, phonon
>>> from crystod.examples import example_path
>>> cell = read_vasp(example_path("221_PPOSCAR_SrTiO3"))
>>> vib = phonon.SymmetryOnlyVibrations(cell, standardize=False)
>>> symbols = vib.primitive_cell.symbols
>>> sites = [i for i, s in enumerate(symbols) if s == "O"]   # 3 sites
>>> q = [0, 0, 0]
>>> irreps, spin_rep, mapping = mag.get_spin_representation(vib, sites, q)
>>> spin_rep.shape
(48, 9, 9)
>>> vib.get_irrep_labels(q, irreps, mapping)[6]
'GM4+(3)'

  • positions – Fractional coordinates, (n_atoms, 3).

  • numbers – Atomic numbers (or any type labels), (n_atoms,).

spglib’s identification of the group sometimes fails for one choice of the cell and succeeds for an equivalent one (the bcc H-point arrangement with the moments along x). crystod.mag.separate_ferro_combination(spaces, representation, n_sites, realify_aligned=True)[source]#

Split the ferromagnetic combination off the spaces of one irrep.

At q = 0 an irrep can occur several times in the spin representation (2 x GM4+ for the three Ni sites of AlNi3); crystod-mag calls this function once per irrep label with all of its projected spaces. The spaces are first aligned to a common partner basis (an intertwiner with representation is found for every space, and complex circular bases are recombined into real ones), then the net moments sum_i S_i of the aligned spaces are combined into one unit vector: its unitary completion gives the unique combination that carries a net moment (the cluster dipole, printed as FM) and the orthogonal combinations with sum_i S_i = 0 (AFM, net moment zero). crystod-mag names the AFM combinations by the remaining entries of the irrep’s rank list (get_multipole_rank_lists): octupole, … for an irrep even under the inversion, quadrupole, hexadecapole, … for an odd one (Cr2O3, Cr: GM1- quadrupole, GM2- hexadecapole). When no space carries a net moment, or the completion does not separate cleanly, the aligned spaces are returned unchanged as antiferromagnetic.

Parameters:

  • spaces (list[ndarray[tuple[Any, ...], dtype[float64]]]) – Real basis arrays of shape (dim, 3N) (one row per partner of the irrep; 3N = three Cartesian components of N spins) that all carry the same irrep, e.g. the output of spgrep.representation.project_to_irrep for one irrep.

  • representation (ndarray[tuple[Any, ...], dtype[complex128]]) – The spin-representation matrices of shape (order, 3N, 3N) returned by get_spin_representation; used to align the spaces to a common basis.

  • n_sites (int) – The number N of spin-carrying sites.

  • realify_aligned (bool) – Rotate every aligned row by its own phase to make it real (needed for complex spaces). Pass False for spaces that are already real and transform with one set of real matrices, where a per-row phase would flip the sign of single partners.

Returns:

(ferro, afm). ferro is the basis array of shape (dim, 3N) with a net moment (None when no combination has one) and afm is the list of the remaining net-zero basis arrays; together they span the same space as the input.

Return type:

tuple

Example

>>> # spin_rep, irreps, sites: see get_spin_representation (SrTiO3, O)
>>> import numpy as np
>>> from spgrep.representation import project_to_irrep
>>> gm4p = irreps[6]                                     # GM4+, twice
>>> spaces = [np.real(s) for s in project_to_irrep(spin_rep, gm4p)]
>>> ferro, afm = mag.separate_ferro_combination(spaces, spin_rep, 3)
>>> ferro.shape, len(afm)
((3, 9), 1)
>>> bool(np.abs(afm[0].reshape(3, 3, 3).sum(axis=1)).max() < 1e-8)
True
