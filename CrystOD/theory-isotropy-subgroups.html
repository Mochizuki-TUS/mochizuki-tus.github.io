Order parameters and isotropy subgroups#

Background for the symmetry-lowering tools of crystod-group: how a complex or pseudoreal irrep is turned into the physically irreducible real form that an order parameter actually lives in (--parent, section 20), how the reverse lookup (--child, section 22) and the group-subgroup graph (--graph, section 23) are built, how the symmetry-mode decomposition of a distorted structure is constructed (--supergroup-cif, section 26), and how all of them are validated against the ISOTROPY and Bilbao reference implementations. The same machinery serves the daughter structures of crystod-phonon --subgroup --modulate (section 41) and the magnetic isotropy subgroups of crystod-mag --parent (section 44), which close the page.

Isotropy subgroup of an order-parameter direction (crystod-group --parent)#

Command: crystod-group --parent — 20. Isotropy subgroups

What the subgroup that --parent prints for an order-parameter direction is, and which pair labels the partner rule gives.

When a distortion transforming as an irrep condenses, the symmetry drops from the parent group to the isotropy subgroup H(eta) = {g : D(g) eta = eta}, which depends on the order-parameter direction eta.

For a complex- or pseudoreal-type irrep, taking as the partner of the pair label the irrep with the same real form on every group element, lattice translations included (see Complex- and pseudoreal-type irreps (doubled real form)), gives the pair labels of the ISOSUBGROUP tables (K2KA2 and K3KA3 at K of P3, H3H6 and H4H5 at H of P6_3/m).

Complex- and pseudoreal-type irreps (doubled real form)#

When the Frobenius-Schur indicator of the induced irrep vanishes (complex type) or is -1 (pseudoreal type) — as at zone-boundary points of non-symmorphic space groups, where the translation phases are genuinely complex — the real order parameter transforms as the physically irreducible doubled real form (the realification of D + D*), the dimension doubles, and the output of crystod-group --parent carries the ISOTROPY-style pair label:

crystod-group --parent Ia-3d --irrep P2

* Irrep *
P1P2: order parameter dimension 8 (star of 2 arm(s) x small dim 2 x 2; complex-type irrep -> physically irreducible real form)

* Order parameter directions and isotropy subgroups *
irrep                                               subgroup           size  index
P1P2(a,a,b,b;-a,a,-b,b)                             23 I222            4     48
P1P2(a,-a,b,-b;a,a,b,b)                             24 I2_12_12_1      4     48
P1P2(0,0,0,0;0,a,0,b)                               82 I-4             4     48
P1P2(2a,2b,2c,2d;a+b-c+d,a+b+c-d,a-b+c+d,-a+b+c+d)  2 P-1              4     96
P1P2(0,a,0,b;c,d,d,-c)                              5 C2               4     96
P1P2(a,b,c,d;-a,b,-c,d)                             5 C2               4     96
P1P2(a,b,c,d;e,f,g,h)                               1 P1               4     192

The partner named in the pair label is the tabulated irrep whose real form is the same representation: the one with the same real part of the character on every group element, lattice translations included. The characters of the coset representatives alone do not decide it — at H of P6_3/m, P of I4/mcm or K of P3 several irreps share them and differ only on the translations. +k/-k pairs whose -k star is tabulated separately pair across the stars (I-42d P1 -> P1PA1, P3 H1 -> H1HA1); conjugate-gauge and origin-choice tabulations are matched automatically, and real-type irreps whose induced matrices are complex (P3 of Ia-3d) are realified exactly through the antilinear real structure of the group-averaged intertwiner.

Reverse lookup of isotropy subgroups: coupled pairs and enantiomorphs (crystod-group --child)#

Command: crystod-group --parent SG --child H — 22. Reverse lookup

How --child --coupled searches the pairs of irreps, and why an enantiomorphic partner type counts as a match in some parents and not in others.

How crystod-group --child searches the coupled pairs#

Every isotropy subgroup of a coupled order parameter is S1 & S2', with S1 the isotropy subgroup of a single stratum of the first irrep (the representative of the table) and S2' any conjugate of one of the second, and its fixed space is the sum of the fixed spaces in the two irreps. The single strata of the cached table are the candidates; only irreps with a stratum whose point group has an order divisible by that of H take part (and, with --size and --index, whose size and index divide the requested ones), and a candidate is identified with spglib only after its point group and cell size match.

Enantiomorphic partner types in the reverse lookup#

In a parent with improper operations, the two members of an enantiomorphic pair (P4_122/P4_322, P4_132/P4_332, …) are stabilizers of order parameters of one stratum related by an improper operation of the parent, so either may be listed (section 20 of crystod-group). A stratum whose listed type is the partner of H then counts as a match, and a note: names those rows.

A Sohncke parent (no improper operation, e.g. P4_222 or P3_1) is different: there the two types are different, non-conjugate subgroups from different strata, only rows of type H itself are listed and no note is printed (--parent P4_222 --child P4_122 gives Z1 and Z3, while Z2 and Z4 give P4_322, as in ISOSUBGROUP).

Group-subgroup graphs of isotropy subgroups (crystod-group --graph)#

Command: crystod-group --parent SG --irrep IR [IR2 ...] --graph — 23. Group-subgroup graph

When --graph joins two isotropy subgroups, and how the graph of the two perovskite tilts R4+ and M3+ of Pm-3m compares with the tilt systems of Howard and Stokes.

The group theory: a line joins two isotropy subgroups when one is a maximal subgroup of a conjugate of the other among the nodes.

The 15 tilt systems of Howard and Stokes (a0a0a0 Pm-3m, a+a+a+ Im-3, a+b+c+ Immm, a0a0c+ P4/mbm, a0b+b+ I4/mmm, a0a0c- I4/mcm, a0b-b- Imma, a-a-a- R-3c, a0b-c- C2/m, a-b-b- C2/c, a-b-c- P-1, a+a+c- P4_2/nmc, a0b+c- Cmcm, a+b-b- Pnma, a+b-c- P2_1/m) are 15 of the 25 nodes, and the lines of the graph among them are the 25 group-subgroup relations that their Fig. 1 draws (the testsuite derives this list independently from the Glazer patterns of their Table 1, by setting tilts equal or zero). The other ten nodes are the strata in which one axis carries an in-phase and an out-of-phase tilt at once (R-3, P2_1/c, Pmmn, and second embeddings of P4/mbm, Cmcm, C2/m, C2/c, P2_1/m and P-1), which the tilt classification leaves out; the generic P-1 with cell size 8 is the kernel.

Symmetry-mode analysis: algorithm internals and AMPLIMODES validation#

In the symmetry-mode analysis of crystod-group --supergroup-cif (section 26 of the crystod-group page), the direction and isotropy-subgroup columns are computed with the same induced-irrep machinery as crystod-group --parent (the isotropy-subgroup construction described on this page), non-invariant subgroup lattices are enlarged to the largest parent-invariant sublattice (complete k stars, exact amplitude rescaling), polar subgroups get the minimum-distortion origin (acoustic component removed), the lattice matching tolerates strong relaxation (principal strains up to 20%), and a projector-completeness check closes every run. The direction column names each mode by the ISOTROPY representative of its stratum (the row that --parent lists), with one parent operation applied to all modes so that the rows describe one domain of the child; the direction of the child as given is named in a note under the table and is domain_label in Python. Validated against the Bilbao AMPLIMODES output (SrTiO3 Pm-3m -> I4/mcm: R5- 0.3303 A; F-centred ZrO2 Fm-3m -> P4_2/nmc: X2- 0.5773 A), the ferroelectric BaTiO3 -> P4mm and large-tilt AlF3 -> R-3c cases, and the modulation structures of crystod-phonon --modulation (section 37). If you use this feature, please cite: D. Orobengoa, C. Capillas, M. I. Aroyo and J. M. Perez-Mato, “AMPLIMODES: symmetry-mode analysis on the Bilbao Crystallographic Server”, J. Appl. Cryst. 42, 820-833 (2009).

The atom mapping and its group-subgroup setting#

Both structures are standardized by spglib, and the parent is expressed in the primitive setting of the ISO-IR tables. The child primitive basis is written as an integer matrix S over the parent primitive basis (rows; det S is the cell multiplication), and the child atoms are placed at x = Sᵀ x_child + p for an origin shift p. The first mapping is the one of least total distortion Σ|u|² over the candidate matrices S (principal strains up to 20%), each with a greedy nearest-site pairing and a continuous origin refinement; a tie between settings that a symmetric parent makes equivalent goes to the one closest to the orientation of the input files.

That minimum is not necessarily a group-subgroup setting. The child is idealized by spglib, so in a correct (S, p) every child operation (R, τ) becomes a parent operation (W, v) with W = Sᵀ R S⁻ᵀ and an exact translation part, and the subgroup H is the set of parent operations that leave the mapped structure invariant to machine precision (10⁻⁸ Å). A strained twin setting or a shifted origin can pair the atoms marginally better while some child rotation is not a parent rotation in it, or an origin can match the rotations but not the screw and glide translations; then fewer operations are exact than the child’s own group has, and thresholding the spectrum of near-misses accepts pseudo-operations. The mapping is therefore validated before the decomposition: (a) at least as many exact operations as the child group has, (b) every child operation, carried over by S and p, among them, and (c) a displacement field invariant under them to machine precision, |u(gx) − R_g u(x)| ≤ 10⁻⁶ Å (a pairing can break the symmetry of an invariant structure by exchanging atoms, and then does so by an interatomic distance). None of the three depends on --tolerance, which only sets how spglib identifies the two space groups.

A mapping that fails either test is redone in a group-subgroup setting:

  1. Sublattice. Only the bases S in which Sᵀ R S⁻ᵀ is the rotation of a parent operation for every child rotation R are kept.

  2. Origin. The translation parts give a congruence system (I − W_k) p ≡ v_k − Sᵀ τ_k (mod ℤ³) over the child operations. Stacking the matrices I − W_k and reducing them to diagonal form (U A V = D, U and V unimodular) yields every solution class modulo ℤ³ and the free (polar) directions; a nonzero residual of the compatibility conditions means that the translation parts fit no origin, so the child group is not a subgroup of the parent in that setting. Along polar directions the origin is not fixed by symmetry: the first child atom of every species is put level with each reference atom of its species, through the lattice image whose non-polar remainder is shortest, and each start is refined twice by the polar part of the mean displacement (the AMPLIMODES minimum-distortion origin).

  3. Pairing. At each origin the atoms are paired by the optimal assignment per species (minimum image, at most 1.8 Å per atom). It is accepted only if the field it gives is invariant under the conjugated child operations; otherwise the reference is paired orbit by orbit with child atoms of the same stabilizer, the rest of each orbit following by symmetry, and a child atom half a lattice vector from its site (whose displacement the site symmetry fixes only modulo the lattice) is rejected. A parent operation kept by the child that fixes a different number of sites of one species in the reference and in the child (a Wyckoff conflict) excludes every symmetric pairing at that origin.

  4. Choice and members. Among the valid (S, p) the least Σ|u|² wins (ties: the orientation rule above). The subgroup members are the child operations themselves, plus any parent operation that leaves both the structure and the field exactly invariant (the strain-only part of a ferroelastic lowering), and the structure and the field must be invariant under all of them to machine precision before the decomposition starts.

When no valid (S, p) exists the run stops with the reason: no sublattice carries the child point group, the translation parts are incompatible, every setting has a Wyckoff conflict, or no symmetric pairing stays within 1.8 Å. On a survey of materials-database structure pairs this step resolves 25 of the 32 pairs that a projection without it reports as “the distortion is not fully captured” (7 stop with a specific error), and 66 of the 106 that it reports as “cannot separate the surviving from the broken parent operations” (40 stop with a specific error). For every solved pair the reference plus the displacement field has exactly the child’s space group (spglib, 10⁻³ Å). Since the displacement field is invariant under H, the final completeness check (the listed modes sum to the whole field) can only fail on an internal inconsistency.

Complex-type irreps: the D + D* projector#

The displacement representation is decomposed star by star. For an induced irrep τ of dimension d_τ, the isotypic projector over the star block is P_τ = (d_τ/|G|) Σ_g χ_τ(g)* D(g), with the characters split per star arm so that lattice translations enter as phases. A real displacement field u has conjugate Fourier components on the arms k and −k, and for a complex-type τ (Frobenius-Schur indicator 0) the conjugate irrep τ* is a different irrep: the physically irreducible real representation is ρ = τ ⊕ τ*, of real dimension 2 d_τ, with the real projector

P_ρ = P_τ + P_τ*,

reported as one mode whose amplitude is |P_ρ u| and whose number of independent modes is tr(P_ρ P_H). On a star that contains its −k arms, P_τ* is the complex conjugate of P_τ with the arms k and −k exchanged; on a star without them, P_τ* lives on the −k star, the projected field is 2 Re(P_τ û) and the mode count 2 Re tr(P_τ P_H), and the −k partner is not listed again. The partner is recognized from the conjugate per-arm characters, independently of the labels; the line carries both labels (GM2+GM3+). A pseudoreal τ (indicator −1) is equivalent to its conjugate, P_τ is already real, and the single name stays; a real-type τ keeps the real part (P_τ + P_τ*)/2 = P_τ as before. In general P_ρ = dim ρ/(e |G|) Σ_g χ_ρ(g) D(g) with e = 1, 2, 4 for real, complex and pseudoreal type. The squared amplitudes of all lines add up to the squared total distortion.

Symmetry-mode analysis: settings, domains and comparison with AMPLIMODES (crystod-group --supergroup-cif)#

Command: crystod-group --supergroup-cif — 26. Symmetry-mode analysis

Which of the equivalent settings and order-parameter directions the mode table of --supergroup-cif reports, how modes at non-special k points and complex-type pairs are checked, and which differences from AMPLIMODES and ISODISTORT are conventions. The examples (SrTiO3 Pm-3m -> I4/mcm, La3Ni2O7 I4/mmm -> Cmcm, PbZrO3 Pm-3m -> Pbam, K2SeO4 Pnma -> Pna2₁, FeS2 Pa-3) are those of the command page.

Transformation matrix of the symmetry-mode analysis and AMPLIMODES#

The * Transformation matrix (parent -> child) * block uses the convention of AMPLIMODES and of the International Tables: the columns of P are the child conventional basis vectors in units of the parent conventional basis, (a', b', c') = (a, b, c) P, and the last column is the child origin p in parent fractional coordinates, so that a point with child coordinates x' sits at x = P x' + p in the parent cell.

The child basis of the * Transformation matrix (parent -> child) * block is the sublattice setting the analysis uses, the one closest to the orientation of the input files among the equivalent ones, so the matrix can differ from the AMPLIMODES one by a point operation of the parent (for SrTiO3 Pm-3m -> I4/mcm it is exactly the AMPLIMODES matrix, [[1, 1, 0], [-1, 1, 0], [0, 0, 2]] with origin (1/2, 1/2, 1/2); for a polar subgroup the origin along the polar direction is the one that minimizes the distortion, as in AMPLIMODES).

Which equivalent sublattice setting the symmetry-mode analysis reports#

A symmetric parent leaves the sublattice basis degenerate: every basis S·W (W a point operation of the parent) pairs the atoms equally well and merely presents the same distortion in a rotated setting — 24 of them for a cubic parent. CrystOD picks the one whose child axes are rotated least against the parent axes, both lattices taken as the input files orient them (a CIF is placed with c along z and a in the xz plane; a POSCAR keeps its lattice vectors as written), so the displacement table and the per-irrep VESTA files follow the axes of the subgroup file: PbTiO3 P4mm polarized along c is shown polarized along c of the cubic cell, not along b, and a POSCAR whose polar axis lies along Cartesian y comes out along b. When no setting aligns the axes — standard settings that permute them, a √2×√2 cell, a rhombohedral child in its hexagonal setting — the residual rotation is printed with the cell relation, as in the La3Ni2O7 example of the command page (Cmcm puts the long axis first: 90° plus the 45° in-plane rotation of the √2 cell). Irrep, isotropy subgroup, number of modes and amplitude do not depend on the setting, and neither does the direction column (next section).

Which order-parameter direction the symmetry-mode table prints#

The irreps of --supergroup-cif are labelled with the ISO-IR tables of ISOTROPY, and the direction column follows ISOTROPY as well: it names each order parameter by the ISOTROPY representative of its stratum, the row that crystod-group --parent SG --irrep IR (section 20) and ISOSUBGROUP list for that isotropy subgroup (for an irrep at a non-special k point, which these tables do not list, the simplest direction of the stratum), whichever of the domain-equivalent orientations the child file describes. When the child as given sits on another direction of the same stratum (a parent operation carries one onto the other), a (direction: ...) note under the table names it; the note is saved in sym_mode_<formula> with the table. The SrTiO3 child of the command page sits on R5-(a,0,0), and the table prints R5-(0,0,a), the I4/mcm row of --parent Pm-3m --irrep R5-. V2O3 R-3c -> C2/c is the same case for a two-dimensional irrep (its structures are in debug9_V2O3/ of the example directory example/26_symmetry_mode, which is not in the pip package):

crystod-group --supergroup-cif debug9_V2O3/POSCAR_V2O3_R-3c.cif --subgroup-cif debug9_V2O3/POSCAR_V2O3_C2c.cif --conventional

* Transformation matrix (parent -> child) *
R-3c (No. 167) -> C2/c (No. 15), conventional settings of the input files
  [     1     1  -1/3 ] [   1/3 ]
  [    -1     1   1/3 ] [   1/6 ]
  [     0     0   1/3 ] [   1/6 ]
(columns of P: the child basis vectors a', b', c' in parent units, (a', b', c') = (a, b, c) P;
 last column: the child origin p in parent fractional coordinates, x_parent = P x_child + p;
 the AMPLIMODES / Bilbao convention)

* Symmetry-mode decomposition *
k-vector         irrep   direction    isotropy subgroup   dim  amplitude (A)
(0,0,0)          GM1+    (a)          167 R-3c            2    0.0425
(0,0,0)          GM3+    (a,0)        15 C2/c             5    0.1222
(direction: GM3+ sits on its ISOTROPY representative as listed by
 crystod-group --parent 167 --irrep GM3+; the input child as given sits on the
 domain-equivalent direction GM3+ (0.577a,-a))

GM3+(a,0) is the C2/c row of crystod-group --parent R-3c --irrep GM3+; the child file is the monoclinic domain (0.577a,-a) of the same stratum. Only the label is moved to the representative: the cell relation, the transformation matrix (P, p) above, the displacement table, the normalized mode components and the VESTA files keep describing the child as given, in the setting closest to the input files, and the number of modes and the amplitude are the same for every domain. When the child already sits on the representative, as the La3Ni2O7 child of the command page does, no note is printed.

With several irreps the rows have to describe one domain together, so one parent operation moves all of them: the largest mode that has domain-equivalent directions is put on its --parent row, and each smaller mode, in order of decreasing amplitude, on the simplest direction left to it in that domain. A secondary mode can therefore read differently from its own --parent row; the note then names the deciding mode and the representatives of the other strata on their own. The Pbnm perovskite (POSCAR_ScF3_Pbnm and the CaTiO3 pair of example/26_symmetry_mode) prints R4+(0,a,a), the Imma row of --parent Pm-3m --irrep R4+, with M3+(a;0;0), as in the coupled row R4+(0,a,a) M3+(d;0;0) (Pnma) of --parent Pm-3m --irrep R4+ M3+, although M3+ on its own is tabulated as M3+(0;0;a). The isotropy-subgroup column is that of the direction the child as given sits on in that irrep (74 Imma for R4+ of a Pnma child), conjugate to the subgroup of the direction shown: the same type and index, except for a child on the mirror-image domain of a stratum of an enantiomorphic type (P4_32_12 for a row tabulated as P4_12_12), whose partner type the note names. In Python, each entry of SymmetryModeAnalysis(...).modes returns the printed direction, subgroup and index from label_info(); domain_label is the direction of the child as given and representative_label the representative of its stratum on its own.

Symmetry modes at non-special k points: gauge and AMPLIMODES agreement#

For the PbZrO3 antiferroelectric (Pm-3m -> Pbam, with modes at the Sigma point (1/4,1/4,0) and the S point (1/4,1/2,1/4)), every number agrees with the Bilbao AMPLIMODES reference — the dominant antipolar Pb mode SM2 (1.2871 Å) and the octahedral tilt R4+ (1.5232 Å), with the secondary S4, R5+, X3- and M5- — including each mode’s isotropy subgroup. The command does not re-measure the subgroups: testsuite section 26 freezes each single-mode displacement field of this example into the parent structure and checks with spglib that it measures as the isotropy subgroup in the table. The (direction: ...) note is the one of Which order-parameter direction the symmetry-mode table prints above: R4+, the largest mode, sits on its --parent Pm-3m --irrep R4+ row and fixes the domain in which the other rows are written. Small irreps at such points are computed with spgrep at the exact k and named/represented through the bundled ISO-IR tables (SM2, S4, …); since the ISOTROPY web tables fix their order-parameter axes with a phase gauge that is not part of the bundled data, the direction pattern of a line mode may differ from ISOSUBGROUP’s while the subgroup, dimension and amplitude — gauge-independent quantities — always agree.

The K2SeO4 lock-in ferroelectric (Pnma -> Pna2₁ at 3x the cell along a, SM = (1/3,0,0)) again matches AMPLIMODES entry by entry (including the polar GM4- that carries the spontaneous polarization of the lock-in phase).

Symmetry-mode conventions that differ from AMPLIMODES and ISODISTORT#

The physically meaningful columns — which irreps are active, their k-vectors, isotropy subgroups, dimensions and amplitudes — reproduce AMPLIMODES and ISODISTORT case for case. Four things can legitimately read differently, and none of them is a disagreement about the distortion:

  • The origin of a polar subgroup is free, so the amplitude of a polar irrep depends on where it is pinned. CrystOD places it at the minimum of the total distortion (the AMPLIMODES convention) and prints a note: the subgroup is polar line when it does. ISODISTORT pins the first orbit instead, which typically leaves its polar amplitude larger by exactly the removed rigid translation: the two differ by that translation and nothing else, so every other irrep is untouched.

  • Parity superscripts of a k ≠ 0 irrep depend on the parent’s origin. Two descriptions of the same crystal related by a translation that is in the Euclidean normalizer but not in the space group (rutile VO₂ with the metal at 2a rather than 2b) give the same subgroup and the same amplitude, but swap R1+ and R1-.

  • The k-vector is printed in the parent’s primitive basis, while Bilbao quotes the conventional one. For a C-centred monoclinic parent the M point reads (1/2,1/2,1/2) here and (0,1,1/2) there — the same point.

  • Order-parameter directions are those of ISOTROPY. The direction column is written in the basis of the bundled ISO-IR tables and names each stratum by its ISOTROPY representative, the row of crystod-group --parent and of ISOSUBGROUP, whatever domain the child file describes (the note under the table names that domain). AMPLIMODES gives the direction of the domain its transformation matrix describes, in the basis of the Bilbao representation matrices, so the letter pattern can differ ((a,-a,-a) against (a,a,a)) while naming the same stratum: for V2O3 R-3c -> C2/c AMPLIMODES prints GM3+ (-0.500a,0.866a), where CrystOD prints GM3+ (a,0) and names the input domain (0.577a,-a) in the note. The isotropy subgroup column, which is basis-independent, is the one to compare.

Complex-type mode pairs: the isotropy-subgroup check#

A complex-type irrep and its complex conjugate are listed by --supergroup-cif on one line because a real displacement field always carries the two together, with conjugate coefficients, so they form one physically irreducible order parameter (the D + D* projector above).

In the FeS2 (Pa-3) example, whose folding stars Γ and R carry complex-type pairs, the squared amplitudes add up to the squared total distortion (0.2540 Å), and freezing a pair’s projected field into the parent gives exactly the isotropy subgroup of its line.

Group-subgroup remapping: acceptance and effect on earlier analyses#

--tolerance (the spglib symmetry tolerance in Å, default 0.01 for --supergroup-cif) enters the mapping checks only through the space groups spglib identifies in the two files; it is not a threshold of the mapping. A mapping is accepted when the child operations leave the mapped structure and the displacement field invariant to machine precision (10⁻⁶ Å): spglib idealizes the child, so a symmetric pairing always passes, and a pairing that exchanges atoms breaks the symmetry by an interatomic distance, however weak the distortion.

Analyses whose least-distortion mapping already is a group-subgroup setting are not affected by the remapping (the NOTE line of the cell relation): on 2938 structure pairs of a materials survey and on the earlier inputs of testsuite section 26 the cell relation, the displacements, the mode table and the total are the same as without this step.

Matching the order-parameter directions of imaginary phonon modes (crystod-phonon --subgroup --modulate)#

Command: crystod-phonon --subgroup --modulate — Generating the daughter structures

How crystod-phonon --subgroup --modulate finds, for every order-parameter direction of the isotropy-subgroup table of an imaginary phonon level, the combination of degenerate modes (and of star arms) that realizes it, and why some directions end up as a note instead of a structure file.

Which combination of the degenerate modes realizes which direction is not fixed by any convention CrystOD could assume, so it is measured, not assumed: candidate combinations are generated (all with positive coefficients first, then the same ones with sign changes, which directions such as N1+(a;-a;a;a) need), the space group of each generated structure is determined with spglib, and the (space group, cell size, index) triple is matched against the enumerated table. When two directions of one irrep share that triple — R5+ of Pm-3m puts both (0,a,b) and (a,a,b) at C2/m, size 2, index 24 — the conventional cell metric separates them, so a domain of one is never written out under the other’s name, and the pattern of zero and equal components decides which label a structure takes.

A direction that no candidate reproduces is reported, never guessed at (the note: no candidate reproduced ...; generate it with --modulation by hand. line of the command page). Every enumerated row therefore ends up either as a file or as a note. The directions that need a note belong to large order parameters (a multi-arm star times a degenerate level), where the candidate search is cut off before it reaches them — not only low-symmetry ones: for X5- of cubic ScF3 (three arms times a pair) the six-component directions such as X5-(a,a;a,a;a,a) (R-3c) are among them.

Validation of crystod-group --parent against ISOSUBGROUP#

crystod-group --parent is the offline counterpart of ISOSUBGROUP of the ISOTROPY Software Suite (https://iso.byu.edu), and is validated against every reference table at hand: a sweep over the 910 downloaded ISOSUBGROUP tables in SUBGROUP/ (space groups 62-230, every parameter-free high-symmetry k point — 3535 irreps; script/validate_isosubgroup.py 62 230) reproduces the complete (subgroup, size, index) multiset of every strata table under the same irrep label: 3506 irreps agree exactly and 28 up to the enantiomorphic partner (a representative choice within one stratum orbit, printed as a note under the output whenever an enantiomorphic subgroup appears); no table agrees only under another label, so no label note is printed (results in SUBGROUP/VALIDATION.md; the last full sweep ran on 2026-10-05 with v0.4.3 and took about 32 minutes). The one irrep of the 3535 not compared is the six-dimensional L1 in the reference table of R-3m (No. 166) at L: the file SUBGROUP_SG166_L.txt holds the L table of R-3c (No. 167), so it lists an irrep that R-3m does not have (the L irreps of R-3m are L1+, L2+, L1- and L2-, which --parent 166 --kpoint L enumerates). Four reference files hold no result rows (SG 167 at F and GM, SG 207 at M and X), so those irreps are not compared and are not counted in the 3535, and there are no reference tables for space groups 1-61. If you use this feature, please cite: H. T. Stokes, S. van Orden and B. J. Campbell, “Tool for Generating Isotropy Subgroups of Crystallographic Space Groups”, J. Appl. Cryst. 49, 1849-1853 (2016).

Magnetic isotropy subgroups (crystod-mag --parent)#

Command: crystod-mag --parent SG --qpoint K — 44. Magnetic isotropy subgroups

Which magnetic space group a magnetic order of one irrep gives — the magnetic counterpart of the isotropy subgroups of crystod-group --parent — and how crystod-mag --parent identifies these groups and was validated against the tables of the ISOTROPY Software Suite.

The magnetic isotropy subgroup of a time-odd order parameter#

The paramagnetic phase has the grey group G1’ = G + θG, and a magnetic order that transforms as one irrep lowers it to the magnetic isotropy subgroup

H(η) = { g ∈ G : D(g) η = η } + { θg : D(g) η = −η }.

A moment is odd under time reversal, so θ acts as −1 on the order parameter (the irrep is ISOTROPY’s mGM4+; it is printed without the m, as everywhere in crystod-mag).

In Pm-3m at Γ (crystod-mag --parent Pm-3m --qpoint GM), GM4+ is the ferromagnet: moments along a cube axis give P4/mm’m’, along [111] R-3m’, along [110] Cmm’m’. Because θ acts as −1, the group acting on the order parameter is D(G) together with −D(G), and its strata include directions that the structural table does not have: moments in a (100) plane (P2’/m’) or a (110) plane (C2’/m’) are the general direction P-1 of crystod-group --parent Pm-3m --irrep GM4+. The index [G1’ : H] is the number of magnetic domains (6 for P4/mm’m’: ±x, ±y, ±z). At a k point off Γ the subgroups are of type IV wherever a translation reverses the order parameter (R4+ of Pm-3m: I_c4/mcm, R_I-3c, …).

How crystod-mag identifies the magnetic isotropy subgroups#

Each stabilizer is turned into a structure of two generic orbits with generic moments and identified by spglib, and the number of operations spglib finds must equal that of the stabilizer. spglib’s name is also checked against the type (from the operations) and the space groups of the unitary operations and of all operations: the BNS number begins with the family group for types I-III and with the unitary group for type IV. spglib 2.x cannot name three type IV groups, 37.184 C_ccc2, 37.185 C_acc2 and 37.186 C_Acc2 (unitary group Ccc2): its database holds operations of type Cmc2_1 for two of them, so it returned 36.177 or 36.179 or nothing. They are named from their unitary and family groups (Ccc2.1'_c[Cmm2], Ccc2.1'_a[Pcc2], Ccc2.1'_A[Fmm2]). The same check runs inside mag.magnetic_space_group, so crystod-mag --qpoint names these three groups correctly as well.

Validation of crystod-mag --parent against ISOTROPY data_magnetic.txt#

For every special k point of the 230 space groups, the multiset of (BNS number, free parameters) of every irrep equals the magnetic isotropy subgroups tabulated in the ISOTROPY Software Suite (data_magnetic.txt: 16721 subgroups of 4777 irreps). Two kinds of difference are allowed: an enantiomorphic partner as the representative of a stratum (28 irreps), and the spelling of a complex pair whose partner lies at -k (P1PA1 here, P1P1 there). Testsuite section 44 repeats the comparison for 24 space groups when ISOTROPY’s data files are in iso/, and for all 230 with CRYSTOD_MAG_ISO_SWEEP=1. It also checks that every spin basis of crystod-mag --qpoint for AlNi3 and TiO2 has a magnetic isotropy subgroup of its irrep as its group.
