05-01. Magnetic arrangements of given irreps (--irrep with -c)#

Example directory: example/05-01_mag_irrep (testsuite section 05-01); after pip install: crystod-example 05-01

crystod-mag --parent SG --irrep IR tells which magnetic space group every order-parameter direction of an irrep gives, without a structure. With a structure and an element instead of --parent, every row of that table (with several irreps, every row of the coupled table) is placed on the sites of the element: the moments of the arrangement in a magnetic cell common to every arm of every star, its magnetic space group identified again from the structure and shown next to the table’s, the spin listing, the MAGMOM line, one VESTA file per row and the magnetic-cell POSCAR. An irrep that the spins of the element do not carry is refused with the decomposition at its k point.

See also

Theory: Arrangements of the magnetic isotropy subgroups on a crystal — the spin modes that transform with the matrices of the table, why the group of every arrangement is the row’s group by construction, copies, the free amplitudes and the coupled case.

How to read it:

  • Rows are those of crystod-mag --parent SG --irrep ..., in the same order, with the same labels, groups, sizes and indices: the arrangement of a row is built from the order parameter the table identifies its group from, so its group is the row’s group by construction. The column structure and the check (on the written moments): line are the independent check, made on the moments exactly as the MAGMOM line, the listing and the VESTA file carry them (4 decimals, moment tolerance 3e-4 with the largest moment 1): spglib on the magnetic cell with all atoms and moments (in the cell of its translations without time reversal, the larger cell as fallback), and the numbers of elements of G1’/T_S that fix the moments and the order parameter. A disagreement is printed as a NOTE on the row.

  • Reproduce. Every block ends its header with reproduce: --order-parameter "..." (and --copies ... when an irrep has several copies): those numbers, given back with the same --irrep, rebuild the same written moments, weights included.

  • Letters. A row with several letters is shown with generic values (the ones printed, largest 1): other values with the same pattern of zero and equal components give the same group, and symmetry does not fix them. The moments: line tells whether the moments of that representative are equal on all sites, take several sizes or vanish on some sites.

  • Direction components refer to the irrep basis of the table (as with --parent), not to the axes of the POSCAR: GM4+(0,0,a) of Mn3GaN puts the moments along y. The spin listing gives the moments.

  • Copies. An irrep that occurs more than once (GM4+ twice on Mn of Mn3GaN; several elements or Wyckoff orbits) gives one arrangement per choice of copies (c1, c2, … in the block titles and file names). The copies are split by element, by Wyckoff orbit and, at q = 0, into the ferromagnetic combination and the antiferromagnetic ones, named by multipole rank as the --qpoint report names its bases. Any mixture of copies with the same order parameter has the same group.

  • Several irreps. The arrangements are those of the coupled rows (every irrep condensed); the parts are scaled to largest moment 1 each and weighted. Without --coefficients every row takes the first of the weight sets 1 : 1, 1 : 0.5, 1 : 0.75, 1 : 0.25, 1 : 0.6, 1 : 0.4 (three irreps: analogous sets) under which no site that a part reaches has a vanishing moment, and says why when it is not 1 : 1 (weights: 1 : 1 would cancel the moments on 8 sites; Mn3GaN R4- M3-: rows 1, 5, 6, 13 use 1 : 0.5). The moments: line separates sites that no irrep reaches (zero on 8 sites (no moment in R4-, M3-)) from moments that given --coefficients cancel (cancelled on 8 sites by the weights). --irrep R4- alone exports the rows of one irrep.

  • Many copies. All choices of copies are exported while there are at most 6 per row; otherwise only the first, with a NOTE, and --copies selects another one. --order-parameter runs save their report as ..._op and tag their blocks and files with the copies.

  • Conventional cell. The magnetic cell is a diagonal supercell of the primitive cell, which is awkward to look at for a centred lattice. --conventional writes every arrangement in the conventional cell instead: the cell that --qpoint --conventional uses for the same POSCAR (the conventional matrix of the centring letter applied to the primitive cell as given, so the input should be the standard primitive cell of phonopy --symmetry, a PPOSCAR), times the smallest multiples along its axes for which k . T is an integer for every arm of every star. The spin modes, weights, rows and the counts over G1’/T_S stay on the diagonal magnetic cell (named in the Magnetic (commensurate) supercell: line); the moments are re-tiled onto the conventional cell, which is exact because the arrangement is periodic under every such translation. The spin listing, the MAGMOM line, the site counts of the moments: line, the net moment, the files and the column structure (spglib on the conventional cell as written) refer to the conventional cell, and the reproduce: line ends with --conventional. On a P lattice only the names and the Display cell: line change. An input primitive cell off the standard primitive setting gives a cell that is not the conventional one, and a NOTE under the Display cell: line says so (the arrangements hold for the cell as written).

Errors

An irrep that the spins of the element(s) do not carry, an unknown label, an irrep given twice, --qpoint (the label carries its k point) and the options of the --qpoint report (--superpose, --show-operations, --subgroup-poscar, --phonon-angular-momentum) stop with an ERROR: line and no files; every invalid irrep is reported at once:

$ crystod-mag -c POSCAR_Mn3GaN_Pm-3m --element Mn --irrep R4+
ERROR: R4+ is not contained in the spin representation of Mn at R [0.5, 0.5, 0.5]; the decomposition there is 1.0 [R1-(1)] + 1.0 [R3-(2)] + 1.0 [R4-(3)] + 1.0 [R5-(3)]

--coefficients with a zero, nan or inf value, the wrong number of values or a weight below 1e-3 of the largest (it would vanish in the written moments), --copies beyond the copies of an irrep, and a -c file that is not a POSCAR are refused the same way. On a structure off its ideal positions whose irreps the survey cannot label at the given --tolerance, the decomposition is printed with irrep_N names and a NOTE, the copies come from the irrep matrices alone, and every row is still checked on the written moments; symmetrizing the structure first is better.

The magnetic cell is the diagonal supercell commensurate with every arm of every star; it is refused above 64 primitive cells or 12 cells along one axis. With --conventional the conventional cell is refused above 12 conventional cells along one axis or 256 primitive cells; the ERROR names it and suggests running without --conventional.

05-02. Symmetry-adapted spin bases#

Example directory: example/05-02_spin_basis (testsuite section 05-02); after pip install: crystod-example 05-02

Note

The example/... paths on this page are those of a clone of the repository, used from its root. After pip install CrystOD, run crystod-example CC-NN (the number of the example directory) in your working directory first: it copies that directory and the shared test_POSCARs/ into ./example/, and the commands then run from there unchanged (see crystod-example).

Which magnetic orders can the moments on the sites of one element take in this crystal? crystod-mag enumerates every symmetry-distinct ferromagnetic and antiferromagnetic arrangement at a q point, sorted by irrep, and prints each with its per-atom spin directions, a ready-to-paste VASP or Quantum ESPRESSO magnetization input, a POSCAR of the magnetic cell and a VESTA file with spin arrows. The enumeration follows the cluster-multipole / symmetry-adapted multipole moment (SAMM) framework of M.-T. Suzuki et al. [PRB 95, 094406 (2017); PRB 99, 174407 (2019)].

See also

Theory: Spin bases as axial-vector SALCs — why the spins are treated as axial vectors, how the FM dipole is split from the higher multipoles and the multipole ranks are assigned, how complex and two-dimensional irreps get real partners, and which combined directions symmetry singles out.

How to read the output.

  • * Spin (axial-vector) representation *: the spin degrees of freedom of the magnetic sites (3 per site, 9 for the three Ni of AlNi3) and their irreps at q; 2 x GM4+(3) is the three-dimensional irrep GM4+ occurring twice.

  • * Symmetry-adapted spin bases *: one line per copy of an irrep. FM and AFM refer to the net moment; every AFM basis satisfies sum_i S_i = 0 exactly. The copies of an irrep are named after the ranks at which it occurs among the magnetic multipoles, lowest first: dipole (rank 1, the FM copy), quadrupole (rank 2), octupole (rank 3), hexadecapole (rank 4), …; an AFM basis is not always an octupole or higher (the in-plane orders of Cr2O3 are GM3-(2) [AFM, quadrupole], section 05-03).

  • For the Mn3Ir-type Ni 3c cluster of AlNi3, 9 dims = 2 x GM4+(3) + GM5+(3): the GM4+ (T1g) cluster dipole (FM), the GM4+ (T1g) cluster octupole (AFM: the experimentally realized 120-degree structure of Mn3Ir, which shares the irrep with the dipole and hence allows the anomalous Hall effect), and the GM5+ (T2g) cluster octupole (AFM).

  • component 111: one exported arrangement of the basis, tagged by its direction (the tags are listed in section 05-03); Ni1: S = [...] the spin of each magnetic site, net moment their sum.

  • magnetic space group … collinear order: the symmetry of the arrangement, the responses it allows and its class (section 05-03).

  • spin directions (all atoms, POSCAR order) and MAGMOM =: printed by default for every basis vector, the spins of all atoms of the magnetic cell, in the atom order of the magnetic-cell POSCAR written by the run.

  • Survey mode (no --qpoint): the spin (axial-vector) irrep decomposition is listed for every special k point of the space group — the magnetic counterpart of the crystod SALC survey (e.g. for AlNi3: GM: 2.0 [GM4+(3)] + 1.0 [GM5+(3)], R: R2+ + R3+ + R4+ + R5+, …). At a time-reversal-invariant point a complex irrep and its conjugate are counted once under their pair label, as with --qpoint (2.0 [GM3+GM4+(2)] for the Ti sites of P4/m Ti2Ga3). The main command prints the same terms in the format of its orbital SALC, with the little group of every k point: crystod -c POSCAR --element Ni --axial (see Spin configurations).

Combined directions of two- and three-dimensional bases

Magnetic supercell at q ≠ 0

Magnetic supercell at q ≠ 0.

  • For q != 0 (e.g. --qpoint R) the commensurate magnetic supercell is built automatically, with the Bloch phase applied to the spins and the MAGMOM/VESTA output referring to the supercell.

  • The atoms of the supercell are listed atom by atom, as in the SPOSCAR of phonopy: each atom of the primitive cell is followed by its images in the other cells of the magnetic cell (for AlNi3 at R, the 2x2x2 cell lists Al1 to Al8 and then Ni9 to Ni32), so the species blocks of the input stay grouped and the POTCAR of the input cell matches the SPOSCAR_* file. 05-03. Magnetic space groups and collinear classes (--classify-only)#

Example directory: example/05-03_magnetic_group (testsuite section 05-03); after pip install: crystod-example 05-03

Which of the magnetic orders that the symmetry of the crystal admits is an altermagnet, and which one allows a magnetoelectric effect? Every spin basis vector that crystod-mag --qpoint exports is also an arrangement of moments with its own symmetry: for each exported component the command gives the magnetic space group (MSG) of that arrangement, its magnetic point group (MPG), the macroscopic responses the MPG allows by symmetry (a net magnetization, an electric polarization, the linear magnetoelectric effect, piezomagnetism) and, for a collinear arrangement, its class: ferromagnet, ferrimagnet, conventional antiferromagnet, or altermagnet with its d-, g- or i-wave type.

How to read the output.

  • component: the tag of the listing and of the VESTA file name of the component: x, y, z, 110, 111, 1m10, aa, …, with _2, _3 when the same tag occurs again in the same basis (x_2 is the second x component and the file ..._x_2.vesta), and comp<N> for a component without a single direction: the N-th basis vector of its space, counted from 1 like the mode numbers of the other commands (POSCAR_AlNi3_spin_R4+_R_comp1.vesta is the first of the three R4+ components). At a q that is not time-reversal invariant a d-dimensional basis has 2d components, the rows w (comp1 .. comp<d>) and their partners i w (comp<d+1> .. comp<2d>, or the axis tag of a collinear row); dir<k> is a one-parameter order-parameter direction exported on its own (section 05-06).

  • MSG (BNS), magnetic space group: the group of the arrangement as spglib finds it, with its Belov-Neronova-Smirnova (BNS) symbol and number (P4_2'/mnm', 136.499) and its type: I, no operation with time reversal; II, the grey group of a non-magnetic crystal; III, half of the operations combined with time reversal (primed); IV, a lattice translation combined with time reversal (the subscript of the lattice letter, P_I, C_c, R_I). The operations are counted in the magnetic cell.

  • MPG, magnetic point group: derived from the BNS symbol and checked against the operations (a symbol that disagrees is printed with ?).

  • MSG (UNI), UNI symbol: the unified symbol of Campbell et al., Acta Cryst. A78, 99 (2022): the BNS symbol with .1 (type I) or .1' (type II), unchanged for type III (P4_2'/mnm'); a type IV group reads .1'_t[F], t the anti-translation and F the family space group (P_I4/mnc is P4/mnc.1'_I[I4/mmm]).

  • net magnetization, allowed by symmetry: the direction of a net magnetization the MPG allows, in the Cartesian axes of the input cell (along z, in the xy plane, any direction, none), and the numbers of independent components of the linear magnetoelectric tensor and of the piezomagnetic tensor. Symmetry decides what is allowed, not how large it is: an allowed response can be small. An AFM basis vector that shares its irrep with the FM dipole (the GM4+ octupoles of AlNi3) allows a net magnetization by symmetry although its own moments add up to zero.

  • electric polarization, after the net magnetization: the direction of a spontaneous electric polarization the MPG allows, in the same words. The polarization is a time-even polar vector, so time reversal does not constrain it: it is allowed when the point group of the spatial parts of all operations is polar, and an MPG with -1' (Cr2O3, -3’m’) forbids it as -1 does. The direction is followed by (induced by the spin order) when the crystal without spins (the same structure, same tolerance) allows no polarization, and by (the crystal without spins: along z) when that crystal allows fewer components than the spin arrangement; nothing follows it when both allow the same components.

  • class, collinear order, from the signs of the moments along their common axis only (nonrelativistic): ferromagnet and ferrimagnet (nonzero net moment), compensated, sublattices not related by symmetry, antiferromagnet (T tau) (a pure translation exchanges the sublattices) and antiferromagnet (P T) (the inversion does; the bands stay spin degenerate in the nonrelativistic limit), altermagnet (d-wave) / (g-wave) / (i-wave) (only rotations or mirrors exchange the sublattices), and noncollinear (not classified). For an altermagnet the listing adds the lowest-order spin splitting allowed by symmetry (~ k_x k_y for rutile) and the spin-degenerate planes, in the Cartesian axes of the input cell.

More on reading the output

  • Two frames meet in one row: the MSG and MPG symbols are those of the standard BNS setting, whatever the orientation of the input cell, while the net magnetization, the spin-splitting polynomials and the degenerate planes are given in the Cartesian axes of the input cell. A row Pnn'm' ... mm'm' ... along y is therefore consistent: the standard setting of Pnn’m’ allows a moment along its own x axis, which is y of the input cell.

  • magnetic space group: not identified by spglib (N operations, M with time reversal): the MSG, type and MPG cells of the table hold -, and the responses and the class are still computed from the operations.

  • At q = 0 every AFM basis vector has a zero net moment, and the closing sentence All AFM bases satisfy sum_i S_i = 0 says so. A note under the table names every merged pair of a complex irrep and its conjugate (GM3+GM4+(2), the label that crystod-group --parent prints for the same pair); each copy of such a pair has two real partners.

Limits

Limits.

  • Only collinear arrangements are classified; a noncollinear component gets noncollinear (not classified) (its MSG and responses are still given).

  • The table describes the components as crystod-mag exports them: for an irrep of dimension two or three each exported component is one arrangement, and another partner direction can have another MSG.

  • At q != 0 the components span the real order-parameter space of their basis (at a q that is not time-reversal invariant, the rows w and their partners i w), and every one-parameter direction of it is exported, so no magnetic isotropy subgroup depends on a phase convention; the components of a direction with two or more free parameters are not singled out (free in * Order-parameter directions *).

  • The MPG symbol is derived from the BNS symbol and checked; the operations (MagneticGroupInfo.rotations, .translations, .time_reversals) are the authority.

  • --classify-only needs --qpoint; the survey mode (no --qpoint) does not classify.

  • At q = 0 the partners of a two- or three-dimensional irrep lie along directions that symmetry fixes; which of several equivalent choices is printed is a convention (in a trigonal or hexagonal crystal the a axis of the input cell and the direction perpendicular to it, whichever of the equivalent in-plane axes spglib calls a), and a rotated input cell gives the same groups and classes with the directions rotated. The partners of a merged complex pair are not fixed by symmetry and follow a convention (Real partners of merged complex pairs and repeated irreps).

Reading notes: secondary order parameters, sizes and indices

  • Every row condenses all irreps given. The coupled table never repeats a one-irrep direction with the other irreps at zero.

  • Rows with the group of a one-irrep row. Some coupled rows have the same magnetic space group, size and index as a row of one irrep alone. In Pm-3m --irrep GM4+ GM5+, 8 of the 12 one-irrep directions do so: the ferromagnet along [011], Cmm’m’, is the coupled row GM4+(0,a,-a) GM5+(0,d,d) 65.486 Cmm’m’ with the index 12 of GM4+(0,a,a) alone, whereas the ferromagnet along z (P4/mm’m’) induces no GM5+ and has no coupled row of its own.   • Sizes. The cell of the coupled subgroup contains the cells of both irreps: R4+ (size 2) and M3+ (size 2 or 4) give 4 or 8.

  • Indices. The index is the number of domains of the coupled order. The coupled subgroup lies in the subgroup of each irrep alone, so its index is a multiple of theirs (P_C4/mbm: 12, twice the 6 of R4+(0,0,a) and of M3+(0;0;d) alone).

  • Labels. The irreps are printed without the m of ISOTROPY (mR4+), as everywhere in crystod-mag; mR4+ is accepted as input.

Time-reversal-invariant q and merged pairs

At a time-reversal-invariant q (R, X, M of a cubic crystal) the bases are real and have their d components as before; the block lists their directions as well, matched to the components and combinations: for AlNi3 at R, R4+(0,0,a) is comp1 (I_c4/mcm), R4+(0,a,a) aa0 (I_bmma) and R4+(a,a,a) aaa (R_I-3c), so no dir<k> is needed. A merged complex pair (A5A6(4) of ZnO at A) has only directions with two or more free parameters, all free. A mirror-image representative is matched to its table row with a note (Z3: 96.148 P_c4_32_12 is the enantiomorphic partner of the table's 92.116 for TiO2 at Z).

Repeated irreps and other q points

At a q that is not time-reversal invariant, the copies of an irrep that occurs more than once are chosen so that the operations taking q to -q keep each copy (spgrep’s copies are mixed by them): for Mn of YMnO3 at q = (1/3,0,0) (--qpoint 1/3 0 0, the line SM, no table) each of SM2, SM3 and SM4 occurs twice, and each copy has its two one-parameter directions (Cm’cm, Cm’c’m, …), exported as dir1, dir2 and named by their coefficients. Where no such choice exists (an irrep paired with another one), the directions of the joint space are listed under both labels (SM4(1) + SM4(1)); a basis whose fields do not span a space the operations keep gets a NOTE in the block instead.

How to read the output.

  • * Spin bases on the subgroup structure *, one row per field, grouped by the directions of * Order-parameter directions *:

    • a component as exported (comp1, z_3, aa, dir1): the irreps of CHILD it is made of, with the squared norms of their parts on CHILD (0.25 GM1 + 0.75 GM4), and its magnetic space group on CHILD (spglib on the cell of CHILD with these moments);

  • * K3(1) [AFM (q != 0)] on 185_PPOSCAR_YMnO3 *: every aligned field with the spins of the magnetic sites of CHILD (Mn1 .. How the alignment works

The cell relation comes from the structure mapping of crystod-group --correlate --supergroup-poscar (SymmetryModeAnalysis), expressed between the cells of the two input files. For every basis, the real order-parameter space V and the action of G1’ on it are those of * Order-parameter directions *. The fields of V placed on CHILD are decomposed with the projection operators of the irreps of CHILD at GM (its spin representation on the magnetic sites, ISO-IR labels in the frame of CHILD, a complex pair merged): U_Γ is the subspace of V whose fields are of irrep Γ alone. For every one-parameter direction, its domains (the distinct images of its line under G1’) that lie in some U_Γ are the aligned fields; one row is kept per irrep and magnetic space group on CHILD, the domain nearest to an exported component of the direction. Two checks in section 05-07 are independent of this construction: every aligned field lies in the span of the bases that crystod-mag -c CHILD --qpoint GM finds for its irrep, and in the magnetic cell of the parent its magnetic space group is the magnetic isotropy subgroup of its direction (it is a domain of that direction).

At a time-reversal-invariant q the same holds without the phase: for AlNi3 (Pm-3m) at R with a two-cell child whose Al atoms carry an R-point displacement (I4/mmm), R4+ folds onto GM1- + GM5-, the exported R4+ comp2 is already GM1- (comp2_GM1-, 0 degrees, I4/m’m’m’), and the (a,a,a) direction has no domain in one irrep.

Options and requirements

  • q ≠ 0, and the cell of CHILD must be a magnetic cell of q: q . a’, q . b’, q . c’ integers for its lattice vectors (q folds onto GM of CHILD). At GM, run crystod-mag on CHILD itself; a q that does not fold (M of YMnO3 on the √3×√3 cell) stops with an ERROR that gives q . a’, q . b’, q . c’.

  • CHILD is a primitive cell (POSCAR or CIF) of the same compound, with size times the magnetic atoms of the parent, size the number of primitive cells of the parent in it; a conventional cell or a supercell is refused (the PPOSCAR of phonopy --symmetry is a primitive cell).

  • The mapping and the groups on CHILD use the tolerance of crystod-group --correlate (0.01 Å), or --tolerance when that is larger; a magnetic atom of CHILD more than 1.8 Å from its site stops the run.

  • --classify-only prints the three blocks and writes no files; --format qe gives the Quantum ESPRESSO lines of the aligned fields; --conventional changes only the files of the parent cell.

  • When an operation of CHILD takes q to another arm of its star (a child of a multi-arm order), the fields of one basis are not all sums of fields of single irreps of CHILD; a NOTE says so and only the directions inside one irrep are aligned.

05-08. Phonon angular momentum near GM (--phonon-angular-momentum)#

Example directory: example/05-08_phonon_angular_momentum (testsuite section 05-08); after pip install: crystod-example 05-08

Can the phonons of a magnetic arrangement carry angular momentum, and how does it vary with the wavevector near Γ? The angular momentum l(q) of a phonon branch is an axial vector, odd under time reversal, and the magnetic point group fixes the lowest-order polynomial in q that each component can follow: s-wave (a constant, as for a net magnetization), p-wave (linear in q), d-wave, f-wave and so on up to j-wave (degree 7), together with the nodal planes through Γ on which a component must vanish. --phonon-angular-momentum adds this to every component of crystod-mag --qpoint, and with --classify-only gives one table that starts with the paramagnetic crystal itself, so the option is useful for non-magnetic crystals as well.

How to read the output.

  • paramagnetic: the grey group of the crystal without spins (4/mmm1'), the symmetry of phonons computed from ordinary force constants. Rutile is centrosymmetric, so P T is in it and no component survives: the phonon angular momentum of rutile needs the magnetic order and a time-reversal-odd term in the lattice dynamics.

  • l_x, l_y, l_z: the wave letter of the lowest allowed degree; l_i ~: the polynomials of that degree (Cartesian axes of the input cell; the letters s, p, d, f, g, h, i, j for the degrees 0 to 7 are the convention used here); planes: only the nodal planes through Γ pinned by a single operation, which can be fewer than the degree (the other nodes are then not fixed in position by symmetry). The altermagnetic GM2+ arrangement (moments along z, 4'/mmm') allows the d-wave l_z ~ q_x q_y with two planes, the same pattern as its spin splitting k_x k_y.

  • s / const: a constant component, allowed exactly where the group allows a net magnetization along that axis (GM3+, the ferromagnet along z, has l_z s-wave; the GM5+ octupole along x allows a magnetization along y and has l_y s-wave).

  • The number of planes can be smaller than the degree: the group 4' gives l_z ~ q_z with no plane, 4'/m the d-wave pair q_x^2 - q_y^2, q_x q_y with no plane.

Details: what is computed, layers, limits

  • For each degree n = 0, 1, …, 9 the homogeneous polynomial vector fields F(q) with F(A q) = B F(q) for every operation (A = s R, B = s det(R) R, s = -1 with time reversal) are found as a null space; the wave of l_i is the lowest n at which a field has a nonzero i-th component. A degree beyond j-wave is printed as its number (8), and a component with no form up to degree 9 as none up to degree 9 (it does not happen in the groups tested).

  • The enforced nodal planes of l_i come from single operations: a mirror without time reversal whose plane contains e_i, or a 2’ along the plane normal when e_i is along it.

  • The magnetic point group is that of the exported component (spglib on the magnetic cell, the operations in the Cartesian axes of the input cell); a group spglib does not name is still used through its operations (MPG -).

  • Layers (two-dimensional systems): crystod.mag.phonon_angular_momentum(..., plane_normal=[0, 0, 1]) restricts q to the plane; the nodes are then lines, and the group must map the normal to plus or minus itself. There is no command-line option for layers yet.

  • Not printed with --subgroup-poscar for the aligned fields on the child, and not in the survey (it needs --qpoint); --parent rejects the option.

05-09. Several magnetic elements (--element A B, --superpose)#

Example directory: example/05-09_multi_element (testsuite section 05-09); after pip install: crystod-example 05-09

Which magnetic arrangements does each irrep give when the spins sit on two different elements (two Wyckoff orbits)? The spin space is then the direct sum of the spin spaces of the elements, and an irrep can occur on both. --element Al Ni analyses every element as its single-element run does, prints the decomposition per element and in total, checks that the copies of one irrep on different elements transform with the same irrep matrices, and lists the arrangements with one copy per element along every high-symmetry direction of the listing ((a,0,0), (a,a,0), (a,a,a), … at Γ, the strata of section 05-06 at q ≠ 0) with the relative signs (+,+) and (+,-). --superpose adds any listed bases, of any irreps and elements, with given coefficients, the way crystod-phonon --modulation --mode ... --amplitude ... adds modes.

See also

Theory: Spin bases on several elements — the direct sum over the orbits, the copies of one irrep, why a superposition along one direction keeps the group of each copy (the table of the single-element runs is complete), and what symmetry leaves free.

How to read the output.

  • Decomposition: per element and in total (the direct sum). The bases of every element are exactly those of its single-element run; their rows in the table are numbered (no.), the numbers --superpose takes.

  • * Copies of one irrep on several elements *: for every irrep found on two or more elements, how the basis rows of each copy relate to those of the first copy. same matrices on the rows: the operations of the crystal act on both with the same matrices. rows mapped by (-x, y, z): the same irrep, but one row with the opposite sign convention (the Ni octupole copy of GM4+, whose exported 111 is therefore the domain [11-1]); the combined arrangements follow the direction through that map.

  • The combined rows (no. -, element Al+Ni) take one copy per element along one direction of the first element’s copy (z, 110, 111, …; dir<k> and the ISOTROPY label at q ≠ 0), each copy with largest moment 1 and signed as its listed component; (+,+) and (+,-) are the relative signs of the moments as printed. Their magnetic space group is the one of each copy alone; only the class (ferromagnet, ferrimagnet, compensated) and the moments differ. AlNi3 gives 20 (GM4+ along 5 directions, Al with either Ni copy, two signs); GM5+ occurs on Ni only.

Two different elements are never exchanged by an operation of the crystal, so a pattern that looks like one antiferromagnet on two sites is a different magnetic group when the two sites carry different elements. irrep content: the squared projection of the sum on the space of every basis (weights sum to 1). Without --classify-only the block is followed by the MAGMOM line and the VESTA file is written. With one element the same option works on the single-element listing; the numbers are then the rows of its table of magnetic groups, counted from 1.

Details: what is combined, limits

  • The copies of one irrep are compared through the matrices of the operations of the crystal (with the translations of the magnetic cell) on their basis rows: the map T with D_copy(g) T = T D_first(g) closest to the identity is found as a null space; the identity means the same matrices. At a q that is not time-reversal invariant the maps between copies form a two-dimensional family (a relative phase), and the one closest to the identity is used; every member gives the same groups.

  • The combinations use the first copy of the first element as reference; with three elements there are 2^2 sign patterns, and the copies of every element are combined in all ways (AlNi3: Al with either Ni copy).

  • At most 48 combined arrangements get a VESTA file (all are in the table); a NOTE then points to --superpose for the others.

  • --subgroup-poscar takes one element. MCP access to several elements is not yet available.
