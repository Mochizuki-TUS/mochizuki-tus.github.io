crystod-mag#

Symmetry-adapted spin bases (cluster multipoles / SAMM): every symmetry-distinct magnetic order allowed on the sites of one element, printed with its irrep, its per-atom spin directions, a ready-to-paste VASP or Quantum ESPRESSO magnetization input, and a VESTA file with spin arrows.

I want to …

command

enumerate the magnetic orders at q = 0

crystod-mag -c POSCAR --element Ni --qpoint 0 0 0

survey every special k point

crystod-mag -c POSCAR --element Ni

get a Quantum ESPRESSO input instead

... --format qe

build a magnetic supercell at q ≠ 0

crystod-mag -c POSCAR --element Ni --qpoint R

get the magnetic space group, the responses allowed by symmetry and the altermagnet / AFM class of every basis vector

crystod-mag -c POSCAR --element Ti --qpoint GM --classify-only (37)

36. Symmetry-adapted spin bases#

Example directory: example/36_spin_basis (testsuite section 36); after pip install: crystod-example 36

Note

The example/... paths on this page are those of a clone of the repository, used from its root. After pip install CrystOD, run crystod-example NN (the number of the example directory) in your working directory first: it copies that directory and the shared test_POSCARs/ into ./example/, and the commands then run from there unchanged (see crystod-example).

Treat the spins on the sites of a magnetic element as axial-vector degrees of freedom and decompose them into space-group irreps at a q point by projection — a complete, symmetry-exhaustive enumeration of the ferromagnetic and antiferromagnetic arrangements, following the cluster-multipole / symmetry-adapted multipole moment (SAMM) framework of M.-T. Suzuki et al. [PRB 95, 094406 (2017); PRB 99, 174407 (2019)]:

crystod-mag -c example/test_POSCARs/221_PPOSCAR_AlNi3 --element Ni --qpoint 0 0 0
crystod-mag -c example/test_POSCARs/221_PPOSCAR_AlNi3 --element Ni --qpoint 0 0 0 --format qe

# survey mode: spin-multipole irreps at ALL special k points
crystod-mag -c example/test_POSCARs/221_PPOSCAR_AlNi3 --element Ni

The output opens with the magnetic sites and the complete irrep enumeration — for the Ni 3c cluster of AlNi3 (the Mn3Ir geometry), 9 spin degrees of freedom decompose into exactly three symmetry-adapted families:


* Structure *
  Space group: Pm-3m (#221)
  Magnetic sites: Ni x 3
    Ni1: [0.5, 0.5, 0.0]
    Ni2: [0.5, 0.0, 0.5]
    Ni3: [0.0, 0.5, 0.5]

* Selected Q point *
  Selected q-point: GAMMA = [0.0, 0.0, 0.0]

* Spin (axial-vector) representation *
  Spin (axial-vector) representation on Ni sites: 9 dimensions
  Decomposition: 2 x GM4+(3) + GM5+(3)

* Symmetry-adapted spin bases *
  GM4+(3): dim 3 [FM, dipole]
  GM4+(3): dim 3 [AFM, octupole]
  GM5+(3): dim 3 [AFM, octupole]

Every basis vector is then printed with its per-atom spin directions, net moment, its magnetic space group and class (section 37), and the magnetization input. The [111] component of the GM4+ cluster octupole is the experimentally realized 120-degree structure of Mn3Ir — note the exactly vanishing net moment:

* GM4+(3) [AFM, octupole] *
  ...
  component 111:
    Ni1: S = [ 0.4082,  0.4082,  0.8165]
    Ni2: S = [ 0.4082, -0.8165, -0.4082]
    Ni3: S = [-0.8165,  0.4082, -0.4082]
    net moment: [0.0, 0.0, -0.0]
    magnetic space group: R-3m' (BNS 166.101, type III), 12 operations, 6 with time reversal
    magnetic point group: -3m'
    allowed by symmetry: net magnetization along [11-1]; linear magnetoelectric effect none; piezomagnetism 4 components
    collinear order: noncollinear (not classified)
    spin directions (all atoms, POSCAR order):
      Al1: [0, 0, 0]
      Ni2: [0.4082, 0.4082, 0.8165]
      Ni3: [0.4082, -0.8165, -0.4082]
      Ni4: [-0.8165, 0.4082, -0.4082]
    MAGMOM = 0 0 0   0.4082 0.4082 0.8165   0.4082 -0.8165 -0.4082   -0.8165 0.4082 -0.4082
...

* Output files *
  ...
  Spin basis written to: POSCAR_AlNi3_spin_GM4+_octupole_111.vesta
  ...

Per-atom spin directions and a ready-to-paste noncollinear magnetization input are printed by default for every basis vector. --format selects the input format:

  • vasp (default) prints a noncollinear MAGMOM line;

  • qe prints the Quantum ESPRESSO counterpart — noncolin = .true. with per-type starting_magnetization(i) / angle1(i) (polar angle from z) / angle2(i) (azimuth from x) for the &SYSTEM namelist, where the magnetic element is split into one atom type per distinct spin direction (the atom membership of each type is printed as comments for ATOMIC_SPECIES/ATOMIC_POSITIONS).

With --format qe, the same 120-degree octupole becomes (Ni split into three types because the three spins point in three different directions):

    Quantum ESPRESSO noncollinear magnetization (&SYSTEM):
      noncolin = .true.
      ! type 1 (Al): Al1 -- non-magnetic
      ! type 2 (Ni1): Ni2  S = [0.4082, 0.4082, 0.8165]
      starting_magnetization(2) = 1.0000
      angle1(2) = 35.2611
      angle2(2) = 45.0000
      ! type 3 (Ni2): Ni3  S = [0.4082, -0.8165, -0.4082]
      starting_magnetization(3) = 1.0000
      angle1(3) = 114.0928
      angle2(3) = -63.4378
      ! type 4 (Ni3): Ni4  S = [-0.8165, 0.4082, -0.4082]
      starting_magnetization(4) = 1.0000
      angle1(4) = 114.0928
      angle2(4) = 153.4378
      ! angle1 = polar angle from the z axis (deg); angle2 = azimuth from the x axis in the xy plane (deg).
      ! Split Ni into the types above in ATOMIC_SPECIES / ATOMIC_POSITIONS to realize this spin arrangement.

When --qpoint is omitted, the spin (axial-vector) irrep decomposition is listed for every special k point of the space group — the magnetic counterpart of the crystod SALC survey (e.g. for AlNi3: GM: 2.0 [GM4+(3)] + 1.0 [GM5+(3)], R: R2+ + R3+ + R4+ + R5+, …). At a time-reversal-invariant point a complex irrep and its conjugate are counted once under their pair label, as with --qpoint (2.0 [GM3+GM4+(2)] for the Ti sites of P4/m Ti2Ga3).

For the Mn3Ir-type Ni 3c cluster of AlNi3 this yields 9 dims = 2 x GM4+(3) + GM5+(3): the GM4+ (T1g) cluster dipole (FM), the GM4+ (T1g) cluster octupole (AFM: the experimentally realized 120-degree structure of Mn3Ir, which shares the irrep with the dipole and hence allows the anomalous Hall effect), and the GM5+ (T2g) cluster octupole (AFM). Every AFM basis satisfies sum_i S_i = 0 exactly. FM and AFM refer to the net moment; the copies of an irrep are named after the ranks at which it occurs among the magnetic multipoles, lowest first: dipole (rank 1, the FM copy), quadrupole (rank 2), octupole (rank 3), hexadecapole (rank 4), … In a centrosymmetric crystal the even ranks belong to the irreps odd under inversion, so an AFM basis is not always an octupole or higher: the in-plane orders of Cr2O3 are GM3-(2) [AFM, quadrupole] (section 37).

Each basis vector is exported as POSCAR_<formula>_spin_<irrep>_<dipole|quadrupole|octupole|...>_<direction>.vesta (e.g. POSCAR_AlNi3_spin_GM4+_octupole_111.vesta for the 120-degree Mn3Ir-type state), with spin arrows on the magnetic atoms scaled so that the largest spin gets --amplitude Angstroms (default 1.5). For q != 0 (e.g. --qpoint R) the commensurate magnetic supercell is built automatically, with the Bloch phase applied to the spins and the MAGMOM/VESTA output referring to the supercell. The supercell takes the denominator of each coordinate of q along the primitive axes, up to 12; a q that does not fit such a cell (0 0 0.15, that is 3/20) is refused with an ERROR that names the smallest commensurate supercell (1x1x20), instead of a cell that would cut the modulation. --conventional exports the spin structures in the conventional cell instead of the primitive cell (VESTA files get a _conv suffix, and for q != 0 the conventional cell is multiplied until the Bloch phase is commensurate, again with at most 12 cells per axis: NaCl at 1/5 1/7 0 is refused with --conventional, and the ERROR names the 5x7x1 primitive supercell that works without it). --tolerance sets the symmetry tolerance (default 1e-5, a distance in Angstrom). It is used by spglib and for matching the sites onto each other, both in the spin representation and in the collinear classification, and the Bloch phases are built from the site positions symmetrized by the space group, so a structure slightly off its ideal positions decomposes and classifies like the ideal one (an AlNi3 cell with the Ni coordinates off by about 1e-4 gives 2 x GM4+(3) + GM5+(3) and the groups and classes of the ideal cell with --tolerance 1e-2; the listings keep the input positions).

The input must be a primitive cell. It is used as given (the spin listings and MAGMOM follow its atom order), and a conventional cell or a supercell is refused with the same one-line ERROR in the survey and the --qpoint modes (the PPOSCAR of phonopy --symmetry is a primitive cell). For the two-atom cubic cell of bcc Fe:

ERROR: the input cell is not primitive (2 atoms, the primitive cell has 1); crystod-mag uses the cell as given (the spin listings and MAGMOM follow its atom order) and needs a primitive cell, such as the PPOSCAR of phonopy --symmetry (--conventional then exports the spin structures in the conventional cell).

See also

Theory: the construction is the SALC projection used elsewhere in CrystOD (1. Theoretical background) with the Cartesian part replaced by det(R) R, since spins are axial vectors; irrep labels come from spgrep + the bundled ISO-IR (ISOTROPY, Miller-Love) tables as usual. Within a multiply-occurring irrep the unique net-moment (dipole) combination is split off from the net-zero (higher-multipole) ones; this needs every copy of the irrep in the same real partner basis, so a two-dimensional irrep that spgrep gives in a complex basis (the E irreps of trigonal and hexagonal crystals, GM5+ of rutile) is first brought to a real form, and the partners of all copies are turned together onto directions that a two-fold axis or a mirror fixes (for an in-plane E irrep the a axis of the hexagonal cell, x of a standard cell, and the direction perpendicular to it, in the 321 and the 312 settings alike), as crystod-phonon --vibration chooses the displacement partners. A one-dimensional complex irrep and its conjugate are merged into one real two-dimensional space under their pair label (GM3+GM4+(2), the label crystod-group --parent prints), at q = 0 and at the other time-reversal-invariant q points (Z3+Z4+(2)); its partners are the real and imaginary parts of the complex basis vector, the real partners that crystod-phonon --vibration gives the two members of the pair. Multipole ranks (dipole, quadrupole, octupole, …) are assigned representation-theoretically from the parity-resolved angular-momentum characters (Suzuki’s Table III logic): the rank-p magnetic multipole transforms as angular momentum p with parity (-1)^(p+1) under inversion.

37. Magnetic space groups and collinear classes (--classify-only)#

Example directory: example/37_magnetic_group (testsuite section 37); after pip install: crystod-example 37

Every spin basis vector that crystod-mag --qpoint exports is also an arrangement of moments with its own symmetry. For each exported component the command gives the magnetic space group (MSG) of that arrangement, its magnetic point group (MPG), the macroscopic responses the MPG allows by symmetry (a net magnetization, the linear magnetoelectric effect, piezomagnetism) and, for a collinear arrangement, its class: ferromagnet, ferrimagnet, conventional antiferromagnet, or altermagnet with its d-, g- or i-wave type. It answers “which of these orders is an altermagnet, and which one allows a magnetoelectric effect?” for every order the symmetry of the crystal admits. --classify-only prints the table alone:

crystod-mag -c example/test_POSCARs/136_PPOSCAR_TiO2 --element Ti --qpoint GM --classify-only

...
* Symmetry-adapted spin bases *
  GM3+(1): dim 1 [FM, dipole]
  GM5+(2): dim 2 [FM, dipole]
  GM5+(2): dim 2 [AFM, octupole]
  GM2+(1): dim 1 [AFM, octupole]

* Magnetic groups and collinear classes *
basis                    component  MSG (BNS)             type  MPG      net magnetization  class
GM3+(1) [FM, dipole]     z          P4_2/mn'm' (136.501)  III   4/mm'm'  along z            ferromagnet
GM5+(2) [FM, dipole]     1m10       Cmm'm' (65.486)       III   mm'm'    along [1-10]       ferromagnet
GM5+(2) [FM, dipole]     110        Cmm'm' (65.486)       III   mm'm'    along [110]        ferromagnet
GM5+(2) [AFM, octupole]  1m10       Cmm'm' (65.486)       III   mm'm'    along [1-10]       altermagnet (d-wave)
GM5+(2) [AFM, octupole]  110        Cmm'm' (65.486)       III   mm'm'    along [110]        altermagnet (d-wave)
GM2+(1) [AFM, octupole]  z          P4_2'/mnm' (136.499)  III   4'/mmm'  none               altermagnet (d-wave)

How to read it:

  • magnetic space group is the group of the arrangement as spglib finds it (axial moments, get_magnetic_symmetry_dataset), written with its Belov-Neronova-Smirnova (BNS) symbol and number (P4_2'/mnm', 136.499; the symbol from the pymatgen table, the number from spglib) and its type: I, no operation with time reversal; II, the grey group of a non-magnetic crystal; III, half of the operations combined with time reversal (primed); IV, a lattice translation combined with time reversal (the subscript of the lattice letter, P_I, C_c, R_I). The operations are counted in the magnetic cell. MagneticGroupInfo.og_number holds the Opechowski-Guccione number.

  • magnetic point group is derived from the BNS symbol: the lattice letter and its subscript are dropped, glides (a, b, c, n, d, e) become m, screw axes become plain rotations (4_2' becomes 4'), every prime is kept, and a type II or IV group is the point group followed by 1' (P_I4/mnc gives 4/mmm1'). The symbol is then checked against the operations: the group it generates must have the same rotations with the same time-reversal flags (a symbol that disagrees is printed with ?; the derivation is tested on all 1651 magnetic space groups).

  • allowed by symmetry lists what the MPG allows: the net magnetization (a time-odd axial vector, Jahn symbol aeV) with its direction in the Cartesian axes of the input cell (along z, in the xy plane, any direction, none), and the numbers of independent components of the linear magnetoelectric tensor (aeV2, P_i = alpha_ij H_j) and of the piezomagnetic tensor (aeV[V2], M_i = Lambda_ijk sigma_jk), computed as in section 13 of crystod-group with the time-reversal flag of every operation. Symmetry decides what is allowed, not how large it is: an allowed response can be small. An AFM basis vector that shares its irrep with the FM dipole (the GM4+ octupoles of AlNi3) allows a net magnetization by symmetry although its own moments add up to zero.

  • collinear order classifies a collinear arrangement from the signs of its moments along their common axis: the operations of the space group of the non-magnetic crystal, combined with every lattice translation inside the magnetic cell, either keep the sign pattern, reverse it (exchange the two sublattices), or do neither. A nonzero net moment gives a ferromagnet (all signs equal) or a ferrimagnet; a compensated arrangement with no exchanging operation is listed as compensated, sublattices not related by symmetry; if a pure translation exchanges the sublattices, the order is a conventional antiferromagnet (T tau), as it is if the inversion does (P T): the bands stay spin degenerate in the nonrelativistic limit. When only rotations or mirrors (proper or improper, with or without a fractional translation) exchange the sublattices, the order is an altermagnet.

  • The altermagnet wave type follows L. Smejkal, J. Sinova and T. Jungwirth, Phys. Rev. X 12, 031042 (2022): with chi_A(R) = +1 on the rotation parts that keep the pattern and -1 on those that exchange the sublattices, the wave is the lowest even l (2, 4, 6: d, g, i) at which chi_A occurs in the angular-momentum representation D_l. The lowest-order spin splitting allowed by symmetry has the form of the polynomials of that degree with p(R k) = chi_A(R) p(k) (k_x k_y for rutile, k_x k_z (k_x^2 - 3 k_y^2) for the g-wave hematite order), in the Cartesian axes of the input cell. The spin-degenerate planes are those fixed by a mirror or a two-fold rotation that exchanges the sublattices (both give the same plane, because the nonrelativistic bands satisfy E(k) = E(-k)); lines along the axes of the other exchanging rotations are listed when they do not lie in one of the planes.

  • Two frames meet in one row. The MSG and MPG symbols are those of the standard BNS setting (pymatgen’s table), whatever the orientation of the input cell; the net magnetization, the spin-splitting polynomials and the degenerate planes are given in the Cartesian axes of the input cell. A row Pnn'm' ... mm'm' ... along y is therefore consistent: the standard setting of Pnn’m’ allows a moment along its own x axis, which is y of the input cell.

  • The component column holds the tag of the VESTA file name of the component: x, y, z, 111, 1m10, …, with _2, _3 when the same tag occurs again in the same basis (x_2 is the second x component and the file ..._x_2.vesta), and comp<N> for a component without a single direction: the N-th basis vector of its space, counted from 1 like the mode numbers of the other commands (POSCAR_AlNi3_spin_R4+_R_comp1.vesta is the first of the three R4+ components).

  • When spglib finds the operations of an arrangement but cannot identify the group in the cell given, the cell is re-expressed by its Niggli- and Delaunay-reduced bases and a few sheared bases of the same lattice; if no basis is identified, the line reads magnetic space group: not identified by spglib (N operations, M with time reversal), the MSG, type and MPG cells of the table hold -, and the responses and the class are still computed from the operations.

  • At q = 0 every AFM basis vector has a zero net moment, and the closing sentence All AFM bases satisfy sum_i S_i = 0 says so. Two cases need more than one FM row per irrep or a merged irrep:

    • A one-dimensional complex irrep and its conjugate (the little co-group is 3, -3, 4, -4, 4/m, 6, -6, 6/m, 23 or m-3, for example GM3+ and GM4+ of P4/m) carry the same real spin arrangements: time reversal joins them into one physically irreducible real space of dimension two. They are listed once under their pair label, GM3+GM4+(2), the label that crystod-group --parent prints for the same pair, and each copy has two real partners, the real and imaginary parts of the complex basis vector (the real partners crystod-phonon --vibration gives the two members). Every operation acts on the two partners as a rotation, so no symmetry element fixes a direction in the space, and the partners follow a convention instead: the net moment of the first partner of the FM dipole copy lies along the a axis (x of a standard cell) and that of the second perpendicular to it, in tetragonal, trigonal and hexagonal crystals alike; the first partner of an AFM copy carries as much of its moments along the a axis as it can (then along the direction perpendicular to a and c, then the third axis). The net moments are separated on the complex copies, so the pair has at most one FM dipole copy and net-zero AFM copies. A note under the table names every merged pair. The same pairs are merged at the other time-reversal-invariant q points (Z3+Z4+(2) at Z of P4/m), where the irreps of a pair can also be two-dimensional: each copy then gives one real four-dimensional space under the pair label, the real parts of its partners followed by the imaginary parts (A5A6(4) for Zn at A of ZnO, P6_3mc).

    • An irrep that occurs more than once in a single moment (the moments perpendicular to the two-fold axis of a monoclinic crystal, any moment of a triclinic one) has one FM row per allowed axis: the uniform moments along the a axis, the direction perpendicular to a and c, and the third axis of that frame (the Cartesian axes of a triclinic cell), projected onto the irrep. The AFM rows are the rest of the space, each with its moments along one of those axes where symmetry keeps the axes apart.

Moment directions versus sign pattern. The MSG and MPG use the actual moment directions, as a relativistic (spin-orbit coupled) description requires; the class uses only the sign pattern, as in the nonrelativistic spin-group description of altermagnets. The two answer different questions: in MnO2 (Pnnm) the antiparallel pair along z has the type I group Pnnm and along x the type III group Pnn’m’ (standard setting; here it allows a net magnetization along y of the input cell), while both are d-wave altermagnets:

crystod-mag -c example/test_POSCARs/58_PPOSCAR_MnO2 --element Mn --qpoint GM --classify-only

GM4+(1) [FM, dipole]     y          Pnn'm' (58.398)  III   mm'm'  along y            ferromagnet
GM4+(1) [AFM, octupole]  x          Pnn'm' (58.398)  III   mm'm'  along y            altermagnet (d-wave)

Magnetoelectric Cr2O3 and hematite. On the four Cr sites of R-3c (example/37_magnetic_group/167_PPOSCAR_Cr2O3, rhombohedral primitive cell with the hexagonal c axis along z), the order + - + - along c (GM1- z) is the Cr2O3 order: R-3’c’ (167.106), MPG -3’m’, a conventional antiferromagnet (P T) with the linear magnetoelectric effect allowed by symmetry, alpha_11 = alpha_22 and alpha_33. The order + - - + (GM1+ z, the hematite order below the Morin transition) is the type I group R-3c (167.103), a g-wave altermagnet with piezomagnetism allowed. With the Python API, format_magnetic_responses prints the tensor forms:

from phonopy.interface.vasp import read_vasp
from crystod import mag

result = mag.analyze_spin_basis(read_vasp("167_PPOSCAR_Cr2O3"), "Cr", "GM")
cr2o3 = next(c for c in result.components if c.label.startswith("GM1-"))
print(cr2o3.group.bns_symbol, cr2o3.group.magnetic_point_group)
print("\n".join(mag.format_magnetic_responses(cr2o3.responses)))

The in-plane octupole shares the pair with the dipole, so it allows a net magnetization in the plane by symmetry although its own moments add up to zero, as the GM4+ octupoles of AlNi3 do. In Python, SpinBasisAnalysis.pair_labels lists the merged pairs.

q != 0. The classification refers to the real spin field of the magnetic cell, with the global phase fixed as in the VESTA export; the * Magnetic cell * block says so. The spin of site j in the cell at lattice translation T is Re(w_j exp(2 pi i q.T)), where w_j includes the Bloch factor exp(2 pi i q.x_j) of the site position (the lattice gauge, as in crystod-phonon --modulation), so every arrangement transforms like its irrep whatever the positions of the sites. At a time-reversal-invariant q != 0 a complex irrep and its conjugate are listed under their pair label, as at q = 0, also when they are two-dimensional. For Zn at A of ZnO (P6_3mc) the decomposition is A2A3(2) + A5A6(4), the list of bases reads A5A6(4): dim 4 [AFM (q != 0)], and the table has its four real partners as comp1 … comp4: the real parts of the two complex partners followed by their imaginary parts.

Limits. Only collinear arrangements are classified; a noncollinear component gets noncollinear (not classified) (its MSG and responses are still given). The table describes the components as crystod-mag exports them: for an irrep of dimension two or three each exported component is one arrangement, and another partner direction can have another MSG. For q != 0 the MSG depends on the global phase convention of the real field. The MPG symbol is derived from the BNS symbol and checked; the operations (MagneticGroupInfo.rotations, .translations, .time_reversals) are the authority. --classify-only needs --qpoint; the survey mode (no --qpoint) does not classify. At q = 0 the partners of a two- or three-dimensional irrep lie along directions that symmetry fixes; which of several equivalent choices is printed is a convention (in a trigonal or hexagonal crystal the a axis of the input cell and the direction perpendicular to it, whichever of the equivalent in-plane axes spglib calls a), and a rotated input cell gives the same groups and classes with the directions rotated. The partners of a merged complex pair are not fixed by symmetry and follow the convention described above.

from phonopy.interface.vasp import read_vasp
from crystod import mag

mag.magnetic_space_group(lattice, positions, numbers, magmoms), mag.macroscopic_responses(group) and mag.classify_collinear(...) work on any cell and spin field; their docstrings give the arguments. analyze_spin_basis takes --tolerance as symprec and raises ValueError, with the text of the ERROR lines of the command, for a cell that is not primitive and for a q that does not fit a magnetic cell of at most 12 cells along each axis.
