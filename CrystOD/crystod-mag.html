42. Symmetry-adapted spin bases#

Example directory: example/42_spin_basis (testsuite section 42); after pip install: crystod-example 42

Note

The example/... paths on this page are those of a clone of the repository, used from its root. After pip install CrystOD, run crystod-example NN (the number of the example directory) in your working directory first: it copies that directory and the shared test_POSCARs/ into ./example/, and the commands then run from there unchanged (see crystod-example).

Which magnetic orders can the moments on the sites of one element take in this crystal? crystod-mag enumerates every symmetry-distinct ferromagnetic and antiferromagnetic arrangement at a q point, sorted by irrep, and prints each with its per-atom spin directions, a ready-to-paste VASP or Quantum ESPRESSO magnetization input, a POSCAR of the magnetic cell and a VESTA file with spin arrows. The enumeration follows the cluster-multipole / symmetry-adapted multipole moment (SAMM) framework of M.-T. Suzuki et al. [PRB 95, 094406 (2017); PRB 99, 174407 (2019)].

See also

Theory: Spin bases as axial-vector SALCs — why the spins are treated as axial vectors, how the FM dipole is split from the higher multipoles and the multipole ranks are assigned, how complex and two-dimensional irreps get real partners, and which combined directions symmetry singles out.

How to read the output.

  • * Spin (axial-vector) representation *: the spin degrees of freedom of the magnetic sites (3 per site, 9 for the three Ni of AlNi3) and their irreps at q; 2 x GM4+(3) is the three-dimensional irrep GM4+ occurring twice.

  • * Symmetry-adapted spin bases *: one line per copy of an irrep. FM and AFM refer to the net moment; every AFM basis satisfies sum_i S_i = 0 exactly. The copies of an irrep are named after the ranks at which it occurs among the magnetic multipoles, lowest first: dipole (rank 1, the FM copy), quadrupole (rank 2), octupole (rank 3), hexadecapole (rank 4), …; an AFM basis is not always an octupole or higher (the in-plane orders of Cr2O3 are GM3-(2) [AFM, quadrupole], section 43).

  • For the Mn3Ir-type Ni 3c cluster of AlNi3, 9 dims = 2 x GM4+(3) + GM5+(3): the GM4+ (T1g) cluster dipole (FM), the GM4+ (T1g) cluster octupole (AFM: the experimentally realized 120-degree structure of Mn3Ir, which shares the irrep with the dipole and hence allows the anomalous Hall effect), and the GM5+ (T2g) cluster octupole (AFM).

  • component 111: one exported arrangement of the basis, tagged by its direction (the tags are listed in section 43); Ni1: S = [...] the spin of each magnetic site, net moment their sum.

  • magnetic space group … collinear order: the symmetry of the arrangement, the responses it allows and its class (section 43).

  • spin directions (all atoms, POSCAR order) and MAGMOM =: printed by default for every basis vector, the spins of all atoms of the magnetic cell, in the atom order of the magnetic-cell POSCAR written by the run.

  • Survey mode (no --qpoint): the spin (axial-vector) irrep decomposition is listed for every special k point of the space group — the magnetic counterpart of the crystod SALC survey (e.g. for AlNi3: GM: 2.0 [GM4+(3)] + 1.0 [GM5+(3)], R: R2+ + R3+ + R4+ + R5+, …). At a time-reversal-invariant point a complex irrep and its conjugate are counted once under their pair label, as with --qpoint (2.0 [GM3+GM4+(2)] for the Ti sites of P4/m Ti2Ga3). The main command prints the same terms in the format of its orbital SALC, with the little group of every k point: crystod -c POSCAR --element Ni --axial (see Spin configurations).

Combined directions of two- and three-dimensional bases

Combined directions of two- and three-dimensional bases. The components of a two- or three-dimensional basis are the (a,0) / (a,0,0) directions of its order parameter. The (a,a) / (a,a,0) direction, the equal-weight combination of two components, is exported as well wherever symmetry singles it out (it then has an isotropy subgroup of its own). A three-dimensional x/y/z basis also gets 111 (x + y + z), the (a,a,a) direction; another three-dimensional basis gets its first singled-out pair and the sum of the three when singled out. Output files

Output files.

  • POSCAR_<formula>_spin_<irrep>_<dipole|quadrupole|octupole|...>_<direction>.vesta, one per component (e.g. POSCAR_AlNi3_spin_GM4+_octupole_111.vesta for the 120-degree Mn3Ir-type state), with spin arrows on the magnetic atoms scaled so that the largest spin gets --amplitude Angstroms (default 1.5).

  • mag_salc_<formula>_<element>_<q>: the whole report of a --qpoint run, saved exactly as printed in the current directory, next to the VESTA files, so that > file is not needed: mag_salc_AlNi3_Ni_GM above, mag_salc_AlNi3_Ni_R for --qpoint R or --qpoint 0.5 0.5 0.5. The formula is the reduced formula of the primitive cell (as in the VESTA names), the element is --element, and the q point is named as in the line Selected q-point:, with GM for GAMMA; --conventional adds _conv, as it does to the VESTA names. A point off the special points is named by its line or plane (DT for --qpoint 0 0 1/4), so two points of one line share the name, as their VESTA files do. Warnings on the standard error stream are not in the file.

  • S<POSCAR name>_<q>: the magnetic cell of the MAGMOM lines as a VASP 5 POSCAR with direct coordinates, in the current directory. The name is S before the file name of -c without its directory, and the q part of the report name (GM for GAMMA, a character other than a letter, a digit, + or - replaced by _, _conv with --conventional): -c POSCAR_VO2_P42mnm --qpoint A (or --qpoint 0.5 0.5 0.5) writes SPOSCAR_VO2_P42mnm_A, -c POSCAR --qpoint GM writes SPOSCAR_GM, and -c ../139_PPOSCAR_La3Ni2O7 --qpoint 0 0 0.5 --conventional writes S139_PPOSCAR_La3Ni2O7_X_conv. Its cell and atom order are those of the spin directions listings, the MAGMOM lines and the VESTA files, so a MAGMOM line goes into the INCAR of a noncollinear run (LNONCOLLINEAR = .TRUE.) on this POSCAR as it is (it has 3 numbers per atom of the file). The species keep the blocks of the input (V O with 16 32 for the 2x2x2 cell of VO2 at A), so the POTCAR of the input cell fits. At q = 0 the cell is the input cell itself (the conventional cell with --conventional).

  • The line under * Output files * reads (atom order of the MAGMOM lines), or (atom order of the spin listings) with --format qe, whose input has no MAGMOM line (the Quantum ESPRESSO types then group atoms by direction, not by position in the file).

  • One report and one magnetic-cell POSCAR are written per run, and a file of the same name is overwritten; the survey mode, --classify-only and a run that stops with an ERROR write neither.

Magnetic supercell at q ≠ 0

Magnetic supercell at q ≠ 0.

  • For q != 0 (e.g. --qpoint R) the commensurate magnetic supercell is built automatically, with the Bloch phase applied to the spins and the MAGMOM/VESTA output referring to the supercell.

  • The atoms of the supercell are listed atom by atom, as in the SPOSCAR of phonopy: each atom of the primitive cell is followed by its images in the other cells of the magnetic cell (for AlNi3 at R, the 2x2x2 cell lists Al1 to Al8 and then Ni9 to Ni32), so the species blocks of the input stay grouped and the POTCAR of the input cell matches the SPOSCAR_* file. The same holds for the conventional cell of --conventional.

  • The supercell takes the denominator of each coordinate of q along the primitive axes, up to 12; a q that does not fit such a cell (0 0 0.15, that is 3/20) is refused with an ERROR that names the smallest commensurate supercell (1x1x20), instead of a cell that would cut the modulation.

Options

Options.

option

meaning

--element EL

the magnetic element whose sites carry the spins

--qpoint

the q point, as a name (GM, R, …) or three coordinates (0 0 0, 0.5 0.5 0.5, 0 0 1/4); without it, the survey of every special k point

--format

vasp (default) prints a noncollinear MAGMOM line; qe prints the Quantum ESPRESSO counterpart (above)

--amplitude

the arrow length of the largest spin in the VESTA files, in Angstrom (default 1.5)

--conventional

exports the spin structures in the conventional cell instead of the primitive cell (VESTA files get a _conv suffix, and for q != 0 the conventional cell is multiplied until the Bloch phase is commensurate, again with at most 12 cells per axis: NaCl at 1/5 1/7 0 is refused with --conventional, and the ERROR names the 5x7x1 primitive supercell that works without it)

--tolerance

the symmetry tolerance (default 1e-5, a distance in Angstrom), used by spglib and for matching the sites onto each other, both in the spin representation and in the collinear classification. The Bloch phases are built from the site positions symmetrized by the space group, so a structure slightly off its ideal positions decomposes and classifies like the ideal one (an AlNi3 cell with the Ni coordinates off by about 1e-4 gives 2 x GM4+(3) + GM5+(3) and the groups and classes of the ideal cell with --tolerance 1e-2; the listings keep the input positions)

--classify-only, --show-operations

the magnetic-group table alone; the operations kept and broken (section 43)
Requirements

Requirements. The input must be a primitive cell. It is used as given (the spin listings and MAGMOM follow its atom order), and a conventional cell or a supercell is refused with the same one-line ERROR in the survey and the --qpoint modes (the PPOSCAR of phonopy --symmetry is a primitive cell). For the two-atom cubic cell of bcc Fe:

ERROR: the input cell is not primitive (2 atoms, the primitive cell has 1); crystod-mag uses the cell as given (the spin listings and MAGMOM follow its atom order) and needs a primitive cell, such as the PPOSCAR of phonopy --symmetry (--conventional then exports the spin structures in the conventional cell).

43. Magnetic space groups and collinear classes (--classify-only)#

Example directory: example/43_magnetic_group (testsuite section 43); after pip install: crystod-example 43

Which of the magnetic orders that the symmetry of the crystal admits is an altermagnet, and which one allows a magnetoelectric effect? Every spin basis vector that crystod-mag --qpoint exports is also an arrangement of moments with its own symmetry: for each exported component the command gives the magnetic space group (MSG) of that arrangement, its magnetic point group (MPG), the macroscopic responses the MPG allows by symmetry (a net magnetization, the linear magnetoelectric effect, piezomagnetism) and, for a collinear arrangement, its class: ferromagnet, ferrimagnet, conventional antiferromagnet, or altermagnet with its d-, g- or i-wave type.

See also

Theory: Magnetic space groups and collinear classes of spin arrangements — the BNS types and symbols, the magnetic point group and the UNI symbol, the response tensors, the rules of the collinear classification and of the altermagnet wave type, the real spin field at q ≠ 0, and the order-parameter reading of the kept and broken operations.

How to read the output.

  • component: the tag of the listing and of the VESTA file name of the component: x, y, z, 110, 111, 1m10, aa, …, with _2, _3 when the same tag occurs again in the same basis (x_2 is the second x component and the file ..._x_2.vesta), and comp<N> for a component without a single direction: the N-th basis vector of its space, counted from 1 like the mode numbers of the other commands (POSCAR_AlNi3_spin_R4+_R_comp1.vesta is the first of the three R4+ components).

  • MSG (BNS), magnetic space group: the group of the arrangement as spglib finds it, with its Belov-Neronova-Smirnova (BNS) symbol and number (P4_2'/mnm', 136.499) and its type: I, no operation with time reversal; II, the grey group of a non-magnetic crystal; III, half of the operations combined with time reversal (primed); IV, a lattice translation combined with time reversal (the subscript of the lattice letter, P_I, C_c, R_I). The operations are counted in the magnetic cell.

  • MPG, magnetic point group: derived from the BNS symbol and checked against the operations (a symbol that disagrees is printed with ?).

  • MSG (UNI), UNI symbol: the unified symbol of Campbell et al., Acta Cryst. A78, 99 (2022): the BNS symbol with .1 (type I) or .1' (type II), unchanged for type III (P4_2'/mnm'); a type IV group reads .1'_t[F], t the anti-translation and F the family space group (P_I4/mnc is P4/mnc.1'_I[I4/mmm]).

  • net magnetization, allowed by symmetry: the direction of a net magnetization the MPG allows, in the Cartesian axes of the input cell (along z, in the xy plane, any direction, none), and the numbers of independent components of the linear magnetoelectric tensor and of the piezomagnetic tensor. Symmetry decides what is allowed, not how large it is: an allowed response can be small. An AFM basis vector that shares its irrep with the FM dipole (the GM4+ octupoles of AlNi3) allows a net magnetization by symmetry although its own moments add up to zero.

  • class, collinear order, from the signs of the moments along their common axis only (nonrelativistic): ferromagnet and ferrimagnet (nonzero net moment), compensated, sublattices not related by symmetry, antiferromagnet (T tau) (a pure translation exchanges the sublattices) and antiferromagnet (P T) (the inversion does; the bands stay spin degenerate in the nonrelativistic limit), altermagnet (d-wave) / (g-wave) / (i-wave) (only rotations or mirrors exchange the sublattices), and noncollinear (not classified). For an altermagnet the listing adds the lowest-order spin splitting allowed by symmetry (~ k_x k_y for rutile) and the spin-degenerate planes, in the Cartesian axes of the input cell.

More on reading the output

  • Two frames meet in one row: the MSG and MPG symbols are those of the standard BNS setting, whatever the orientation of the input cell, while the net magnetization, the spin-splitting polynomials and the degenerate planes are given in the Cartesian axes of the input cell. A row Pnn'm' ... mm'm' ... along y is therefore consistent: the standard setting of Pnn’m’ allows a moment along its own x axis, which is y of the input cell.

  • magnetic space group: not identified by spglib (N operations, M with time reversal): the MSG, type and MPG cells of the table hold -, and the responses and the class are still computed from the operations.

  • At q = 0 every AFM basis vector has a zero net moment, and the closing sentence All AFM bases satisfy sum_i S_i = 0 says so. A note under the table names every merged pair of a complex irrep and its conjugate (GM3+GM4+(2), the label that crystod-group --parent prints for the same pair); each copy of such a pair has two real partners.

At a time-reversal-invariant q != 0 a complex irrep and its conjugate are listed under their pair label, as at q = 0, also when they are two-dimensional. For Zn at A of ZnO (P6_3mc) the decomposition is A2A3(2) + A5A6(4), the list of bases reads A5A6(4): dim 4 [AFM (q != 0)], and the table has its four real partners as comp1 … comp4: the real parts of the two complex partners followed by their imaginary parts.

Limits

Limits.

  • Only collinear arrangements are classified; a noncollinear component gets noncollinear (not classified) (its MSG and responses are still given).

  • The table describes the components as crystod-mag exports them: for an irrep of dimension two or three each exported component is one arrangement, and another partner direction can have another MSG.

  • For q != 0 the MSG depends on the global phase convention of the real field.

  • The MPG symbol is derived from the BNS symbol and checked; the operations (MagneticGroupInfo.rotations, .translations, .time_reversals) are the authority.

  • --classify-only needs --qpoint; the survey mode (no --qpoint) does not classify.

  • At q = 0 the partners of a two- or three-dimensional irrep lie along directions that symmetry fixes; which of several equivalent choices is printed is a convention (in a trigonal or hexagonal crystal the a axis of the input cell and the direction perpendicular to it, whichever of the equivalent in-plane axes spglib calls a), and a rotated input cell gives the same groups and classes with the directions rotated. The partners of a merged complex pair are not fixed by symmetry and follow a convention (Real partners of merged complex pairs and repeated irreps).

Operations kept and broken (--show-operations)#

  • {R|t}' is theta{R|t}, the operation followed by time reversal (the prime of the BNS symbols).
