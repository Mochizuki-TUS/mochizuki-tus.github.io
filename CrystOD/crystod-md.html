crystod-md#

MD-trajectory analyses. Three mode flags: --adp (atomic displacement parameters as CIF), --summary (time-averaged lattice statistics) and --project (projection onto the symmetry-adapted modes of the parent cell, or with --yaml onto its phonon eigenvectors).

I want to …

command

get the ADPs of an MD run as a CIF file

crystod-md --adp --dim 4 4 4 --start-step 1000

get the time-averaged cell of an NpT run

crystod-md --summary --start-step 1000

follow the R4+ / M3+ amplitudes of an MD run and see whether they are single- or double-peaked

crystod-md --project -c 221_PPOSCAR_ScF3 --dim 4 4 4 --start-step 1000 --qpoint R M

check that the projected modes carry all of the displacement (sum rule)

crystod-md --project -c 221_PPOSCAR_ScF3 --dim 4 4 4 --qpoint all --no-histogram

compare the fluctuation of every phonon mode with the harmonic model (effective frequencies)

crystod-md --project --yaml phonopy_params.yaml --dim 4 4 4 --start-step 1000 --qpoint R M GM --temperature 300

project every arm of a star (the three M arms of a cubic parent)

crystod-md --project -c 221_PPOSCAR_ScF3 --dim 4 4 4 --qpoint M --star

39. ADPs from an MD trajectory (--adp)#

Example directory: example/39_xdatcar2adp (testsuite section 39); after pip install: crystod-example 39

Note

The example/... paths on this page are those of a clone of the repository, used from its root. After pip install CrystOD, run crystod-example NN (the number of the example directory) in your working directory first: it copies that directory and the shared test_POSCARs/ into ./example/, and the commands then run from there unchanged (see crystod-example).

Compute the time-averaged structure and anisotropic displacement parameters (ADPs, U_ij) from a molecular-dynamics XDATCAR trajectory and write them as a CIF file. The example is an NpT run of a 4x4x4 supercell of ScF3 at 300 K; XDATCAR_every10 holds every 10th frame of its equilibrated part (configurations 2000 to 5000: 301 frames, 3.1 MB), so no frame is discarded (--start-step 0):

cd example/39_xdatcar2adp/ScF3_Pm-3m_NpT_300K
crystod-md --adp --dim 4 4 4 --start-step 0 --xdatcar XDATCAR_every10 --output ADP.cif

The full 41.7 MB XDATCAR of the run (4001 frames; not in the pip package) with --start-step 1000 analyses all 3001 frames of the same part and gives the same to 0.2 % (Ueq 0.005485 and 0.023521 A^2).

The written CIF ends with the symmetry-constrained _atom_site_aniso_U_* loop, ready for thermal-ellipsoid display in VESTA — for NpT ScF3 at 300 K the F ellipsoids are strongly anisotropic (U11 = U33 >> U22, the pancake shape perpendicular to the Sc-F-Sc bond that drives the negative thermal expansion), while Sc stays isotropic by symmetry:

--dim is the MD supercell size relative to the unit cell, given as three diagonal values or a nine-value diagonal matrix, quoted or unquoted (--dim 4 4 4, --dim="4 4 4", --dim 4 0 0 0 4 0 0 0 4; non-diagonal matrices are rejected). --start-step discards the first N MD steps as equilibration (default 0). --xdatcar defaults to XDATCAR and --output to ADP.cif. --format selects the trajectory format (currently vasp only; LAMMPS support is planned).

The workflow: the supercell trajectory is folded and grouped into unit-cell sites (--grouping-tolerance, default 0.1, sets how far an atom may sit from its site and still be grouped with it), the space group of the time-averaged unit cell is detected with spglib (--tolerance, also default 0.1, a Cartesian distance in Angstrom; the same distance selects the site-symmetry operations of each site and maps every site onto its asymmetric-unit representative), the per-atom displacement covariances are rotated into the asymmetric-unit representatives and averaged over all symmetry-equivalent atoms and MD steps, and site-symmetry constraints (e.g. U11=U22=U33, U12=U13=U23=0 on a cubic site) are enforced by projection. Atoms that cannot be mapped onto their representative within --tolerance are left out of the statistics and named, with their 1-based XDATCAR atom numbers, in a WARNING: N atom(s) could not be mapped ... line of the * Trajectory unwrapping * block. The projection acts on the CIF components U^ij through M = N*^-1 R N* with N* = diag(|a*_1|, |a*_2|, |a*_3|), so an isotropic tensor stays isotropic in every cell, the primitive cells of body-centred tetragonal and orthorhombic lattices included (an atom on the 4/mmm site of a bct primitive cell gets U11=U22, U13=U23, an isotropic tensor there having nonzero U23, U13 and U12); conventional cells and F-, C-centred, rhombohedral and bcc primitive cells have M = R. The constraint text lists only relations the projector imposes (Uij=0, Uij=Ukl, Uij=-Ukl); a site constrained only by relations such as U12 = U22/2 (a two-fold axis along a of a hexagonal cell) reads general linear constraints only, and one without constraints no constraint. The printed Ueq is that of the tensor written to the CIF (one third of the trace of its Cartesian form), so the table and the CIF always agree. The CIF opens with the comment line # generated using crystod-md --adp and contains the cell, the symmetry operations, the asymmetric-unit sites, and the _atom_site_aniso_U_* loop, ready for thermal-ellipsoid display in VESTA. Both fixed-cell and variable-cell (NpT, repeated-header) XDATCAR files are supported.

In Python, md.get_site_symmetry_operations(coords, rotations, translations, symprec, lattice=L) and md.build_symmetry_projector(rotations, lattice=L) take the lattice (rows = lattice vectors, Angstrom) for the Angstrom tolerance and the matrices M; without lattice= they keep the fractional tolerance and use R itself, which is exact only for the cells with M = R above.

Based on script/xdatcar_to_adp.py by Ko Sato, with a fast built-in parser in place of the pymatgen reader. For the ScF3 example above the two write the same CIF below the comment line (the script’s reads # generated using xdatcar_to_adp.py); the matrices M, the constraint text, the tolerance in Angstrom, the WARNING and the Ueq of the written tensor are CrystOD’s (the script prints 0.005476 and 0.023485 in the Ueq column).

39.1 Trajectory summary (--summary)#

Example directory: example/39_xdatcar2adp (testsuite sections 39 and 41: --summary runs in the CLI-regression section); after pip install: crystod-example 39

crystod-md --summary reports summary statistics of the same trajectory: the time-averaged lattice parameters (a, b, c, alpha, beta, gamma) and cell volume with standard deviations over the selected step range — useful for extracting the equilibrium cell from an NpT run:

crystod-md --summary --start-step 0 --xdatcar XDATCAR_every10
crystod-md --summary --xdatcar XDATCAR_every10 --start-step 100 --end-step 200   # inclusive step range

40. Mode projection of an MD trajectory (--project)#

Example directory: example/40_md_projection (testsuite section 40); after pip install: crystod-example 40

--project answers how far an MD run moves along each symmetry-adapted displacement pattern of the parent structure, and with what statistics: the order-parameter time series Q(t) of every irrep, mode space and component at the chosen q points. Its typical use is a crystal near a structural instability, such as the octahedral tilts R4+ and M3+ of a perovskite: the width of the distribution of Q tells how large the fluctuations are, and its shape whether they are displacive (one well, a Gaussian about Q = 0) or order-disorder (two populated states +Q0 and -Q0). Every frame is matched to the ideal supercell of the parent primitive cell (-c, --dim), the displacements are projected onto the bases that crystod-phonon --vibration lists (same ISO-IR labels, mode-space and component numbers for a parent in the standard setting), and the statistics of every component are tabulated. The run is the ScF3 trajectory of section 39 (crystod-example 40 copies 39_xdatcar2adp and 33_modulation along):

cd example/40_md_projection
crystod-md --project -c ../test_POSCARs/221_PPOSCAR_ScF3 --dim 4 4 4 \
    --xdatcar ../39_xdatcar2adp/ScF3_Pm-3m_NpT_300K/XDATCAR_every10 --start-step 0 \
    --qpoint R M -o MDPROJ_ScF3_300K

The amplitude Q. Each row of a mode space is expanded on the supercell (SymmetryOnlyVibrations.get_supercell_displacements, the Bloch factor of every atom included) into a real pattern p, normalized over the whole supercell, and

Q(t) = p_hat . u(t) / sqrt(N),     p_hat = p / |p|,   N = n1 n2 n3,

with u(t) the Cartesian displacements of all atoms (after the drift removal below). Q is the displacement norm per parent primitive cell carried by the pattern. At a time-reversal invariant q (2q a reciprocal lattice vector: GM, R, M, X of Pm-3m) it is the amplitude of crystod-phonon --modulation --amplitude and --vibration. The R4+ structure that --modulation --amplitude 0.3 writes on 2x2x2 (example/33_modulation/ScF3_Pm-3m/POSCAR_I4mcm_3), read as a one-frame trajectory, gives

The AMPLIMODES amplitude of crystod-group --supergroup-cif is normalized in the primitive cell of the distorted structure instead: for an irrep it is sqrt(size) times the norm of its Q over all components and all arms of the star, with size the size of that cell in parent cells, so Q sqrt(size) when a single component is nonzero. The same structure gives R4+ ... 140 I4/mcm 1 0.4243, 0.3 sqrt(2); a structure with several components or arms of one irrep, such as the M3+ of Im-3 frozen on three arms with 0.3 each (size 4), gives sqrt(4 x 3 x 0.3^2) = 1.0392. At a q that is not time-reversal invariant the rows are complex Bloch waves; each row e gives the two real patterns of e and of i e (components 1c, 1s), and since q and -q span the same real patterns, only one of the pair is projected (--qpoint all keeps the first of each pair; a -q given explicitly is reported as skipped). A modulation frozen there with --modulation --amplitude A gives A/sqrt(2) as the norm of Q over its c and s components (the rms per-cell displacement norm of the frozen wave); for instance a phonon mode at (1/4, 1/4, 1/4) frozen with --amplitude 0.3 on 4x4x4 ScF3 gives sum Q^2 = 0.045 = 0.3^2/2.

How to read the table.

  • <Q>, sqrt<Q^2>, <|Q|>: the mean, the root mean square and the mean absolute value of Q(t), in Angstrom. In a structure with the parent symmetry on average, <Q> of every non-identity irrep is zero to within the sampling; a nonzero <Q> is a static distortion (or a sampling artefact of a slow mode).

  • ratio = <|Q|>/sqrt<Q^2>: sqrt(2/pi) = 0.798 for a Gaussian about Q = 0 (a displacive fluctuation in one well), 1 for two equally populated states +Q0 and -Q0 (order-disorder), 0.900 for a sinusoid.

  • kurtosis: the excess kurtosis about the mean, 0 for a Gaussian, -2 for two sharp states, -1.5 for a sinusoid, positive for heavy tails.

  • n_eff: the number of frames divided by the integrated autocorrelation time of Q(t) (summed to the first zero of the autocorrelation function), the number of effectively independent samples. The ratio and the kurtosis scatter by roughly 1/sqrt(n_eff); with n_eff below about 50 a single component says little about the shape. For a weakly damped oscillation the autocorrelation function crosses zero within a quarter period while the amplitude and phase stay correlated much longer, so n_eff is then an upper bound.

  • shape: the histogram density at Q = 0 against its maximum (odd number of bins, symmetric about 0): below 0.5 with at least 10 % of the samples on each side, double-peaked (hint); below 0.5 with the weight on one side, off-center; else single-peaked; zero for a component that does not move and n/a for fewer than 20 frames. It is a hint, not a test: a coherent oscillation of large amplitude (a sinusoid, as in the synthetic check) also reads as double-peaked, because it spends most of its time near its turning points.

  • * Pooled components and norm |Q| *: for a mode space with several components. In the parent symmetry the components of one mode space are equivalent and share one distribution, so pooling their samples (columns sqrt<Q^2> to shape) improves the statistics; <|Q|> is the mean norm of the component vector, and |Q| ratio = <|Q|>/sqrt<|Q|^2> is compared with its value for an isotropic Gaussian in that many components (0.886 for two, 0.921 for three). Pooling is meaningful only when the components are equivalent in the run: when their rms values differ by more than a factor 3 (or one is zero), as for a frozen or ordered distortion along one component or for a weakly damped mode sampled in few phases, the pooled ratio, kurtosis and n_eff are not printed and the shape reads unequal.

For ScF3 at 300 K every irrep is single-peaked about Q = 0, as expected for the cubic phase. The tilts carry by far the largest amplitudes (sqrt<Q^2> about 0.1 A per cell for R4+, 0.04-0.08 A for M3+ on its three arms), but they are also the slowest modes: n_eff is 16-19 for each R4+ component and 22-29 for M3+, against about 80-300 for the single components of the other irreps (the stiff modes reach 301, the number of frames: ten configurations apart, their frames are nearly uncorrelated). The ratios of the single R4+ components therefore scatter (0.71, 0.86, 0.74), while the pooled R4+ ratio is 0.768 (n_eff 55) and M3+ gives 0.773, near the Gaussian 0.798; no component is double-peaked, so the fluctuations are displacive. The differences between the three R4+ components are sampling, not symmetry breaking: averaged over the 48 cubic images of the same frames, the widths of the components of every mode space become equal. The full trajectory (not in the pip package; --start-step 1000) gives the same shapes, sqrt<Q^2> within 0.0002 A and ratios within 0.015.

sum over all q, irreps and components of <Q^2>  =  <|u|^2> / N,

Options and files.

  • --qpoint: labels (R M X GM, the names crystod-phonon --vibration --list-qpoints prints), coordinates (--qpoint 1/2 1/2 0, any number of points in one list) or all (every q commensurate with --dim). The default is every tabulated special point commensurate with --dim, one arm each (GM, R, M, X for Pm-3m with an even --dim); the other arms of M and X are reached with coordinates, all or --star. A q that is not commensurate with --dim is refused.

  • --star: replace every requested q (labels, coordinates, or the default special points) by all arms of its star (crystod --star-of-k), each with its own block and file (--qpoint M --star gives the three M arms of Pm-3m, MDPROJ_ScF3_M_0.5_0.5_0.csv and so on); of an arm and its -q partner only the one listed first is projected, as with all.

  • --yaml, --temperature: the phonon-eigenvector basis and the effective frequencies, see below.

  • --irrep R4+ M3+: keep only these irreps (the dimension suffix is optional); the sum rule then covers that subset.

  • --start-step / --end-step: the inclusive range of frames, counted from 0 as in --adp and --summary; the CSV files carry the configuration= numbers of the XDATCAR.

  • --mean-lattice: convert the fractional displacements of every frame with the time-averaged cell instead of the frame’s own cell (no difference for a fixed-cell run).

  • -o PREFIX (default MDPROJ_<formula>): PREFIX_<q>.csv per q (step, then one column per component named <label>_<space>_<component>, e.g. R4+(3)_3_1; when several q share a name, the coordinates are added, MDPROJ_ScF3_M_0.5_0_0.5.csv), PREFIX_hist.pdf (twelve panels per page; --no-histogram skips it), and with --npz PREFIX.npz (steps, qpoints, qpoint_names, Q_<q> and labels_<q> per q).

  • --tolerance: the symmetry tolerance of the parent cell (default 1e-5).

Limits. --dim must be diagonal and the MD cell must be --dim times the parent cell in the same orientation (each lattice vector within 10 % of the parent’s; the patterns are Cartesian in the axes of the parent file). The parent must be the primitive cell, with the origin and setting whose ISO-IR labels you want (the frame rules of the labels apply: the octahedral tilts of SrTiO3 are R4+ with Ti at the origin and R5- with Sr). An atom farther than 0.25 of the shortest parent lattice vector from every ideal site of its element, two atoms on one site, or a composition other than --dim times the parent’s stop the run with an ERROR: line. Only VASP XDATCAR files are read (--format vasp; fixed-cell and NpT). Without --yaml the basis is the symmetry-adapted displacement patterns, not normal modes: without force constants, a mode space of a repeated irrep (the three GM4- spaces of ScF3) is one fixed orthonormal choice, and no mass weighting enters the patterns. The drift removal subtracts the mass-weighted centre-of-mass displacement of every frame (the quantity MD codes conserve; its rms is printed), so polar optical motion, in which atoms of unequal mass move against each other, is kept: a GM4- polar mode frozen with --modulation --amplitude 0.3 projects to a norm of 0.300 over the GM4- components. The ISO-IR names, mode-space and component numbers are those of crystod-phonon --vibration when the parent file is in the standard setting; --project works in the frame of the -c file (as the trajectory does), while --vibration converts the cell to the standard setting, so for a parent in another setting (for example rutile with its axes permuted) the two can name and number q points and mode spaces differently.

Phonon-eigenvector basis (--yaml)#

With force constants the trajectory can be projected onto the phonon modes themselves instead of onto symmetry-adapted patterns: --yaml phonopy_params.yaml makes the basis at every q the eigenvectors of the dynamical matrix, symmetry-adapted and numbered as crystod-phonon --modulation and --vector number them (1-based, sorted by the harmonic frequency, the partners of a degenerate level as --modulation freezes them; the force constants are used without the non-analytical term correction). Normal coordinates. At q, mode nu has the unit eigenvector e_nu (3n complex components, mass-weighted, in phonopy’s atom-position phase convention) and the harmonic frequency nu_harm (THz; an imaginary frequency is printed as a negative number, as phonopy does). Its real pattern on the supercell is

p_nu(j, R) = Re[ e_nu,j exp(2 pi i q.(R + x_j)) ]

(and that of i e_nu, components c and s, at a q that is not time-reversal invariant), built by the same code as the symmetry-only patterns. The trajectory is taken in mass-weighted coordinates, w_j(R) = sqrt(m_j) u_j(R) with the masses of the yaml and the mass-weighted centre of mass of every frame removed, and

a_nu(t) = p_hat_nu . w(t)      (A sqrt(amu), over the supercell)
A_nu(t) = a_nu(t) / sqrt(N)    (per parent cell)

with p_hat the pattern normalized over the supercell. The patterns of all modes at all commensurate q form an orthonormal basis of the mass-weighted displacements, so

sum over all q and modes of <a_nu^2>  =  < sum_jR m_j |u_j(R)|^2 >,

which the * Sum rule * block prints in amu A^2; with --qpoint all the two agree to rounding (302.308248 amu A^2 for this run, relative difference 1.9e-16). The columns of the CSV files are named <mode>_<irrep> (1_R4+, 4c_DT5), the npz arrays a_<q> with frequencies_<q>, and the histogram file has one panel per degenerate level. The default prefix is MDPROJ_<formula>_phonon.

Relation to --modulation. --modulation --amplitude A0 freezes u_j(R) = A0 Re[v_j exp(2 pi i q.R)] with v_j = e_j exp(2 pi i q.x_j)/sqrt(m_j) renormalized over the primitive cell, so at a time-reversal invariant q the mode carries A = A0 |sqrt(m) v| per cell, the mass-weighted norm of the renormalized pattern. At a q that is not time-reversal invariant the norm of A over the c and s columns of the mode is A0 |sqrt(m) v| / sqrt(2), as for Q in the symmetry-only basis. For the R4+ tilt of ScF3 only F moves, |sqrt(m) v| = sqrt(m_F) = 4.358716 sqrt(amu), and the one-frame trajectory of an R4+ structure frozen with --amplitude 0.3 gives A = 1.307615 on that mode and zero on every other mode of the cell (the symmetry-only projection of the same frame gives Q = 0.30000). Where an irrep occurs once at q, as R4+ and M3+ here, the eigenvector is the symmetry-adapted pattern itself and A = sqrt(m) Q for a single-species pattern; where it occurs several times (GM4- three times, M3- twice), the eigenvectors are the combinations the force constants select.

Effective frequency. For a harmonic mode in equilibrium classical MD, equipartition gives for the normal coordinate of the supercell

<a_nu^2> = k_B T / omega_nu^2,

so --temperature T (kelvin) prints

nu_eff = (1 / 2 pi) sqrt( k_B T / <a_nu^2> )

next to nu_harm, with k_B = 0.8314462618 amu A^2 ps^-2 K^-1, omega in rad/ps and nu in THz; the pooled block gives it for the mean <a^2> of each degenerate level. nu_eff is the frequency of the harmonic oscillator that would have the same mean-square normal coordinate at T, a quasi-harmonic, temperature-renormalized frequency. It assumes a classical run (no zero-point motion) in equilibrium, sampled long enough (n_eff: <a^2> has a relative scatter of about sqrt(2/n_eff)), and it is only that mode’s frequency when the harmonic eigenvector is also a normal mode of the run: the coordinate of a mode can mix in the run with another mode of the same irrep at the same q, and then shows a frequency between the two. A soft or imaginary harmonic mode with a finite nu_eff is the anharmonic stabilization of that mode at T; nu_eff below nu_harm means the run fluctuates more along the mode than the harmonic model predicts. Only commensurate q are reached, so the zone boundary appears only through the commensurate set of --dim, and the acoustic modes at GM carry nothing after the centre-of-mass removal.
