crystod (main command)#

The main command performs the SALC (symmetry-adapted linear combination) analysis of crystal orbitals — no mode flag is needed. It also hosts the crystal-orbital diagram (--diagram, with the band structure and DOS of the same calculation), the interactive SALC viewer (--visualize), the star-of-k display (--star-of-k), the band irreps of a finished VASP run (--extract-irrep), the unfolding of the bands of a low-symmetry VASP run onto the Brillouin zone of its high-symmetry parent run (--unfold) and the check of the response tensors of a VASP run against the symmetry of its structure (--check-symmetry).

When --kpoint is omitted, all special k points of the space group are analyzed. Any arm of a star and any copy k + G of a k point get the same labels; on a symmetry line the label is evaluated at the canonical value of the line parameter, and a k point whose star ISO-IR tabulates only through -k is labelled with the complex conjugates of the tabulated irreps under the A suffix (PA of I-4, labelled PA1, …, the conjugates of P1, …); on a line that no symmetry element reverses, the star at the negative parameter is named in the same way after ISOTROPY (LE for LD, DU for DT). Names and labels refer to one frame: the special points are listed, and a name given to --kpoint is resolved, in the frame of the labels, and given coordinates are named by the same labeller, so a printed name has the letters of the labels below it, also for an input outside the ISO-IR setting (--visualize --kpoint and --star-of-k take seekpath names, whose triclinic letters are not the ISO-IR ones; see Irrep labels and the frame of a structure). The labels refer to one frame per structure: an input in the ISO-IR setting keeps its own axes and origin (with Sr or Ti at the origin of SrTiO3 the octahedral tilt is R5- or R4+), and so does a diagonal n1 x n2 x n3 supercell of it of any size, which is labelled like the cell; any other input gets a canonical frame of the crystal, which keeps the input’s own origin when that is the origin of a standard description. Shifted copies of a crystal are therefore labelled alike whatever their orientation, basis or cell size, but a non-diagonal supercell (sqrt2 x sqrt2 x 1) can get other names than the cell, and an unshifted re-based cell other names than a shifted copy of it — see Irrep labels and the frame of a structure. --show-irrep-table additionally prints the little-group character table at the selected k point (with --kpoint; the survey over all special points prints no table). It also writes that table to irrep_table.xlsx in the working directory, without a message and overwriting an existing file; with --atomic-orbital and no --kpoint it prints one table per special k point and the file keeps the last one. The export goes through pandas and needs openpyxl (or XlsxWriter), which is not a dependency of CrystOD: without either the run stops after the table header with ModuleNotFoundError: No module named 'openpyxl' (pip install openpyxl).

The atoms of an element need not be contiguous in the POSCAR. A file whose species blocks repeat, such as SrTiO3 written as O Ti O Sr O / 1 1 1 1 1, gives the same output as the same crystal written as Sr Ti O / 1 1 3: the analysis takes exactly the atoms of the element, wherever they stand in the file. The survey --element O --orbital p prints O (3) under * Element (number of atoms) *, the Wyckoff letters ['c', 'c', 'c'] and, at X, the 9-dimensional O p decomposition 1.0 [X1+(1)] + 1.0 [X2+(1)] + 1.0 [X3-(1)] + 2.0 [X5+(2)] + 1.0 [X5-(2)]; with --kpoint X the species line reads O_p (3) under * Orbital (number of atoms) *, as for the contiguous file. The same holds for --atomic-orbital and --visualize, where the atom labels of the coefficient lines follow the order of the file.

Spin configurations (--axial)#

--axial takes the place of --orbital: every site of the element carries one axial vector (Rx, Ry, Rz), a magnetic moment, and the decomposition is that of the magnetic representation, i.e. the irreps of the spin configurations of those sites. The rotation character of the axial vector is that of the p shell times det(R) (the same on a proper rotation, no sign change on an improper one: inversion keeps a spin), so on an inversion centre the irreps are those of --orbital p with the parity flipped. GM4+ is the ferromagnet, R4+ the G-type antiferromagnet with the moments in any direction; at X the moments along the wave vector (X3+) separate from the two directions perpendicular to it (X5+). The bundled example/test_POSCARs/221_PPOSCAR_SrTiO3 has Sr on the origin and Ti on 1b, and gives the labels of that frame: GM4+, R5-, X1- + X5-, M2+ + M5+.

The labels are the ordinary ISO-IR irreps of the little group, without the m prefix ISOTROPY gives the time-odd irreps (GM4+ here is mGM4+ there), as in crystod-mag. Because a spin configuration is real, at a time-reversal-invariant k point a complex irrep and its conjugate are printed once under the pair label with the real dimension, 2.0 [GM3+GM4+(2)] for the Ti sites of P4/m Ti2Ga3, where --orbital lists GM3+(1) and GM4+(1) separately. These are exactly the terms of the survey of crystod-mag -c POSCAR --element EL. crystod-mag --qpoint K then gives the symmetry-adapted spin bases themselves, with their magnetic space groups and MAGMOM lines. --kpoint (a label or coordinates) and --show-irrep-table work as for --orbital; with --kpoint the header reads * Element (number of atoms) * and * Basis *. --axial does not take --spinor, since a magnetic moment is single-valued, and it is refused with --atomic-orbital, --visualize, --diagram and the other modes. In Python the same decomposition is CrystalOrbital(cell).irreducible_decomposition(k, "Ti", "axial"), which returns the unmerged irreps.

Testsuite section 2 compares the decomposition, at every special point and for every element, with the explicit spin-representation matrices of crystod-mag on one structure per space group: 23 groups by default, all 230 (4050 cases) with CRYSTOD_AXIAL_SWEEP=1.

The names. The bundled ISO-IR tables hold single-valued irreps only, so CrystOD names the double-valued irreps itself, on the ISO-IR labels of the same k point in the same frame, with the numbering of Bradley & Cracknell (1972), Table 6.5 (Koster’s Γ numbers), apart from two exceptions given below. As in Bradley & Cracknell the index continues after the single-valued irreps and parity partners share it (Γn±); the overbar is written as a leading - (as in the Bilbao tables). One rule serves --spinor, --atomic-orbital --spinor, --extract-irrep and the WAVECAR-overlap engine (crystod.wavecar_irreps.double_valued_names in Python), so the four name the irreps of one cell alike:

  • Fingerprint. A double-valued irrep D is identified by the decomposition of D x D(1/2) into the single-valued ISO-IR irreps at k.

  • Name. -K<n><p>: the overbar of the double-group label written as a leading - (R̄6+ is -R6+), the ISO-IR k label (GM, R, DT, …), an index n, and the parity suffix +/- when the single-valued irreps at k carry one (the parity is read off the fingerprint). Parity partners share n.

  • Index. n continues after the largest single-valued index at k. The classes of irreps (a parity pair is one class) are numbered in the order of their dimension, then of their fingerprint (lexicographic in the single-valued labels).

  • Ties. Two one-dimensional irreps that are complex conjugates of each other can have the same fingerprint: the pair of the little co-groups 2, m and 2/m, the one-dimensional pair of 32, 3m and -3m, and their analogues at other k points. They are ordered by their characters at the first operation (in a canonical order of the operations) where the two differ, +i before -i, with every two-fold rotation lifted to SU(2) as the rotation by +pi about its axis [uvw] in the ISO-IR frame, the first nonzero component positive, except that in 2, m and 2/m the unique axis is lifted as +pi about -[uvw]: that is Bradley & Cracknell’s C2z = (C4z-)^2, so -GM3 is their (Koster’s) Γ3, the irrep with chi(C2z) = +i in their lift. At Γ of P2/m, -GM3+ and -GM3- have chi(2) = -i in the lift about +[uvw] (+i in B&C’s), -GM4+ and -GM4- have +i. The C2’ axes of 32, 3m and -3m keep the lift about +[uvw], which makes -GM4 B&C’s Γ5 (1E).

  • Arms of a star. The names are fixed at the representative arm k0 of the star of k, the arm the ISO-IR table lists first (at the parameters of k on a line or plane), and carried to every other arm and to every copy k + G by conjugation, D’(h) = D(g^-1 h g) with g k0 = k; at a k point named through -k (the ‘A’ partners, LE of LD) the irreps are the complex conjugates of those at k0, under the same index. An irrep therefore has one name on the whole star, as its single-valued labels have (crystod.isoir.IsoIRLabeler.representative_k, crystod.wavecar_irreps.transport_double_valued_names).

  • Lift. Otherwise the SU(2) matrices U_g are spgrep’s (rotation angle in [0, pi], U(-R) = U(R): inversion does not act on the spin), and the spin factor of the orbital characters is tr U_g. --show-irrep-table prints the double-valued character table in this lift, with the names.

At Γ the names agree with Table 6.5 for 26 of the 32 point groups. They differ in two families:

  • 32, 3m, -3m. The classes are ordered by dimension (the order of Miller & Love, 1967, which is also that of the CDML and Bilbao tables and of the usual labels at L of the fcc lattice, -L4, -L5 one-dimensional, -L6 two-dimensional), so the two-dimensional E1/2 is the last, -GM6, where Table 6.5 has it first, Γ4; the one-dimensional pair -GM4, -GM5 is B&C’s Γ5, Γ6.

  • 4, -4, 4/m. -GM7 and -GM8 are Table 6.5’s Γ8 and Γ7. In the cyclic co-groups 3, -3, 4, -4, 4/m, 6, -6 and 6/m the complex pairs are not ties: their order follows the single-valued ISO-IR labels through the fingerprints. Bradley & Cracknell number these pairs inconsistently themselves: for the group 4, Table 6.5 has 1E1 = Γ6 and 2E1 = Γ5, Table 6.14 (G8^1 a) 1E1 = Γ5 and 2E1 = Γ8; the Γ indices of Table 6.5 and of Tables 6.13 and 6.14 disagree at Γ of 3, 4, -4, 6, -6 and 6/m, and 4/m read through Tables 6.13 and 6.14 agrees with CrystOD. CrystOD keeps the order of the ISO-IR labels there.

This line appears where the proper parts of the little co-group form the point group 32 (32, 3m and -3m: Γ and T of R-3m, L of Fm-3m and Fd-3m) and the irreps are, per parity, two one-dimensional and one two-dimensional. Where they form a cyclic group of order 3, 4 or 6 and the irreps at k hold a complex-conjugate pair (Γ of P3 or P4, for example), the line is NOTE: the complex-conjugate pairs are ordered after the single-valued ISO-IR labels; Bradley & Cracknell (1972) Tables 6.5 and 6.14 number them inconsistently. The three-line remark under * Atomic Orbital * (survey) or * Spinor * (--kpoint, --atomic-orbital) states the convention once per run.

For all 32 crystallographic point groups at Γ the names pass the checks of group theory: the squared dimensions add up to the order of the group, the names are unique, the indices start after the single-valued ones, every + irrep has its - partner, and the names do not change under a rigid rotation of the crystal, another primitive setting or a supercell, in the ISO-IR frame. Away from Γ they agree with the literature at L of Fd-3m (-L4±, -L5± one-dimensional, -L6± two-dimensional), at L and X of F-43m, at X, R and M of Pm-3m and at K of P6_3/mmc. On every arm of the stars of the special points and of a point on every symmetry line of the structures in example/test_POSCARs, and at k + G, each irrep carries the name of its conjugate at the representative arm, and at -k the conjugate irrep carries the index of the irrep at k. A double-valued irrep that cannot be named keeps the generic key irrep_i(dim), and a NOTE: line under the irreps gives the reason; on the special points of the structures in example/test_POSCARs every irrep is named.

Limits.

  • The names are not the Bilbao Crystallographic Server (BCS) numbering, which numbers all double-valued irreps at k in one sequence without a parity suffix: at Γ of Pm-3m, CrystOD’s -GM6+, -GM6-, -GM8+ and -GM8- are BCS’s -GM6, -GM8, -GM10 and -GM11. Every --spinor run says how the names are numbered in a three-line remark (its text changed in v0.4.7: it used to call the names CrystOD’s own convention and now gives the Bradley & Cracknell numbering and its exceptions). The overlap engine can put IrRep’s Bilbao names on its levels (--vasp-irrep-json).

  • Where the numbering differs from Table 6.5 (32, 3m, -3m and 4, -4, 4/m above), and anywhere away from Γ, compare a name with another table through the characters (--show-irrep-table) or the fingerprint, not through the number: the comparison with Bradley & Cracknell is made at Γ, and their Tables 6.13 and 6.14 are not followed point by point.

  • The order of a tied pair is relative to the ISO-IR frame in use (it no longer depends on the arm of the star or the copy k + G that is given). Frames related by an element of the Euclidean normalizer (an origin shift, an equivalent basis such as (-a,-b,c), a 60-degree rotation in the trigonal groups) keep every single-valued label but can swap such a pair, as they can swap a complex single-valued pair (see Irrep labels and the frame of a structure). The decomposition line is unaffected when the two have equal multiplicity, as always at Γ and the other time-reversal-invariant points for real orbitals; character tables and band labels can swap. Compare names between inputs given in the same setting.

Quantitative crystal-orbital diagrams using extended-Hückel engine (--diagram)#

--diagram draws the quantitative crystal orbital diagram (COD) — the crystalline analogue of the molecular-orbital diagram of crystod-mol --diagram --ao-left ... --ao-right .... --co-left/--co-right split the crystal into two fragment sublattices by chemical formula (every atom must belong to one side; a count such as O3 validates against the primitive cell).

Each fragment carries its full-electron basis — every core and valence shell of every atom: core shells with Slater-rule Slater-type orbital (STO) exponents, valence shells with the extended-Hückel parameters, and the level energies from the archived neutral-atom PySCF calculations (one Hartree-Fock/def2-svp run per element; the runs are archived in reference/atomic_level_{El} of the repository, and the package carries their levels in crystod/atomic_levels.py). Each fragment then feels the removed sublattice as a point-charge lattice with the formal oxidation states (guessed with pymatgen; override with --oxidation Sr=+2 Ti=+4 O=-2): the Sc fragment of ScF3 sits in the field of the F lattice with Q = -1, the F3 fragment in the field of the Sc lattice with Q = +3 — the Madelung ligand field of the pre-bonding states:

The removed sublattice stays in the basis as ghost atoms and at the same time acts on the fragment as its formal-charge point lattice (--oxidation Sc=+3 F=-1 overrides the guessed oxidation states), so all three calculations span one AO space (counterpoise-consistent) and each fragment is one sublattice in the Madelung field of the other — the electronic state before chemical bond formation. Every cell is neutral, but each calculation still pins its own cell-averaged potential, so the raw columns are offset by one rigid, k-independent constant each; the diagram removes it by deep-level (XPS-style) alignment against the deepest chemically inert fragment level (printed with anchor, purity and k-spread), and --no-align keeps the raw references instead.

--oxidation Al=0 N=0 switches the model to neutral-atom sublattices (the extended-Hückel engine’s convention): the point-charge lattice then vanishes, and a sublattice with an odd electron count per cell (neutral Al: 3) is solved spin-restricted with Fermi smearing — the run says so, and --sigma sets the width (default 0.2 eV for such cells; integer aufbau would silently drop the unpaired electron). The isolated-atom columns pick their ground spin state by scanning the parity-consistent spins and keeping the lowest energy — a neutral N atom takes the Hund spin-3 ⁴S state, not a spin-1 doublet. Open-shell atomic columns display the alpha channel (said in the tooltip); for main-group atoms the alpha inter-shell spacings match the spin-averaged ones to ~0.1 eV, but for high-spin d/f atoms the exchange splitting can shift them by ~1 eV against the spin-restricted sublattice reference.

Crystal-orbital lines are colored by bonding character (blue = bonding, black = nonbonding, red = antibonding) from the COOP-style left-right overlap population of each eigenstate, and the fragment columns are drawn in the VESTA color of each level’s dominant element. Degeneracies are exact by construction: all displayed levels are re-diagonalized from the group-averaged Fock, whose invariance under the AO representation is verified against PySCF’s own overlap matrix (D+ S D = S, residual printed per k point).

Main options: --basis (default gth-dzvp-molopt-sr), --pseudo (default gth-pbe), --xc (default pbe, or hf), --ke-cutoff (default 200 Hartree), --kmesh (default round(8 A / |a_i|)), --max-l L (drop basis shells above l = L), --projection lowdin|mulliken, --no-ghost, --no-symmetrize, --sigma EV (Fermi smearing of the SCF occupations in eV; default 0, integer occupations, with two exceptions that apply whatever --sigma is: a column with an odd electron count always smears, and a column whose SCF does not converge is retried with Fermi smearing of width w, then w with a 0.3 Hartree virtual-level shift, then 5w with the shift, where w is --sigma when positive and 0.2 eV otherwise; the run prints each step as not converged; retrying with ... eV Fermi smearing), and --chk (below). The same-irrep resonance-integral tables are written to <output-stem>_coupling.txt next to the HTML. --sigma is refused without --pyscf: the extended-Hückel engine runs no SCF, and the VASP engines read finished runs (the width of the Va point charges of --vasp-setup is --vasp-sigma).

Because a three-SCF --pyscf run is expensive, the converged densities are cached automatically as CHK_{formula}.chk (rutile TiO2: CHK_TiO2.chk, the reduced formula in conventional chemical order). The next run on the same structure with the same options reads it and skips all three SCFs; a run whose options differ says so and recomputes, overwriting the file; --no-chk neither reads nor writes it. --chk FILE keeps the strict behaviour instead: that file is yours, so a parameter mismatch aborts with crystod --chk-info guidance rather than overwriting it.

crystod --help lists every value these take. The short version — of the GTH basis sets PySCF ships, only gth-szv-molopt-sr and gth-dzvp-molopt-sr cover the transition metals and beyond (H–Rn, with the lanthanides La–Lu absent from every GTH set, so --pyscf cannot run on a rare-earth compound at all — the extended-Hückel engine can); the larger sets (gth-tzvp, gth-qzv2p, the gth-aug-* and gth-cc-* families, …) stop at Ar or cover a handful of light elements, so they buy diffuse and polarization freedom only for main-group compounds. A basis or pseudopotential without an entry for one of the elements is refused with the list of sets that do cover the structure. For --pseudo, gth-pbe, gth-pade (LDA), gth-lda and gth-hfrev span H–Rn, gth-blyp reaches Bi, and the rest are light-element only. --xc accepts most libxc names (a name libxc does not know, and a VV10 functional such as wb97m-v — PySCF’s periodic code has no nonlocal correlation — are both refused up front with the reason); LDA (lda, svwn), GGA (pbe, pbesol, revpbe, blyp, bp86, pw91, b97-d), meta-GGA (scan, r2scan, tpss, revtpss) and hybrids (b3lyp, pbe0, hse06, m06, wb97x, …) all run here, plus hf for Hartree–Fock; hybrids evaluate exact exchange on the FFT grid, so give them --ke-cutoff 150 or more.

--onsite — the single-Hamiltonian diagram. Only the crystal SCF runs, and the fragment columns are the per-(element, shell) on-site multiplets of the converged crystal Fock — one level per induced irrep, no point charges and no alignment step, so a crystal level’s drop or rise against its parent is the orbital interaction:

crystod --diagram -c 225_PPOSCAR_NaCl --pyscf --onsite --co-left Na --co-right Cl \
    --kpoint X --kmesh 1 1 1 --ke-cutoff 80

The output. * VASP run * gives the run (NBANDS, NKPTS, ISPIN, spin-orbit, ENCUT from the WAVECAR header), the Fermi level (from OUTCAR, else vasprun.xml, DOSCAR or the WAVECAR header; the source is named) and the valence-band maximum: the highest eigenvalue with an occupation above 1/2 over every k point and spin channel of the file, as --vasp-zero vbm takes it. * Special k points in the WAVECAR * lists the analysed k points: the label, the fractional coordinates as stored in the file, the 1-based index in the file (k#), and the little group as the space-group type of its operations (the SALC mode’s “little group of k”). A complete level always has the degeneracy of its irrep (or the summed dimensions of A/B), so the degeneracies of a k point add up to NBANDS. VASP often returns the two bands of a time-reversal pair as pure states, one irrep each, and the labelling engine then names each band on its own (the overlap engine keeps it that way); --extract-irrep still lists them as one level (7-8  -1.0127  2  -L4/-L5  time-reversal pair (-L4 + -L5) on example/03_hybridization/vasp_wavecar/InP_SOC), and the per-band irreps are in the Python result (BandLevel.parts).

k points. By default every special point of the space group — the ISO-IR points crystod-bz --show-kpoint lists, the same list --diagram draws — that the WAVECAR holds is analysed, found on any arm of its star and modulo reciprocal-lattice vectors in the run’s own reciprocal basis (the fixture stores X as (1/2, 0, 0)). A special point that occurs several times (a band path passes Γ twice; a full mesh holds every arm) is analysed at its first occurrence and the other indices are listed (also k#149). Special points that are absent are named under the block. --kpoint restricts the analysis to special-point labels (GM, X, …; G/Gamma mean GM) and/or fractional coordinates (0 1/2 0, or 0,1/2,0), in the order given; a coordinate off the special points (on a line or plane) is named by the ISO-IR k label of its little group (LD, DT, …). --all-kpoints labels every k point of the file — symmetry lines, planes and general points included (GP, little group P1 (1)); on the 288 points of the SrTiO₃ band-structure run that takes about 40 s (mostly the symmetry set-up of each little group).

Energies. --zero vbm (default), efermi or raw (the run’s own eigenvalues); --window EMIN EMAX lists only the levels in that range, relative to the zero, keeping their level numbers (the band edges are taken from all levels).

Spin and spin-orbit coupling. An ISPIN = 2 run gives one table per spin channel (* Band irreps at GM [0, 0, 0], spin up *, then spin down, at every k point); --spin up|down keeps one channel. Each channel is labelled by the irreps of the space group of the POSCAR, which is exact for a ferromagnet; in an antiferromagnet whose spin sublattices are one species in the POSCAR the levels that the magnetic order splits come out unassigned. A spin-orbit (vasp_ncl, LSORBIT) run is recognised from the WAVECAR (each band record holds two spinor components): one band holds one electron and the levels carry the double-valued irreps (-GM8+, -R6+; the CsPbI₃ valence-band maximum is -R6+, its conduction-band minimum -R6-), named as by crystod --spinor and the overlap engine (Double-valued irreps): Bradley & Cracknell’s Table 6.5 numbering with the exceptions given there, not the Bilbao numbering, the same on every arm of a star and at k + G, and where two complex-conjugate irreps tie (little co-groups 2, m, 2/m, 32, 3m and -3m) their order is relative to the ISO-IR frame of the run’s POSCAR. They have no Mulliken symbols; IrRep’s Bilbao names (--vasp-irrep-json of the overlap engine) are not offered here.

The YAML file. -o FILE writes the same content as YAML, in the layout of phonon_irreps.yaml: the metadata (crystod_version, run with the directory, WAVECAR, structure, NBANDS, NKPTS, ISPIN, spin_orbit, ENCUT, E_fermi and its source, VBM and CBM, then space_group, energy_zero, zero_energy, degeneracy_tol, window), the analysed kpoints (k_label, k_position, k_index, little_group, copies), then irreps: one entry per k point (and spin) with its list of levels — level, bands (1-based), energy (eV relative to the zero), degeneracy, irrep (null when unassigned), mulliken (Γ), occupation and note — and finally band_edges and gap. yaml.safe_load reads it; without -o nothing is written. In Python the same result is crystod.salc.extract_band_irreps(path) (see Python API).

Electronic band unfolding of two VASP runs (--unfold)#

Example directory: example/09_band_unfold (testsuite section 9); not distributed: neither the pip package nor the repository holds its two runs (4.1 MB of WAVECARs), so the commands below run on your own parent and child band runs

A distorted phase and its high-symmetry parent are usually computed side by side: the parent in its small cell, the low-symmetry child in its own, larger cell. The child’s bands then come out folded into its smaller Brillouin zone: I4/mcm SrTiO3 (10 atoms, two cubic cells) has twice the bands of cubic SrTiO3 at every k, and which of them carries which cubic band cannot be read off its band plot. --unfold reads the two finished band runs and draws the child’s bands in the Brillouin zone of the parent: every child Bloch state at the child wave vector K is split over the N = det M parent wave vectors k = M^-1 K + g_i it unfolds onto, with the share of its plane-wave coefficients on each (V. Popescu and A. Zunger, Phys. Rev. B 85, 085201 (2012); the scheme of fold2Bloch, O. Rubel, A. Bokhanchuk, S. J. Ahmed and E. Assmann, Phys. Rev. B 90, 115202 (2014)). No new VASP run is needed: the two runs are the ordinary band calculations of the two phases, each along its own path, and the parent eigenvalues are drawn over the unfolded child wherever the two runs sample the same parent k. It is the electronic counterpart of crystod-phonon --unfold, which unfolds phonon bands from force constants.

crystod --unfold --parent-wavecar SrTiO3_Pm-3m/BAND/WAVECAR --child-wavecar SrTiO3_I4mcm/BAND/WAVECAR
# -> band_unfold.pdf, band_unfold.dat, band_unfold.yaml

What is read:

  • the child (--child-wavecar, required): its WAVECAR, or the run directory that holds it, and next to it the POSCAR (--child-poscar FILE names another file of the same cell; CONTCAR is not read), the KPOINTS (the k weights and the labels of the band path), the OUTCAR for E_fermi (else vasprun.xml, DOSCAR or the WAVECAR header) and, when the KPOINTS gives no path, EIGENVAL or vasprun.xml for the k weights;

  • the parent (--parent-wavecar): the same files of the parent run. Its eigenvalues are drawn over the unfolded child, and its band path is the x axis of page 2 of the figure. Without a parent run, --parent-poscar FILE gives the parent structure alone: the setting and the images, but no parent levels and no page 2.

The two structures fix the transformation between the cells, found as in crystod-phonon --unfold: it is the setting of crystod-group --supergroup-cif PARENT --subgroup-cif CHILD. Of the child WAVECAR, the plane-wave coefficients are read only at the k points that are unfolded, one k point at a time.

The output below is that of two band runs of SrTiO3 that are not in the repository: cubic Pm-3m (5 atoms) and the I4/mcm phase with the antiphase rotation of the octahedra (a0a0c-) in its 10-atom primitive body-centred tetragonal cell, both with METAGGA = LAK and ENCUT = 550. Each is one SCF run whose explicit KPOINTS lists the weighted mesh and then the band path as zero-weight points with ! K.n LABEL comments: Γ-X-M-Γ-R-X|R-M for the parent (244 path points), the Hinuma path of the body-centred tetragonal zone Γ-X-P-N-Γ-M-S|S0-Γ|X-R|G-M for the child (213 points). The child WAVECAR is 349 MB (253 k points, 48 bands), the parent’s 141 MB (300 k points, 32 bands), and the run takes about 6 s on a Mac Studio M3 Ultra. The example directory of the developers (not distributed) holds a small version of the same pair (the same POSCARs, plain PBE, ENCUT = 175, two intervals per leg; its README lists what it gives), run as

cd example/09_band_unfold
crystod --unfold --parent-wavecar SrTiO3/parent --child-wavecar SrTiO3/child

The weights#

The conventions are those of crystod-phonon --unfold: lattice vectors are rows, A_child = M A_parent with an integer matrix M, N = det M, and fractional wave vectors transform as K = M k (k = M^-1 K in the parent). The N parent wave vectors g_i with M g_i integer are those that fold onto the child Γ point; they are named with their ISO-IR labels in the parent (here g0 = GM and g1 = R, the k point of the condensed rotation). A child plane wave K + G, with G an integer triple of the child reciprocal basis as the WAVECAR lists it, is a parent plane wave at M^-1 (K + G) = M^-1 K + g_i with g_i = M^-1 G mod 1. The plane waves of a child state therefore fall into N cosets, and the weight of band n on image i is

w_i(n) = sum_{G in coset i} sum_spinor |C_n(G)|^2  /  sum_G sum_spinor |C_n(G)|^2

The coset of every G is integer arithmetic, so nothing is interpolated. The WAVECAR holds the PAW pseudo wave functions, whose plane-wave norm is not 1 (sum |C|^2 between 0.295 and 1.548 on these runs; the summary prints the range): every band is normalised before the split, and the one-centre (augmentation) part of the PAW wave function is neglected, as plane-wave unfolding codes do. The weights of a band add up to 1 over its images; the summary prints the largest deviation (5.4e-15 here), and above 1e-10 the run stops with an internal ERROR. A spin-orbit (vasp_ncl) run adds both spinor components.

Bands within --degeneracy-tol (default 1 meV, single linkage) form one level. Inside a degenerate level the weights of the single bands depend on the basis VASP happens to return, so a level of deg bands carries the level weight

W_i = Re Tr(S^-1 P_i),    S = C C^H,    P_i = C Pi_i C^H

with C the deg rows of plane-wave coefficients of the level and Pi_i the mask of coset i. W_i adds up to deg over the images, equals w_i(n) for a single band and the sum of the band weights when the rows are orthogonal, and does not change under any mixing of the degenerate bands: mixing all 1070 degenerate levels of the I4/mcm run with random unitary matrices changes W by at most 2.7e-15, while the weights of the single bands change by up to 0.98. The tables, the figure (marker area W, color W/deg), the parent-path view, the checks and the YAML file use W; the .dat file holds both.

Only the integer matrix M enters the weights; the origin shift and the rotation between the two frames do not. The child sits in the parent in several equivalent settings (24 here), which describe one crystal in differently oriented frames: another setting puts another arm of a parent star on an image, with the same weights (with --trans-mat 1 0 1 1 0 -1 -1 1 0 the child X point unfolds onto X (0, 1/2, 0) and M (1/2, 0, 1/2) instead of X (1/2, 0, 0) and M (0, 1/2, 1/2)). --trans-mat chooses one, as in crystod-phonon --unfold; a matrix that is not one of them is refused with the list of the valid ones.

Two k points of the child related by an operation of the child are related in the parent as well: if K' = R K for an operation R of the child point group (on fractional reciprocal coordinates, (W^-1)^T of the real-space rotation W; time reversal adds -R), the weight of the parent point k_p at K is the weight of M^-1 R M k_p at K’. This symmetry transport serves a parent path point whose K = M k_p is not in the child list but one of its images under the child point group or time reversal is. The summary checks it on the equivalent points of the child path.

Energy zero and range#

The energy zero is taken from the child run and applied to both runs (--zero vbm, the default: the child’s valence-band maximum over all its k points), so the offsets between the two runs are the raw ones: on these runs the raw eigenvalues agree to a median of -0.009 eV (the alignment line of the summary), while zeroing each run at its own VBM would add the 0.121 eV by which the two maxima differ. --zero efermi (or fermi) takes the child’s E_fermi, --zero raw (or none) the eigenvalues as written, and --zero EV any energy in eV. VASP puts E_fermi of an insulator anywhere in the gap (the smearing midpoint), so --zero efermi on an insulator is arbitrary within the gap (a NOTE says so). --separate-zero zeros each run at its own reference instead (with --zero vbm or --zero efermi): the setup block prints both values, and the footers of the figure say which zero is in force.

Without --window, the energy range follows the spectrum. The child path levels are sorted, and from the highest level at or below the zero the valence block runs down as long as consecutive levels are closer than 2 eV (here it ends at -4.528 eV, the bottom of the O 2p bands). The figure shows from 1 eV below the block to 1 eV above the completeness limit (or 0.3 eV above the top child level, if lower), the tables from 0.5 eV below the block to 6 eV (not above the figure’s top), both rounded outward to 0.5 eV: -6 .. 10 eV and -5.5 .. 6 eV here. Both pages use the same range, --window EMIN EMAX (eV relative to the zero) sets both, and the summary names the range that holds the whole spectrum (--window -35 12). The .dat and .yaml files hold every level whatever the range.

The child run has N times the bands of one parent cell only up to its lowest top band: above that energy (here 8.554 eV, from 48 child bands = 2 x 24) some child k miss bands that would unfold there, so the unfolded spectrum is incomplete. The figure shades that region, and the summary says where it starts.

Requirements and limits#

  • Two finished VASP runs with LWAVE = .TRUE.: the WAVECAR of vasp_std or vasp_ncl (a gamma-only vasp_gam WAVECAR is refused) and the POSCAR the run was done with. The weights refer to the child’s own cell, which need not be primitive (a NOTE says so); a non-primitive parent cell is unfolded onto as given (the zone the parent run computed), with a NOTE.

  • Any setting, with det M of any size, that crystod-group --supergroup-cif finds for the two POSCARs: the child must be a distorted supercell of the parent, with the same composition per parent cell and no displacement above 1.8 Å.

  • The weights need only the child WAVECAR (and the parent structure); the parent run adds the overlay, the parent columns of the tables, page 2 and the alignment. For a meaningful overlay the two runs should use the same ENCUT, POTCARs and functional (the alignment line shows how well they agree).

  • Spin: an ISPIN = 2 child gets tables and pages per spin channel (--spin up|down keeps one). Its POSCAR symmetry need not be a symmetry of the spin density, so only K and -K relate child k points (for the parent-path view, the overlay and the symmetry checks), and a NOTE says so. An ISPIN = 2 child with an ISPIN = 1 parent is accepted (the parent levels are drawn on both channels, with a NOTE); an ISPIN = 1 child with an ISPIN = 2 parent is refused, and so is a spin-orbit run paired with a scalar one.

  • A spin-orbit (vasp_ncl) child uses the child point group and time reversal, which assumes a non-magnetic, time-reversal-symmetric run (a NOTE says so). For a magnetic vasp_ncl run, read only page 1, the child path view, whose markers use no symmetry of the child.

  • A metallic run is read the same way (the weights do not depend on the occupations); the VBM zero is then the highest level occupied above 1/2, and --zero efermi is usually the better zero.

  • The PAW one-centre part is neglected, and the weights are those of the pseudo wave function (the standard approximation of plane-wave unfolding).

  • The unfolded spectrum is exact at the k points the child run samples; page 2 shows the parent legs the child path misses only at their sampled points (use --unfold-kpoints), and above the completeness limit it is incomplete.

Errors#

User errors stop with one ERROR: line and exit status 1: no WAVECAR at the path, no POSCAR in a run directory (CONTCAR is not used), a POSCAR whose cell is not the cell of its WAVECAR (lattice vectors differing by more than 1e-4 Å), an empty, truncated or gamma-only WAVECAR, two structures that cannot be related by a group-subgroup setting, a --trans-mat that is not one of the equivalent settings (the valid ones are listed), --zero vbm without an occupied band, an ISPIN = 1 child with an ISPIN = 2 parent, a spin-orbit run paired with a scalar one, --spin on a run that is not spin-polarized, or an output directory that does not exist. Wrong option syntax is a usage error with exit status 2: an option of another mode, --unfold together with --unfold-kpoints, a missing --child-wavecar, a --trans-mat that is not nine integers, --separate-zero with a numeric --zero, or --zero nan.

In Python the command is crystod.salc.unfold_bands, with crystod.salc.unfold_kpoints for --unfold-kpoints (Python API).

The forms of --vasp. It takes no path (the current directory is the ROOT), one ROOT with the three directories of the table above, or the three run directories in any order — whatever they are named and wherever they live:

crystod --diagram --co-left SrTi --co-right O3 --vasp .

        --vasp ./BAND ./BAND_sublattice1 ./BAND_sublattice2

With three paths the crystal run is the one whose POSCAR carries no Va species, and the order they are given in makes no difference to the output. Two paths, or more than three, are refused in one line that shows the accepted forms. DIR/band is used when it carries the PROCAR. Which sublattice directory is the left and which the right column is decided from the real (non-Va) elements of its POSCAR, never from the number in its name; --vasp-crystal DIR --vasp-left DIR --vasp-right DIR override any one column in any of the three forms.

-c is optional, and it fixes the setting. Without it the structure is the crystal run’s own POSCAR. With it, the analysis is done in the primitive cell of that file — so the irreps agree level for level with the other engines and with crystod -c FILE --element … --orbital … on the same file — and each run is mapped onto that setting: one global origin shift, a permutation of the atoms and lattice-vector wraps, with Va atoms mapping onto the removed ones. The run’s lattice matrix has to be the -c primitive one within a relative 1e-3. A -c file with Sr at the origin and runs written with Ti there is therefore the same analysis, not an error:

crystod --diagram -c POSCAR-finish --co-left SrTi --co-right O3 --vasp .
#  * Setting *
#   ./BAND/POSCAR = the -c cell shifted by (1/2, 1/2, 1/2);
#   atom order Sr Ti O O O -> Sr#1 Ti#2 O#5 O#3 O#4
#   irrep labels refer to the POSCAR-finish setting

The mapping is stated in the terminal report, in the level table, in the JSON ("setting") and as a chip on the page whenever it is not the identity: irrep labels at R, X and M depend on the choice of origin, so it matters which file they belong to. The energies, the projected weights, the site shifts and the connector weights do not — they are the same numbers in either setting. If the -c file and the crystal run are the same crystal in genuinely different bases, the analysis falls back to the crystal run’s own setting and says so; if they are not the same crystal, the run stops with one line naming what differs and suggesting -c BAND/POSCAR.

--vasp-setup writes in the crystal run’s setting. -c is optional there too: without it the structure is ROOT/BAND/POSCAR. With it, the -c file names the fragments and fixes the formal charges, but the POSCARs that are written always follow the crystal run’s own cell, origin and ion order — they are ROOT/BAND/POSCAR with one sublattice renamed to Va<q><sign>, line for line. The assignment travels there through the same mapping --vasp uses, and the setting is printed ( setting       : …). Otherwise the two fragment runs would sit on another origin — and, through the -c file’s own lattice constant, on a slightly different cell — than the crystal run they are compared with. A -c file that is not the crystal run’s cell in another setting stops --vasp-setup in one line suggesting -c BAND/POSCAR.

A run directory that already holds a finished calculation (OUTCAR, PROCAR or vasprun.xml) is not rewritten: --vasp-setup stops in one line naming the directory and the files, because the INCAR it would write is not the INCAR that was run. --force overwrites anyway.

Names. With an ordinary -c file the page is CrystOD_{cell}_vasp.html, as in the other engines. Without -c, or when the -c file lives inside one of the run directories, the name comes from the composition and the space group — CrystOD_SrTiO3_Pm-3m_vasp.html, titled SrTiO3, Pm-3m — so that it is never the run directory’s BAND.

The point-charge sublattices. A sublattice run names its switched-off atoms Va<q><sign> (Va2-, Va4+) — a species of a modified VASP that represents them as a smeared Coulomb charge plus a short repulsive wall, the wall standing for the Pauli repulsion of the removed ion’s core, as in total-ion-potential embedding. The formal charges come from CrystOD’s own oxidation-state resolution (--oxidation), so the cell stays neutral and each fragment is one sublattice in the Madelung field of the other. VASP itself is proprietary and is not distributed with CrystOD; the patch that adds the Va species is available to VASP licensees on request. --vasp itself needs no patched VASP — it only reads the output files.

The wall is what a point charge needs to stop being a bare −q/r well, and it is calibrated: --vasp-setup writes VACSIGMA 0.5, VACRWALL 0.45 and VACWALL = 2.5 q e²√(2/π)/σ (--vasp-sigma, --vasp-rwall, --vasp-wall-factor change them; one height is written per Va species, so the charge scaling stays automatic). A 72-point scan on SrTiO₃ and ScF₃ shows the whole family organized by one coordinate, the radius r₁₀ = b√(2 ln(A/10 eV)) at which the wall still repels by 10 eV: below 0.7 Å the anion sublattice collapses into the bare well (bands below the projection floor, metallic points), above 1.4 Å the wall expels the anion valence shell (an O 2p manifold three times too wide). The setting above sits at r₁₀ = 0.99 Å for q = +2 and 1.13 Å for q = +4 — the middle of the 0.85–1.25 Å plateau, one setting for every charge — and is the joint minimum of the anchor-residual rms on both test systems while matching the crystal’s anion p-manifold width to 0.1 eV. Fluorite CaF₂, a third space group with a tetrahedral anion cage, transfers without retuning. What the wall chooses is the anion column’s position: over the whole scan the shift ranges over 45 eV while the residual rms on the plateau moves by 0.1 eV.

Irreps from the projections. A LORBIT = 12 PROCAR lists the complex projections ⟨Y_lm at atom | ψ_nk⟩. CrystOD treats that vector as a vector in an AO-like (atom, l, m) basis and classifies it with the same representation matrices and character projectors it builds for a genuine AO basis — the Bloch-phased site permutation times the real Wigner matrices, with no further gauge conjugation, because the PROCAR coefficients are already in that Bloch gauge — after reordering VASP’s m = −l..l rows into its own component order. Degenerate bands are grouped adaptively until the irrep multiplicities come out integral. The result is checked against the site-symmetry induced representation (the SALC decomposition the report prints for every element and shell), and a level whose best irrep weight falls below 0.9 is listed in a purity warning. PAW sphere projections do not sum to one, so levels whose projected weight per degenerate partner falls below --vasp-projection-floor (0.30) are free-electron-like and are dropped; the report prints the weights bracketing the cut.

Alignment. Each of the three runs pins its own G = 0 average potential to zero, so the raw columns are offset by one constant each. The crystal column is the reference (shift 0) and every fragment level is raised onto it by a site-resolved shift: one constant per (column, element), because the shells of one atom move together but different sites do not — a cation that receives covalent back-donation in the crystal has all its levels raised by the intra-atomic Coulomb repulsion the bare-point-charge fragment lacks (on SrTiO₃ the Ti site moves 11.7 eV more than the ionic Sr site). Each element’s shift is fitted to the symmetry-forbidden probes alone wherever it has any — fragment levels whose irrep has no partner in the other sublattice at that k, which therefore must coincide with their crystal counterpart, an exact constraint. The pure XPS-style counterparts (absolute composition ≥ 0.80) are then reported as what they are, the measured bonding shift of each pair, instead of being averaged into the scale; an element with no forbidden probe (CaF₂ Ca) is fitted to them and flagged, because their residuals average to zero by construction. The anchor pool is cut at the default window, never at --vasp-window, so the drawn range cannot move the energy scale. The report lists every anchor, the per-shell means, the spread per element and the site-potential difference between the elements of one column, plus an independent occupied-band trace diagnostic that uses no projection at all. --vasp-zero vbm|efermi|raw sets the energy zero of the page and the tables (default E − E_VBM, the VBM being the highest occupied crystal eigenvalue over all k of the crystal run). --vasp-align rigid falls back to one shift per column (whose numbers are printed as a diagnostic in either mode), --vasp-anchor EL nl pins a column on a shell of your choice — and selects rigid, since that is what it means — and --no-align keeps the raw VASP energies.

Bond character. A plane-wave calculation has no overlap matrix, so there is no COOP population to quote. The character is read from the aligned energies instead: a crystal level carrying 95 % or more of its projected weight on one sublattice is nonbonding; otherwise it is bonding when it lies below, and antibonding when it lies above, the composition-weighted energy of the fragment levels its connectors point at (0.3 eV deadband, quoted beside the alignment residual the run itself measures — the centre-of-gravity sum rule over every complete (k, irrep) manifold, 0.59 eV mean on SrTiO₃ and 0.19 eV on CaF₂ — so a colour inside the noise says so). A level with a parent on an element whose own anchors disagree by more than 1 eV keeps the neutral grey stroke, and so does one whose drawn parents hold less than half of it or whose character would change if the levels the window hides were drawn: one shift does not describe that site, so the sign of E − E(parents) carries no chemistry there. With bare point charges that happens on the site whose shells disagree most — on SrTiO₃ the Sr site, whose empty, diffuse 4d shell sits 1.5 eV away from what its own semicore says.

WAVECAR-overlap engine (--diagram --vasp --vasp-engine overlap)#

The anchor engine above puts the three columns on one scale with one shift per site, fitted to the levels that symmetry forbids to mix. Where a site has no such level — the B-site s and p shells of SrGeO₃ (Ge 4s/4p) and CsPbI₃ (Pb 6s/6p) at Γ, X, M and R — the fit absorbs the very bonding shift the diagram is meant to show. --vasp-engine overlap needs no energy alignment at all: it uses only the wavefunctions of the two sublattice runs and the eigenvalues of the crystal, and writes the crystal Kohn–Sham Hamiltonian in the basis of the sublattice orbitals, so every column is on the crystal’s own scale (E − E_VBM).

crystod --diagram --co-left SrTi --co-right O3 --vasp-setup ROOT --vasp-engine overlap
#   the inputs of --vasp-setup, with LWAVE = .TRUE.; checks the crystal run's NBANDS
crystod --diagram --co-left SrTi --co-right O3 --vasp ROOT --vasp-engine overlap \
        --vasp-cache sto_overlaps.npz
#   -> CrystOD_SrTiO3_Pm-3m_vasp_overlap.html + .json + .txt, and the overlap cache
crystod --diagram --co-left SrTi --co-right O3 --vasp --vasp-engine overlap \
        --vasp-cache sto_overlaps.npz
#   the same analysis from the cache alone (no run directory: no WAVECAR is read)

What it computes. Stage 1 reads the three WAVECARs at the special k points and computes the all-electron PAW overlaps A_fn = ⟨φ_f|ψ_n⟩ between the Bloch states of the sublattice runs and those of the crystal (the plane-wave basis is the same in the three runs; the one-centre corrections come from the crystal POTCAR, including the term at the sites where a sublattice run has a point charge). That takes seconds, and --vasp-cache FILE keeps the result (a few MB of derived numbers, no PAW data). Stage 2 builds the model space: the crystal levels up to VBM + 14 eV (the frozen window) are kept exactly, and the empty active sublattice orbitals that lie higher are drawn from the bands above by a projection-only disentanglement — the effective outer levels, drawn dotted. A crystal level is bonding, nonbonding or antibonding by the sign of its inter-sublattice COHP in the Löwdin picture, COHP_n(f,g) = 2 Re[c*_nf H_fg c_ng] summed over the pairs of sublattice orbitals on different sublattices (negative = bonding; below 0.05 eV in magnitude nonbonding), split by shell pair in the tooltip. The connectors are the Löwdin populations distributed over the frozen-ion parents of the same sublattice and irrep. Every parent carries its ledger: bare d_f = ⟨φ|H|φ⟩ → Pauli shift (orthogonalization to the occupied orbitals of the other sublattice) → closed-shell (filled–filled) or empty–empty mixing → covalent shift (occupied–empty mixing) → crystal level. The covalency count — electrons in formally empty shells per cell — is a chip and a note per k point. The irrep labels are computed from the plane waves of each run (crystod.wavecar_irreps): CrystOD’s ISO-IR labels, the two sublattice runs taken in the crystal’s frame. The page has no hover sketch: the overlaps carry no atomic-orbital coefficients.

What the runs need. The layout of --vasp-setup (the crystal and the two Va point-charge sublattice runs, --vasp resolving them exactly as for the anchor engine), with

  • the same primitive cell, site order, ENCUT, PREC and k list in the three runs, the special points in that list (the --vasp-setup KPOINTS carries them; on a full mesh any arm of the star is used);

  • LWAVE = .TRUE. in all three runs — --vasp-setup --vasp-engine overlap writes it, and the engine stops in one line when a WAVECAR is missing;

  • enough bands: the crystal bands must reach VBM + 14 eV plus a few eV of outer bands at every k point (--vasp-setup --vasp-engine overlap checks a finished crystal run and suggests an NBANDS; the runs behind the example caches used 64 for SrTiO₃ and SrGeO₃, 96 for CsPbI₃ and 128 with spin-orbit coupling);

  • the crystal POTCAR, whose PAW data serve every site (the sublattice POTCARs, when present, are checked against it), with semicore datasets as for the anchor engine;

  • ISPIN = 1; spin-orbit (vasp_ncl) runs are read as well.

Options. --vasp-shells sets the active sublattice shells (Sr-4s Sr-4p Sr-5s Sr-4d Ti-3d Ti-4s Ti-4p O-2s O-2p; default auto = the POTCAR valence shells plus one standard empty shell per element — (n−1)d for groups 1–2, np for groups 3–12 — and auto-full = the occupied levels plus the lowest empty manifold of every cation shell); the chosen shells are printed. --vasp-frozen-window EV (default 14) and --vasp-frozen-qmin Q (default 0.5: window levels with less projection on the active orbitals are left to the disentanglement) set the model space. --vasp-cache FILE is written after stage 1 and reused when it covers the same runs (a cache of more k points serves a subset; one of other runs is never overwritten). --kpoint, -c (it names the outputs and must be the runs’ crystal; the analysis is done in the crystal run’s own cell, the frame of the WAVECAR and of the labels), --output, --vasp-crystal/--vasp-left/--vasp-right and --tolerance mean what they mean for the anchor engine, and --vasp-window EMIN EMAX (relative to the VBM) leaves the levels outside off all three columns of the page — by default every level of the model space is drawn, and the view opens on the frontier states. The alignment options of the anchor engine are refused.

Outputs. CrystOD_<cell>_vasp_overlap.html (the page), .json (every number: crystal and sublattice levels, the three pictures with populations, COHPs and verdicts, the ledger, the checks — the layout of the published spectral_onsite analysis plus a crystod block with the options) and .txt (the report: settings, Key results, checks, the sensitivity of the parents to the frozen window and to the cross-sphere term, and per k point the ledger and the levels of the three pictures). The names follow the anchor engine’s (_soc is appended for spin-orbit runs), and the terminal prints the Key results.

Spin-orbit coupling. For vasp_ncl runs one band holds one electron, a Kramers pair is one level (drawn with one bar per pair), and the levels carry double-valued irreps: -K<n><p>, named as by crystod --spinor (Double-valued irreps), drawn with an overbar (the CsPbI₃ VBM is R̄6+, the CBM R̄6−). --vasp-irrep-json PATTERN puts IrRep’s Bilbao names (-R6, -R8) on the levels instead, by band index — a path with {run} for the run directory’s name, or a directory holding <run>/irrep-output.json; IrRep is run separately (irrep -code=vasp -spinor -fWAV=RUN/WAVECAR -fPOS=RUN/POSCAR -kpoints=… -kpnames=GM,X,M,R -refUC=1,0,0,0,1,0,0,0,1 -shiftUC=0,0,0 -json_file=irrep-output, without -Ecut, whose truncated basis mislabels high bands). --vasp-scalar-reference JSON names the results of the scalar runs of the same crystal: every spin-orbit level and parent then lists the compatible scalar levels (Γ × D(1/2)) — the CsPbI₃ CBM R̄6− at +0.61 eV comes from the scalar R4− at +1.77 eV.

Caveats. The active space is a minimal valence set, as a COHP code uses a minimal basis: diffuse higher shells (Pb 6d and 7s, Cs 6p, Ge 4d, Sr 5p) in a symmetric Löwdin basis produce spurious populations and COHPs, so the default rule leaves them out — change it with --vasp-shells, and test the sensitivity with auto-full. The PAW data of every site come from the crystal POTCAR. The effective outer levels depend on the bands above the frozen window, i.e. on NBANDS. A multiplet cut by the top of the band list stays unlabelled.

With --dos, --sigma EV is the width of the Gaussian broadening of the curves (default 0.15 eV; it must be positive, and the TXT summary records it in its header line), not an SCF smearing, and --xrange XMIN XMAX sets the energy range of the plot in eV on the --align scale (default: the whole spectrum); --xrange is refused in every other mode. --band takes no --sigma: its SCF smearing is not on the crystod command line, and neither is that of --dos, so both run their SCFs with integer occupations, apart from the automatic smearing of an odd-electron column and the smeared retry of a column that does not converge (0.2 eV, then 1 eV).

Both write a PDF, a CSV of every curve (or every band energy and weight) and a TXT summary, both accept --onsite (neither needs the fragment densities), and both share one checkpoint with the diagram runs. The SCF smearing is one of the parameters a checkpoint records (--chk-info prints a positive value on its method line and adds --sigma to its reuse line), so a checkpoint written by --diagram --pyscf or --visualize --pyscf with a positive --sigma is refused by --band and --dos as written with different parameters. --no-chk is accepted by --band, --dos and --visualize --pyscf as by --diagram --pyscf, but changes nothing there: these modes never use the automatic CHK_{formula}.chk cache and read or write a checkpoint only through --chk FILE.

4. Dipole selection rules (--diagram)#

Example directory: example/04_selection_rules (testsuite section 4); after pip install: crystod-example 04

Every --diagram run (extended Hückel, --pyscf and --vasp, both VASP engines) ends each k point with the electric-dipole selection rule of its band edge: whether light can drive the transition from the valence-band maximum to the conduction-band minimum at that k point, and for which polarizations. It is meant for reading an absorption edge or a photoluminescence spectrum against the diagram (a dipole-forbidden direct gap absorbs weakly). The group theory: a vertical transition i -> f is allowed for light polarized along a only if Γf* ⊗ ΓV ⊗ Γi contains the identity of the little group of k with an invariant that has a nonzero a-component (ΓV: the polar vector). No flag is needed:

...
* Dipole selection rules at X (0,1/2,0) *
   VBM X2+ #1 (-13.81 eV) -> CBM X2- #1 (-12.30 eV): forbidden

How to read it:

  • The block is part of the report of its k point (its content is indented like that of the * k point <k> * block above it); the k coordinates of its title are those of the * k point * blocks (the primitive reciprocal basis of the standardized cell).

  • VBM is the highest crystal level holding electrons and CBM the lowest empty one (the HOMO/LUMO markers of the page; levels within 1 meV count as one edge), named as in the diagram (GM5- #1: irrep and running number), with their energies.

  • allowed (x, y, z) lists the polarizations that can drive the transition; the answer is resolved per component with the representation matrices of the two small irreps (at X of SrTiO3 the pair X1+ / X3- is allowed only for the polarization along k, y here, the pair X1+ / X5- only perpendicular to it, x and z). forbidden means that no polarization can.

  • When the band edge is forbidden, first allowed: gives the allowed occupied -> empty pair of smallest energy difference, with dE.

  • x, y, z are the Cartesian axes of the input POSCAR, the frame of the Raman tensors of crystod-phonon --raman-tensor (the operations of the standardized cell are rotated back into the input axes with spglib’s std_rotation_matrix). An allowed polarization subspace that is not spanned by coordinate axes is printed as vectors in the input frame.

In the HTML page, click one crystal orbital and then another: the second level is marked allowed (x,y,z) (green) or forbidden (grey), the first from, and the level panel repeats the verdict with the same text as the terminal block. Limits. The rules are for vertical (direct) one-photon electric-dipole transitions between the levels of the diagram: “allowed” means that symmetry does not forbid the transition, and its strength needs the matrix elements. Which levels form the band edges is the engine’s answer (extended Hückel can get the level order wrong; cross-check with --pyscf or --vasp). Spin-orbit (double-group) levels of --vasp --vasp-engine overlap are not evaluated (the block says so).

Python API. dipole_selection_rules returns a list of DipoleSelectionRules records (one per special k point), and little_group_dipole_table a LittleGroupDipoleTable; there is no MCP tool for this section:

from crystod import salc
from crystod.vasp_io import read_poscar_cell

cell = read_poscar_cell("221_PPOSCAR_SrTiO3")
diagram = salc.CrystalOrbitalDiagram(cell, ["SrTi"], ["O3"])   # or any engine's diagram
rules = salc.dipole_selection_rules(diagram)       # one record per special k point
[(r.name, r.band_edge.verdict) for r in rules]     # [('GM', 'allowed (x, y, z)'), ...]
salc.dipole_selection_rules(diagram, "X")[0].first_allowed.verdict   # 'allowed (x, z)'
print("\n".join(salc.format_dipole_selection_rules(rules[0], "(0,0,0)")))

levels, _ = diagram.solve_at([0, 0, 0])            # from levels solved elsewhere
edge = salc.band_edge_selection_rules(diagram.builder, "GM", [0, 0, 0], levels["mo"])
table = salc.little_group_dipole_table(diagram.builder, [0, 0, 0])
table.components("GM5+", "GM1+")                   # () = forbidden
table.subspace("GM5+", "GM4-")                     # orthonormal rows of the allowed polarizations

dipole_selection_rules(diagram, kpoint=None) solves each k point with diagram.solve_at unless the engine report has already done so (the rules are cached on the diagram); kpoint takes a special-point name or three primitive reciprocal coordinates. It takes the diagram objects of the EHT, PySCF and VASP PROCAR engines; for the page object of the VASP overlap engine it returns the rules that engine’s report cached. little_group_dipole_table(..., axes="standardized") gives the rules in the axes of the standardized primitive cell instead of the input cell.

Two textbook checks (testsuite section 4): in cuprite Cu2O (Pn-3m, the Ag2O structure type) the Cu 3d GM5+ -> Cu 4s GM1+ transition at Γ is even-to-even and dipole-forbidden, the origin of the weak yellow exciton series; in Si (Fd-3m) GM5+ (Γ25’) -> GM4- (Γ15), the E0’ critical point, is allowed. The extended-Hückel engine places the Cu 4p band of Cu2O below Cu 4s, so its band-edge line there names a d -> p transition; the d -> s pair can be checked on the page by pressing “Show all energy levels” (GM1+ #7 lies outside the opening energy window) and clicking the two levels.

  • --real-coefficient re-combines degenerate SALC components into real-coefficient form whenever the projected space is closed under complex conjugation (real-type irreps at k = -k points). For example, the GM3+ (Eg) SALCs of Sc_d, which are otherwise produced as (d_z2 +/- i d_x2-y2)/sqrt(2), become d_z2 and d_x2-y2. The spanned space is unchanged; only the unitary basis choice within the irrep space is rotated. The closure is tested in the atomic gauge, where complex conjugation is time reversal, and the real basis is the one the page draws; the printed lines stay in the site gauge, so at a k point with a gauge NOTE they can still carry factors of i (Si p X3 at X: Si1 (atom 0): p_z: +0.7071 / Si2 (atom 1): p_x: (+0.0000+0.7071j), drawn as real lobes). An irrep space that is not closed under conjugation keeps its complex basis, and a NOTE: no real-coefficient basis exists for ... line names it.

  • --mode-index N restricts the output to one irrep-grouped mode space (1-based).

  • --bond EL1 EL2 MAX is repeatable, so several bond types can be drawn at once.

PySCF eigen-levels in the viewer (--visualize --pyscf)#

The SALCs above are the symmetry-adapted basis — the states before any Hamiltonian. With --pyscf the same page shows the actual PySCF eigenstates, either of one fragment sublattice (the pre-bonding states in the removed sublattice’s point-charge field, exactly the --diagram --pyscf columns) or of the full crystal (the states after bonding):

crystod --visualize --pyscf -c 221_PPOSCAR_ScF3 --bond Sc F 3 --real-coefficient --chk scf3.chk

No --element/--orbital/--kpoint are needed: the special k points come from the space group automatically (one page per k point; --kpoint GM restricts the output). The SALC-basis table becomes Mode | Irrep | Comp. | Energy (eV) — one row per degenerate partner, level labels in the diagram convention (GM4- #2, Sc 3d R3+), energies on the shared deep-level-aligned scale, so the Sc, F3 and crystal pages are directly comparable (--no-align keeps the raw references). Clicking a row draws the eigenvector’s wave function on the k-commensurate display cell (or the conventional cell with --conventional), with the usual VESTA-style bonds and polyhedra. With a shared --chk the three commands above pay the SCF once (~5 s per page set afterwards). The SCF options are those of --diagram --pyscf, --sigma EV (the Fermi smearing of the SCF occupations) included, and a checkpoint is shared only between runs with the same values.

Two options control what the sketches show:

  • --diagonalize canonicalizes the degenerate partners (RREF), so the arbitrary unitary mixture the SCF returns becomes axis-aligned — the R5+ t2g triplet of ScF3 turns into pure d_xy / d_yz / d_xz; energies are unchanged.

  • --valence-only drops semicore shells (occupied fragment bands more than 12 eV below the crystal VBM — Sc 3s, Sc 3p, F 2s here; detected automatically and printed) from the drawn wave functions. Their admixture in a valence level is an on-site orthogonality tail whose radial node makes a genuinely bonding σ level look antibonding; levels a semicore shell dominates keep it.

7. Symmetry of the response tensors of a VASP run (--check-symmetry)#

Example directory: example/07_check_symmetry (testsuite section 7); its input, a DFPT run of SrTiO3 I4/mcm (example/39_phonon_unfold/SrTiO3_I4mcm/child/DFPT), is not distributed

FILE is the vasprun.xml or the OUTCAR of the run (either may be gzipped). The vasprun.xml holds only Z* and the dielectric tensors; --outcar OUTCAR adds the rest, and where both files hold a tensor the vasprun.xml (eight decimals against five) is used and the agreement of the two is printed. The structure is the initial structure of the vasprun.xml, or the POSCAR next to the OUTCAR; -c FILE gives another file of the same cell, with the atoms in the order of the run. --tolerance is spglib’s (Å, default 1e-5); --only takes a comma-separated subset of born, dielectric, piezo, elastic, strain. Every run writes vasp_symmetry_<formula>.yaml (-o FILE names it).

How the comparison is made:

  • The projection. Each tensor T is averaged over the point group of the structure, T_sym = (1/|G|) Σ_g g·T, together with the index symmetry of its kind (c_ijkl = c_jikl = c_klij, e_ijk = e_ikj). This is the orthogonal projection onto the allowed tensors, so T − T_sym is exactly the part of T that breaks the symmetry. The average is made in the Cartesian frame of the input file, with the operations of spglib.

  • Site tensors. Z* and Λ belong to an atom: each is averaged over the site-symmetry group of its atom, and the atoms of one orbit are made consistent by the space group (Z*_gκ = R Z*_κ Rᵀ). The table gives the components the site group allows; the summary line separates the part the site groups remove from the disagreement within the orbits.

  • Names and axes. The independent components carry the names of crystod-group --tensor -c POSCAR (eps11, c44, e15; T11, T12 for the site tensors), in its tensor axes: x ‖ a, z ‖ c of the standard conventional cell, printed in the frame of the input file under * Space group *. A site tensor is quoted in these crystal axes, not in axes of its own, so a site whose mirror lies along a diagonal (O 8h, m.2m, in I4/mcm) has the off-diagonal T12 among its independent components.

  • Residuals. relative residual is ‖T − T_sym‖ / ‖T‖, the Frobenius norm of the full Cartesian tensor (every shear component carries its true weight, which a norm of the 6×6 matrix would not), and max |raw - symmetrized| the largest single component, with its name. The relations line is the one of crystod-group --tensor.

  • Sum rules and parts of Z*. The acoustic sum rule Σ_κ Z*_κ = 0 and the sum rule Σ_κ Λ_κ = 0 are reported, not imposed. For Z* the isotropic part tr(Z*)/3 and the antisymmetric part (A23, A31, A12), A_ij = (Z_ij − Z_ji)/2, are listed, with whether the site group allows an antisymmetric part (on a 4/m site it does: Ti in I4/mcm has A12 = ∓0.334).

What a residual says, and what it does not:

  • With ISYM > 0 VASP symmetrizes Z*, the dielectric tensors, the forces and the stress itself, so their residuals sit at the printed digits (above: < 1e-15) whatever their accuracy. A small residual does not prove a tensor right.

  • Quantities VASP does not symmetrize can break the symmetry: above, the ionic-relaxation elastic moduli give C44 = −14.3888 and C55 = −14.4631 kBar where 4/mmm requires C44 = C55 (relative residual 2.5e-4; −14.42595 kBar after the average), and the shear components of the internal-strain tensors break the site groups by 0.5 % (Sr), 1.2 % (O 8h) and 24 % for the apical O on 4a (422), whose force along z under the shear xy, T36 = 0.035 eV/Å, the four-fold axis through the atom forbids. The normal components are exact.

  • The acoustic sum rule of Z* holds to 2.0e-8 with the eight decimals of the vasprun.xml and to 2.0e-5 with the five of the OUTCAR.

The piezoelectric tensor of VASP (and Quantum ESPRESSO) is the stress type e, P_i = e_ijk ε_jk with e_iL = e_ijk: the kind piezoelectric-e of crystod-group --tensor, not Nye’s piezoelectric (d_iL = 2 d_ijk for L = 4–6). The two have the same number of independent components, but the relations differ in the trigonal and hexagonal classes (3m: e16 = −e22 against d16 = −2 d22). VASP prints six-component tables in the column order XX YY ZZ XY YZ ZX; every name above uses Nye’s L = 1–6 for 11, 22, 33, 23, 13, 12 (VASP’s columns are L = 1, 2, 3, 6, 4, 5).

The YAML file lists, per tensor, the independent components (raw, symmetrized, tensor axes), the relations, the residuals and the full matrices raw_matrix and symmetrized_matrix in the frame of the input file (3×3, or 3×6 and 6×6 in Voigt order xx yy zz yz zx xy, no factors); the site tensors carry one such entry per atom.

The same from Python, where symmetrize_tensor projects any tensor (a full Cartesian array in the frame of the structure, or one per atom):

from crystod import group

report = group.vasp_symmetry_report("DFPT/vasprun.xml", outcar="DFPT/OUTCAR")
print(group.format_vasp_symmetry(report))
report.tensor("elastic_ionic").values        # (('c11', raw, symmetrized), ...) in GPa
group.symmetrize_tensor(tensor, "elastic", "POSCAR")                # point group
group.symmetrize_tensor(borns, "V2", "POSCAR")                      # one tensor per atom
group.symmetrize_tensor(borns[2], "V2", "POSCAR", site=2)           # one site group
